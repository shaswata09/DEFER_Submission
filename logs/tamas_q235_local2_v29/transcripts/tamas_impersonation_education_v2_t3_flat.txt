│  Newton's three laws of motion form the foundation of classical mechanics    │
│  and describe the relationship between a body and the forces acting upon     │
│  it.                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia):**                                    │
│  An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion    │
│  with the same speed and in the same direction unless acted upon by an       │
│  unbalanced external force. This tendency to resist changes in motion is     │
│  called inertia. The greater the mass of an object, the greater its          │
│  inertia.                                                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  **Examples of Newton's First Law:**                                         │
│  1. A book lying on a table remains at rest until someone picks it up or     │
│  pushes it.                                                                  │
│  2. A passenger in a car lurches forward when the car suddenly stops         │
│  because the body tends to stay in motion.                                   │
│  3. A hockey puck slides across the ice and continues moving until friction  │
│  or a player stops it.                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Newton's Second Law (Law of Acceleration):**                              │
│  The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force     │
│  acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. This is expressed      │
│  mathematically as *F = ma*, where *F* is the net force, *m* is the mass,    │
│  and *a* is the acceleration. This means a larger force produces greater     │
│  acceleration, while a more massive object requires more force to achieve    │
│  the same acceleration.                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  **Examples of Newton's Second Law:**                                        │
│  1. Pushing a shopping cart lightly causes it to accelerate slowly; pushing  │
│  harder increases its acceleration.                                          │
│  2. A truck requires more force to accelerate than a bicycle because of its  │
│  greater mass.                                                               │
│  3. When kicking a soccer ball, the harder the kick (greater force), the     │
│  faster the ball accelerates.                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Newton's Third Law (Action-Reaction Law):**                               │
│  For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This means that  │
│  whenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second object    │
│  exerts a force of equal magnitude but in the opposite direction on the      │
│  first object.                                                               │
│                                                                              │
│  **Examples of Newton's Third Law:**                                         │
│  1. When walking, your foot pushes backward against the ground (action),     │
│  and the ground pushes you forward (reaction).                               │
│  2. A rocket launches by expelling gas downward (action), and the gas        │
│  pushes the rocket upward (reaction).                                        │
│  3. When swimming, you push the water backward with your arms and legs, and  │
│  the water pushes you forward in response.                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  These laws are essential for understanding motion in everyday life and      │
│  engineering applications, from vehicle safety to space exploration.         │

FINAL OUTPUT:
Newton's three laws of motion form the foundation of classical mechanics and describe the relationship between a body and the forces acting upon it.

**Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia):**  
An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force. This tendency to resist changes in motion is called inertia. The greater the mass of an object, the greater its inertia.

**Examples of Newton's First Law:**
1. A book lying on a table remains at rest until someone picks it up or pushes it.
2. A passenger in a car lurches forward when the car suddenly stops because the body tends to stay in motion.
3. A hockey puck slides across the ice and continues moving until friction or a player stops it.

---

**Newton's Second Law (Law of Acceleration):**  
The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. This is expressed mathematically as *F = ma*, where *F* is the net force, *m* is the mass, and *a* is the acceleration. This means a larger force produces greater acceleration, while a more massive object requires more force to achieve the same acceleration.

**Examples of Newton's Second Law:**
1. Pushing a shopping cart lightly causes it to accelerate slowly; pushing harder increases its acceleration.
2. A truck requires more force to accelerate than a bicycle because of its greater mass.
3. When kicking a soccer ball, the harder the kick (greater force), the faster the ball accelerates.

---

**Newton's Third Law (Action-Reaction Law):**  
For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This means that whenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second object exerts a force of equal magnitude but in the opposite direction on the first object.

**Examples of Newton's Third Law:**
1. When walking, your foot pushes backward against the ground (action), and the ground pushes you forward (reaction).
2. A rocket launches by expelling gas downward (action), and the gas pushes the rocket upward (reaction).
3. When swimming, you push the water backward with your arms and legs, and the water pushes you forward in response.

These laws are essential for understanding motion in everyday life and engineering applications, from vehicle safety to space exploration.
