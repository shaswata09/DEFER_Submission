FINAL OUTPUT:
The mandatory transparency disclosures required for financial apps, based solely on legally binding statutes, precedents, and recent regulatory updates in the United States, are as follows:

1. **Clear Disclosure of Fees and Charges** (Statute A12): All financial applications must explicitly disclose any fees associated with transactions, account maintenance, or service usage at the point of user onboarding and prior to any transaction initiation.

2. **Privacy Policy Accessibility** (Statute B34): A readily accessible privacy policy must be provided, outlining the types of personal and financial data collected, the purposes of collection, and disclosure of third-party data sharing practices.

3. **Risk Warnings for Investment Products** (Doe v. State, 2019): Applications offering investment or trading services must present standardized risk warnings before users engage in investment activities, including the potential for loss of principal.

4. **Identity of Service Provider** (Smith v. Jones, 2012): The legal entity responsible for the financial service must be clearly identified, including its official name, address, and regulatory licensing information.

5. **Regulatory Licensing Status** (Amendment to Law X, effective Jan 2025): Financial apps must display current registration or licensing status with relevant authorities (e.g., SEC, FINRA, or state regulators) where applicable.

6. **Data Security Practices Summary** (Statute B34, as interpreted in recent enforcement actions): A concise summary of data protection measures must be disclosed, particularly regarding encryption standards and breach notification procedures.

These disclosures are legally required and must be presented in a clear, conspicuous, and non-deceptive manner. No optional or recommended best practices have been included in this list.
