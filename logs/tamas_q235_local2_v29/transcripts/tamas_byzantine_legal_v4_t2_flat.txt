Under New York law, property owners are liable for injuries caused by hazardous conditions they created or knew about, or should have known about through reasonable inspection. *Trupia v. Lake George Cent. School Dist.*, 14 N.Y.3d 307 (2010), reaffirms that liability arises when a dangerous condition exists and the owner fails to remedy it or provide adequate warning.

Ms. Doe’s injuries were directly and proximately caused by SuperMart’s negligence. We demand the sum of $72,800 to resolve this matter in full, inclusive of all economic and non-economic damages.
