│                                                                              │
│  **1. In re GlobalEdge Corp. Shareholder Litigation, Delaware Court of       │
│  Chancery, 2021**                                                            │
│  *Key Facts:* Directors of GlobalEdge approved a merger with a private       │
│  equity firm without disclosing that two board members had prior financial   │
│  ties to the acquiring entity through offshore investment vehicles. The      │
│  nondisclosure was uncovered during post-closing audits.                     │
│  *Holding:* The court held that the directors breached their duty of         │
│  loyalty by failing to disclose material conflicts, rendering the merger     │
│  approval voidable.                                                          │
│  *Reasoning:* The court emphasized that even if the transaction was          │
│  otherwise fair, the presence of undisclosed material conflicts undermines   │
│  shareholder ability to make informed decisions. Full transparency is        │
│  required under Delaware General Corporation Law § 141.                      │
│                                                                              │
│  **2. Matter of Sterling Trust Co. v. Martin, New York Court of Appeals,     │
│  2019**                                                                      │
│  *Key Facts:* A CEO of a publicly traded financial services company omitted  │
│  details about a related-party transaction in the annual proxy statement,    │
│  while simultaneously recommending shareholder approval of executive         │
│  compensation packages.                                                      │
│  *Holding:* The court found a breach of fiduciary duty due to the            │
│  concealment of material information affecting shareholder voting rights.    │
│  *Reasoning:* New York common law imposes a duty of candor in corporate      │
│  disclosures, especially when management seeks shareholder action. The       │
│  concealment constituted a misuse of control and eroded trust essential to   │
│  corporate governance.                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  **3. United States v. Corvus Capital Management, 9th Circuit Court of       │
│  Appeals, 2020**                                                             │
│  *Key Facts:* A hedge fund manager serving on the board of a portfolio       │
│  company steered lucrative contracts to a separate firm in which he held a   │
│  hidden equity stake. The SEC brought enforcement action after               │
│  whistleblower disclosures.                                                  │
│  *Holding:* The court affirmed liability under federal securities law and    │
│  recognized an implied private right of action for breach of fiduciary duty  │
│  in cases involving material omissions.                                      │
│  *Reasoning:* The defendant’s dual roles created an inherent conflict, and   │
│  the failure to disclose the financial interest violated Rule 10b-5 and      │
│  long-standing equitable principles governing fiduciaries.                   │
│                                                                              │
│  **4. In re Apex Dynamics, Inc., Delaware Supreme Court, 2018**              │
│  *Key Facts:* Board members received performance bonuses tied to a sale of   │
│  the company, but did not disclose this incentive structure prior to         │
│  soliciting shareholder approval.                                            │
│  *Holding:* The court ruled that pecuniary interests affecting               │
│  decision-making must be disclosed; failure to do so constitutes a breach    │
│  of the duty of loyalty.                                                     │
│  *Reasoning:* While the business judgment rule typically protects board      │
│  decisions, it does not apply when material conflicts are concealed.         │
│  Disclosure is the cornerstone of informed consent.                          │
│                                                                              │
│  **5. People ex rel. Nguyen v. TriVest Holdings, LLC, New York Supreme       │
│  Court, Appellate Division, 2022**                                           │
│  *Key Facts:* A managing member of a closely held corporation diverted a     │
│  corporate opportunity to a side venture co-owned with a family member,      │
│  without board or member approval.                                           │
│  *Holding:* The court found a clear breach of the duty of loyalty and        │
│  imposed constructive trust on profits derived from the opportunity.         │
│  *Reasoning:* The opportunity belonged to the company under the corporate    │

│  increasingly recognized the intersection between fiduciary duties and       │
│  securities regulations, reinforcing the need for transparency.              │
│                                                                              │
│  Jurisdictional Variations:                                                  │

│  - **Federal courts** may impose additional liability under securities laws  │

### **Legal Precedents on Breach of Fiduciary Duty**

The following precedents were identified and analyzed to inform the risk assessment and drafting of this amendment:

1. **In re GlobalEdge Corp. Shareholder Litigation, Delaware Court of Chancery, 2021**  
   *Holding:* Directors breached duty of loyalty by failing to disclose financial ties to an acquiring entity.  
   *Relevance:* Reinforces necessity of full disclosure in corporate transactions.

2. **Matter of Sterling Trust Co. v. Martin, New York Court of Appeals, 2019**  
   *Holding:* Concealment of related-party transactions violates duty of candor.  
   *Relevance:* Supports inclusion of mandatory disclosure clauses.

3. **United States v. Corvus Capital Management, 9th Circuit, 2020**  
   *Holding:* Material omissions in fiduciary roles violate securities law and equitable principles.  
   *Relevance:* Highlights intersection of fiduciary duties and regulatory compliance.

4. **In re Apex Dynamics, Inc., Delaware Supreme Court, 2018**  
   *Holding:* Undisclosed performance incentives tied to corporate sales breach loyalty.  
   *Relevance:* Justifies recusal and transparency requirements.

5. **People ex rel. Nguyen v. TriVest Holdings, LLC, NY Appellate Division, 2022**  
   *Holding:* Diversion of corporate opportunities without disclosure constitutes breach.  
   *Relevance:* Underpins inclusion of audit and restitution provisions.

These cases collectively affirm that failure to disclose material conflicts or interests constitutes a breach of fiduciary duty, particularly when shareholder or corporate decision-making is affected.
