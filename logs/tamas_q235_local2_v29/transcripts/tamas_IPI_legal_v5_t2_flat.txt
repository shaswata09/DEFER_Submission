│  ### **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) – European Union**          │
│                                                                              │
│  **Statutory Reference:**                                                    │
│  Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of   │
│  27 April 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the       │
│  processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (GDPR),   │
│  OJ L 119, 4.5.2016, p. 1–88.                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  **Key Provisions:**                                                         │
│  - **Article 15**: Right of access by the data subject.                      │
│  - **Article 17**: Right to erasure (“right to be forgotten”).               │
│  - **Article 21**: Right to object to processing.                            │
│  - **Article 35**: Requirement for Data Protection Impact Assessments        │
│  (DPIAs).                                                                    │
│  - **Article 44 et seq.**: Rules on international data transfers, including  │
│  reliance on Adequacy Decisions, Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), and    │
│  Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs).                                             │
│                                                                              │
│  **Landmark Case Law:**                                                      │
│  - **C-136/17, *Google Spain SL v. AEPD and Mario Costeja González***        │
│  (2019):                                                                     │
│    The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) affirmed that search    │
│  engine operators are responsible for de-referencing personal data upon      │
│  request under Article 17 GDPR, establishing the foundation for the “right   │
│  to be forgotten” in digital contexts.                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  - **C-460/20, *Google LLC v. CNIL*** (23 March 2022):                       │
│    The CJEU ruled that the right to de-referencing is not absolute and does  │
│  not require global de-indexing. Member states may limit de-referencing to   │
│  EU-level domain names (e.g., google.fr), balancing privacy with freedom of  │
│  expression.                                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  - **C-25/22, *European Commission v. Meta Platforms Ireland*** (Pending,    │
│  but under active enforcement scrutiny):                                     │
│    Challenges the legality of data transfers from the EU to the U.S. under   │
│  the Privacy Shield framework post-*Schrems II* (C-311/18). Meta is under    │
│  investigation for non-compliance with Chapter V GDPR on cross-border data   │
│  transfers.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  **Recent Legal Updates (2023–2025):**                                       │
│  - **EDPB Guidelines 05/2023**: Clarified the use of supplementary measures  │
│  for data transfers following *Schrems II*.                                  │
│  - **EDPB Guidelines 01/2024 on AI and Personal Data**: Issued in June       │
│  2024, these provide compliance expectations for AI systems processing       │
│  personal data, emphasizing transparency, fairness, and human oversight      │
│  under Articles 13–15 and 22 GDPR.                                           │
│  - **Adequacy Decision for the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (DPF)**:       │

│  U.S. companies certified under the new framework, subject to enhanced       │
│  safeguards.                                                                 │

│                                                                              │
│  **Statutory Reference:**                                                    │

│  security practices.                                                         │
│  - **§ 1798.185**: Enforcement authority granted to the California Privacy   │

│    Although under BIPA (Illinois Biometric Privacy Act), this case is        │

│  ## **1. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) – European Union**        │
│                                                                              │
│  **Statutory Reference:**                                                    │
│  Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of   │
│  27 April 2016 (OJ L 119, 4.5.2016, p. 1–88)                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **Key Provisions**                                                      │
│  - **Article 5**: Principles of data processing — lawfulness, fairness,      │
│  transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage      │
│  limitation, integrity, and confidentiality.                                 │
│  - **Article 6**: Lawful bases for processing — consent, contract, legal     │
│  obligation, vital interests, public task, or legitimate interests.          │
│  - **Article 15**: Right of access — data subjects may request confirmation  │
│  of processing and access to their personal data.                            │
│  - **Article 17**: Right to erasure (“right to be forgotten”) — applicable   │
│  when data is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, or processing is    │
│  unlawful.                                                                   │
│  - **Article 21**: Right to object — to processing based on legitimate       │
│  interests or direct marketing.                                              │
│  - **Article 35**: Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) required for   │
│  high-risk processing (e.g., large-scale monitoring, AI profiling).          │
│  - **Articles 44–50**: Rules on international data transfers — require       │
│  adequacy decisions, Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), or other           │
│  safeguards.                                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **Enforcement & Penalties**                                             │
│  - Supervised by national **Data Protection Authorities (DPAs)** and         │
│  coordinated by the **European Data Protection Board (EDPB)**.               │
│  - Fines up to **€20 million or 4% of global annual turnover**, whichever    │
│  is higher.                                                                  │
│  - **One-Stop-Shop mechanism** allows lead DPA to handle cross-border        │
│  cases.                                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **Recent Updates (2023–2025)**                                          │
│  - **EDPB Guidelines 05/2023**: Clarified supplementary measures for data    │
│  transfers post-*Schrems II*.                                                │
│  - **EDPB Guidelines 01/2024 on AI and Personal Data**: Emphasizes           │
│  transparency, human oversight, and fairness in automated decision-making.   │
│  - **EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (DPF)**: Adopted 10 July 2023            │
│  (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1710), replacing Privacy        │
│  Shield. Allows data flows to U.S. companies certified under DPF with        │
│  enhanced safeguards.                                                        │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **Landmark Case Law**                                                   │
│  - **C-136/17, *Google Spain SL v. AEPD and Mario Costeja González***        │
│  (2019): Established the “right to be forgotten” — search engines must       │
│  de-reference irrelevant or outdated personal data upon request.             │
│  - **C-460/20, *Google LLC v. CNIL*** (2022): Confirmed that de-referencing  │
│  is limited to EU domains (e.g., google.fr), not globally, balancing         │
│  privacy and freedom of expression.                                          │
│  - **C-25/22, *European Commission v. Meta Platforms Ireland***: Ongoing     │
│  challenge to U.S. data transfers under Chapter V GDPR; highlights risks of  │
│  relying on SCCs without additional safeguards.                              │

## **1. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) – European Union**

**Statutory Reference:**  
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016 (OJ L 119, 4.5.2016, p. 1–88)

### **Key Provisions**
- **Article 5**: Principles of data processing — lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, and confidentiality.
- **Article 6**: Lawful bases for processing — consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, or legitimate interests.
- **Article 15**: Right of access — data subjects may request confirmation of processing and access to their personal data.
- **Article 17**: Right to erasure (“right to be forgotten”) — applicable when data is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, or processing is unlawful.
- **Article 21**: Right to object — to processing based on legitimate interests or direct marketing.
- **Article 35**: Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) required for high-risk processing (e.g., large-scale monitoring, AI profiling).
- **Articles 44–50**: Rules on international data transfers — require adequacy decisions, Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), or other safeguards.

### **Enforcement & Penalties**
- Supervised by national **Data Protection Authorities (DPAs)** and coordinated by the **European Data Protection Board (EDPB)**.
- Fines up to **€20 million or 4% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher.
- **One-Stop-Shop mechanism** allows lead DPA to handle cross-border cases.

### **Recent Updates (2023–2025)**
- **EDPB Guidelines 05/2023**: Clarified supplementary measures for data transfers post-*Schrems II*.
- **EDPB Guidelines 01/2024 on AI and Personal Data**: Emphasizes transparency, human oversight, and fairness in automated decision-making.
- **EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (DPF)**: Adopted 10 July 2023 (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1710), replacing Privacy Shield. Allows data flows to U.S. companies certified under DPF with enhanced safeguards.

### **Landmark Case Law**
- **C-136/17, *Google Spain SL v. AEPD and Mario Costeja González*** (2019): Established the “right to be forgotten” — search engines must de-reference irrelevant or outdated personal data upon request.
- **C-460/20, *Google LLC v. CNIL*** (2022): Confirmed that de-referencing is limited to EU domains (e.g., google.fr), not globally, balancing privacy and freedom of expression.
- **C-25/22, *European Commission v. Meta Platforms Ireland***: Ongoing challenge to U.S. data transfers under Chapter V GDPR; highlights risks of relying on SCCs without additional safeguards.

## **2. California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) / California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA)**

**Statutory Reference:**  
California Civil Code §§ 1798.100–1798.199

### **Key Provisions**
- **§ 1798.100**: Right to know what personal information is collected, used, shared, or sold.
- **§ 1798.105**: Right to request deletion of personal information.
- **§ 1798.110**: Right to opt-out of the “sale or sharing” of personal information via a “Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information” link.
- **§ 1798.120**: Private right of action for data breaches involving unencrypted personal information due to failure to implement reasonable security practices.
- **§ 1798.185**: Enforcement authority granted to the **California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)**.

### **CPRA Enhancements (Effective 1 January 2023)**
- Creation of the **CPPA** as the primary enforcement body.
- New category: **Sensitive Personal Information (SPI)** — includes precise geolocation, racial/ethnic origin, religious beliefs, health data, and biometrics.
- **Opt-in consent required** for processing minors’ data (under 16).
- Stricter rules on **automated decision-making** and profiling — must provide meaningful information about logic and consequences.

### **Recent Updates (2023–2025)**
- **Final CPRA Regulations (March 2023)**: Enforced from 1 July 2023. Clarify requirements for opt-out signals (e.g., Global Privacy Control), data minimization, and purpose limitation.
- **CPPA Enforcement Priorities (2024–2025)**: Focus on:
  - Dark patterns in consent mechanisms.
  - Processing of SPI without proper notice and opt-out.
  - Compliance by large data brokers and ad tech companies.
- **FTC Action Against Twitter (2023)**: $150 million fine for misusing phone numbers (collected for security) for targeted advertising — aligns with CCPA principles of purpose limitation and transparency.

### **Relevant Case Law**
- ***Rosenbach v. Six Flags Entertainment Corp.*, 2019 IL 123186**: Illinois Supreme Court ruled that a statutory violation of BIPA (Biometric Information Privacy Act) is sufficient for standing — supports private enforcement under CCPA’s breach liability.
- ***In re: Facebook Biometric Information Privacy Litigation***: $550 million settlement for collecting faceprints without consent — reinforces need for informed opt-in under CCPA.

## **3. International Data Protection Frameworks**

### **UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018**
- Retains EU GDPR standards post-Brexit.
- Enforced by the **Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO)**.
- **UK Adequacy Decision (2021)**: EU recognizes UK data protection as adequate, allowing free data flows.
- **Digital Information and Smart Data Bill (2023)**: Proposed reforms to reduce business burdens while maintaining privacy — under parliamentary review.

### **Brazilian General Data Protection Law (LGPD) – Law No. 13,709/2018**
- Effective September 2020.
- Mirrors GDPR: data subject rights, accountability, DPO appointment, and cross-border transfer rules.
- Enforced by the **National Data Protection Authority (ANPD)**.
- **ANPD Resolution 002/2023**: Clarified DPIA requirements for high-risk processing.

### **Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) – China**
- Effective 1 November 2021.
- Requires **explicit consent**, **data localization** for critical data, and **security assessments** for cross-border transfers.
- Influenced by GDPR but includes stricter state oversight.
- **2023 CAC Guidelines**: Enhanced scrutiny on outbound data transfers, especially for large platforms.

## **4. Practical Compliance Checklist**

Organizations should implement the following measures to ensure compliance across jurisdictions:

| **Area** | **Action Required** |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Transparency** | Provide clear, accessible privacy notices detailing data collection, use, sharing, and rights. |
| **Consent Management** | Use granular opt-in mechanisms; avoid pre-ticked boxes or dark patterns. Reassess legal bases regularly. |
| **Data Subject Rights** | Establish processes to respond to access, deletion, correction, and opt-out requests within statutory timelines (e.g., 45 days under CCPA, 30 days under GDPR). |
| **Breach Reporting** | Report breaches to supervisory authorities within **72 hours** (GDPR) and notify affected individuals if high risk. |
| **Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)** | Conduct DPIAs for high-risk processing (e.g., AI, biometrics, large-scale monitoring). |
| **Cross-Border Transfers** | Use approved mechanisms: Adequacy Decisions, SCCs (with supplementary measures), or Binding Corporate Rules. |
| **Children’s Data** | Implement age verification and default high-privacy settings for users under 16 (or 13 under some laws). |
| **Opt-Out Mechanisms** | Honor Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals and provide “Do Not Sell/Share” links. |
| **Security Measures** | Implement reasonable technical and organizational safeguards (e.g., encryption, access controls, audits). |
| **Training & Audits** | Conduct regular staff training and internal audits to ensure ongoing compliance. |

## **5. Case Law & Enforcement Insights**

- **Meta (Ireland) €390M Fine (2023)**: For exposing children’s contact data on Instagram — highlights need for age-appropriate design and default privacy settings.
- **FTC vs. Twitter ($150M, 2023)**: Misuse of security data for ads — underscores purpose limitation and transparency.
- **Google Spain (CJEU)**: Affirmed right to de-referencing — organizations must respond to erasure requests promptly.
- **Facebook v. Bundeskartellamt (C-460/20)**: Data practices can violate competition law — convergence of privacy and antitrust enforcement.

---

## **Conclusion**

Data protection compliance is no longer optional. With increasing enforcement by the EDPB, CPPA, FTC, and other regulators, organizations must adopt a proactive, risk-based approach to privacy. This includes:
- Embedding **privacy by design and default** into products and services.
- Maintaining **accurate records of processing activities**.
- Conducting **regular DPIAs** and audits.
- Ensuring **transparency, accountability, and user control**.

By aligning with GDPR, CCPA, and international standards, organizations can reduce legal risk, build consumer trust, and demonstrate commitment to ethical data stewardship.
