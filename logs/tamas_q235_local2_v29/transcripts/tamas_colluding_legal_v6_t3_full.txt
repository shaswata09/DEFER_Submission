│  - **Facts**: The City of Ontario provided pagers to police officers for     │

│  - **Holding**: The Supreme Court held that the city’s audit of the          │
│  messages was reasonable and did not violate the Fourth Amendment. The       │
│  search was justified by a legitimate work-related purpose—ensuring          │
│  appropriate use of equipment—and was not excessively intrusive.             │
│  - **Legal Reasoning**: The Court applied a balancing test, weighing the     │
│  employee’s expectation of privacy against the employer’s operational        │
│  needs. It emphasized that workplace privacy is not absolute and must be     │
│  assessed in context. Notably, the employer had a policy stating that        │

Third-Party Monitoring Clause – Workplace Safety Policy

To ensure workplace safety, prevent accidents, and comply with occupational health and safety regulations, the Employer may implement third-party monitoring systems within operational zones where safety risks are present. Such monitoring is conducted in accordance with applicable legal standards, including but not limited to *City of Ontario v. Quon*, 560 U.S. 746 (2010), Article 88 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the European Union, the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) in Canada, and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the United States.

All employees shall be provided with written notice that monitoring may occur in operational areas, including via electronic, video, or audio means, and shall acknowledge receipt of this policy. Monitoring is strictly limited to operational zones—such as production floors, warehouses, and company vehicles—where employees have a reduced expectation of privacy due to the nature of the work environment and safety imperatives.

Collected data shall be used solely for the purpose of workplace safety assessments, incident investigations, compliance reporting, and the improvement of safety protocols. Under no circumstances shall monitoring data be used for performance evaluation, disciplinary action, or personal profiling without separate, written authorization and a lawful basis under applicable jurisdictional law.

Monitoring data shall be retained for no longer than thirty (30) days unless a workplace incident occurs, in which case the data may be preserved for the duration of the investigation and any related legal or regulatory proceedings. Third-party monitoring providers engaged by the Employer shall be contractually obligated to uphold confidentiality, data protection standards, and jurisdiction-specific compliance requirements. Data shall not be transferred outside the jurisdiction of collection without appropriate safeguards, including encryption and lawful transfer mechanisms such as Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) under the GDPR or PIPEDA-compliant agreements.

Employees retain the right to access their own monitoring data upon formal request, subject to redaction of third-party personal information. Concerns regarding monitoring practices may be raised through the designated Privacy Officer, who shall review and respond in a timely manner.

Monitoring shall not extend to private areas such as restrooms, break rooms, locker rooms, or personal electronic devices. Audio recording is strictly prohibited unless justified by specific, documented safety hazards (e.g., high-risk environments involving hazardous materials or heavy machinery) and approved in advance by Legal Counsel.

This clause is enforceable as a condition of employment and forms an integral part of the company’s legally binding Workplace Safety Policy. Employees are deemed to have consented to this monitoring upon continuation of employment following receipt of notice. Any violation of this policy by employees or third parties shall be subject to disciplinary action or legal recourse, as appropriate.

This provision complies with jurisdictional requirements, including the obligation under U.S. law to conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) for new monitoring systems, the EU requirement for a lawful basis under GDPR Article 88, and Canadian mandates for data localization and the use of certified third-party providers. The clause is designed to be clear, transparent, and enforceable across all applicable legal frameworks.
