│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The following is a comprehensive list of applicable statutes and            │
│  international conventions governing international arbitration, including    │
│  key provisions, adoption dates, and jurisdictional scope:                   │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral      │
│  Awards (New York Convention, 1958)**                                        │
│     - **Adoption Date**: June 10, 1958                                       │
│     - **Entry into Force**: June 7, 1959                                     │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Article II: Obliges contracting states to recognize arbitration      │
│  agreements and refer parties to arbitration when a dispute falls within     │
│  its scope.                                                                  │
│       - Article III: Requires courts to recognize arbitral awards as         │
│  binding and enforceable, on equal footing with domestic judgments.          │
│       - Article V: Sets out limited grounds for refusing enforcement of      │
│  foreign awards, including procedural irregularities, incapacity,            │
│  invalidity of the arbitration agreement, lack of due process, or if the     │
│  award deals with matters beyond the scope of submission to arbitration.     │
│  Public policy is also a recognized ground for refusal.                      │
│       - Article VII: Allows for more favorable treatment under national or   │
│  other international laws.                                                   │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Ratified by over 170 countries, making it    │
│  the cornerstone of international arbitration enforcement. Applies to        │
│  recognition and enforcement of foreign arbitral awards made in the          │
│  territory of a state other than the state where recognition is sought.      │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (1985,      │
│  with 2006 amendments)**                                                     │
│     - **Adoption Date**: Originally adopted in 1985; amended in 2006         │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Provides a comprehensive legal framework for international           │
│  commercial arbitration.                                                     │
│       - Defines "international arbitration" (Article 1): where parties have  │
│  places of business in different states or the seat, performance, or         │
│  subject matter of the dispute is connected to more than one state.          │
│       - Article 8: Requires courts to refer parties to arbitration when a    │
│  valid arbitration agreement exists.                                         │
│       - Article 16: Grants tribunals competence-competence (authority to     │
│  rule on their own jurisdiction).                                            │
│       - Articles 18–24: Outline procedural fairness, equal treatment, and    │
│  the right to present one’s case.                                            │
│       - Article 34: Sets out grounds for setting aside an award (similar to  │
│  Article V of the New York Convention), including procedural unfairness,     │
│  lack of jurisdiction, or if the award violates public policy.               │
│       - Article 35–36: Governs recognition and enforcement of awards,        │
│  aligning closely with the New York Convention.                              │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Not binding unless adopted into domestic     │
│  law. Adopted in whole or in part by over 80 countries, including Canada,    │
│  Australia, India, and several U.S. states (via the Uniform Arbitration      │
│  Act). Serves as a blueprint for modern arbitration legislation.             │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Federal Arbitration Act (FAA), United States (9 U.S.C. §§ 1–16)**      │
│     - **Adoption Date**: Enacted in 1925                                     │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Chapter 1: Applies to both domestic and international arbitration;   │
│  establishes enforceability of arbitration agreements.                       │
│       - Chapter 2: Implements the New York Convention (1970); governs        │
│  recognition and enforcement of foreign awards in U.S. courts.               │
│       - Section 202: Allows enforcement of arbitration agreements covered    │
│  by the New York Convention.                                                 │
│       - Section 207: Directs U.S. courts to enforce foreign arbitral awards  │
│  in accordance with the Convention.                                          │
│       - Grounds for vacatur (Section 10): Fraud, corruption, evident         │
│  partiality, misconduct, or exceeding powers.                                │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Applies to arbitration agreements and        │
│  awards in the United States, particularly where interstate or foreign       │
│  commerce is involved. Chapter 2 applies to awards made outside the U.S.     │
│  under the New York Convention.                                              │
│                                                                              │
│  4. **Arbitration Act 1996 (United Kingdom)**                                │
│     - **Adoption Date**: 1996 (came into force January 31, 1997)             │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Section 1: Sets out the overarching objectives—fair resolution       │
│  without unnecessary delay or expense, and minimal court intervention.       │
│       - Section 4: Defines "international arbitration" (where at least one   │
│  party is not British, or the seat is outside the UK).                       │
│       - Section 66: Provides for enforcement of arbitral awards (domestic    │
│  and foreign).                                                               │
│       - Section 67: Allows challenge of an award on jurisdictional grounds.  │
│       - Section 68: Permits challenge on grounds of serious irregularity     │
│  (e.g., procedural unfairness, bias, or exceeding powers).                   │
│       - Section 69: Allows appeal on a point of law, unless excluded by      │
│  agreement.                                                                  │
│       - Schedule 1: Incorporates the UNCITRAL Model Law for international    │
│  arbitrations seated in England and Wales.                                   │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Applies to arbitrations seated in England    │
│  and Wales. Widely used as a seat for international commercial arbitration   │
│  due to its pro-arbitration stance and judicial restraint.                   │
│                                                                              │
│  5. **International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes Convention  │
│  (ICSID Convention, 1965)**                                                  │
│     - **Adoption Date**: March 18, 1965                                      │
│     - **Entry into Force**: October 14, 1966                                 │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Establishes ICSID as a forum for resolving investment disputes       │
│  between states and nationals of other states.                               │
│       - Article 25: Defines jurisdiction (consent-based disputes involving   │
│  investments).                                                               │
│       - Article 53: Awards are binding and not subject to appeal; may be     │
│  annulled only on limited grounds (e.g., lack of jurisdiction, corruption,   │
│  or procedural irregularity).                                                │
│       - Article 54: Requires contracting states to recognize ICSID awards    │
│  as binding and enforce them as domestic judgments.                          │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Ratified by over 160 countries. Unique in    │
│  that ICSID awards are enforceable directly in member states without         │
│  recourse to the New York Convention.                                        │
│                                                                              │
│  6. **European Convention on International Commercial Arbitration (Geneva,   │
│  1961)**                                                                     │
│     - **Adoption Date**: April 21, 1961                                      │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Article 4: Requires courts to refer parties to arbitration if a      │
│  valid agreement exists.                                                     │
│       - Article 8: Limits grounds for challenging awards to those similar    │
│  to the New York Convention.                                                 │
│       - Article 9: Provides for enforcement of awards in signatory states.   │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Applies to Council of Europe member states.  │
│  Largely superseded by the New York Convention and UNCITRAL Model Law but    │
│  still in force in several countries.                                        │
│                                                                              │
│  7. **Panama Convention (Inter-American Convention on International          │
│  Commercial Arbitration, 1975)**                                             │
│     - **Adoption Date**: January 30, 1975                                    │
│     - **Entry into Force**: March 1976                                       │
│     - **Key Provisions**:                                                    │
│       - Article II: Requires recognition of arbitration agreements.          │
│       - Article III: Mandates enforcement of awards in all contracting       │
│  states.                                                                     │
│       - Article IV: Lists grounds for refusal of enforcement (similar to     │
│  New York Convention).                                                       │
│     - **Jurisdictional Scope**: Ratified by over 20 countries in the         │
│  Americas, including the U.S., Mexico, and several Latin American nations.   │
│                                                                              │
│  These instruments collectively form the backbone of international           │
│  arbitration law, ensuring the enforceability of agreements and awards,      │
│  procedural fairness, and limited judicial interference. Their               │
│  harmonization through the UNCITRAL Model Law and widespread adoption of     │
│  the New York Convention have significantly enhanced the predictability and  │
│  reliability of international dispute resolution.                            │
│                                                                              │
