│  compliance:                                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  ### I. STRUCTURE OF LEGAL LETTERS IN FEDERAL BREACH OF CONTRACT MATTERS     │
│                                                                              │
│  A legal letter alleging breach of contract that may be used in federal      │
│  litigation should be structured as a formal demand letter or pre-suit       │
│  notice, with the following components:                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Letterhead**: Include the law firm’s name, address, contact            │
│  information, and bar identification.                                        │
│  2. **Date**: The current date of issuance.                                  │
│  3. **Recipient Information**: Full name, title, address, and contact        │
│  details of the party alleged to have breached the contract.                 │
│  4. **Subject Line**: Clearly state “Notice of Breach of Contract –          │
│  [Contract Title or Description]”.                                           │
│  5. **Introduction**: Identify the sender, the client, and the purpose of    │
│  the letter.                                                                 │
│  6. **Statement of Facts**:                                                  │
│     - Identify the parties to the contract.                                  │
│     - Reference the date and nature of the agreement.                        │
│     - Summarize key contractual obligations.                                 │
│     - Specify the alleged breach (e.g., failure to deliver goods,            │
│  non-payment).                                                               │
│  7. **Legal Basis for Claim**:                                               │
│     - Cite the applicable legal standard for breach of contract under        │
│  federal or state law.                                                       │
│     - Reference Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8(a)(2), which requires a    │
│  “short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is         │
│  entitled to relief.”                                                        │
│  8. **Demand for Cure or Performance**:                                      │
│     - Specify a reasonable time frame (typically 14–30 days) for the         │
│  breaching party to cure the breach.                                         │
│     - State consequences of non-compliance (e.g., initiation of              │
│  litigation).                                                                │
│  9. **Reservation of Rights**: Include a clause reserving all legal rights   │
│  and remedies under the contract and applicable law.                         │
│  10. **Conclusion and Signature**: Formal closing with attorney’s            │
│  signature, bar number, and firm affiliation.                                │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### II. CITATION STYLE AND FORMATTING STANDARDS                             │
│                                                                              │
│  Legal letters intended for federal court use must adhere to the following   │
│  citation and formatting rules:                                              │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Citation Format**: Use *The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation*     │
│  (21st ed.).                                                                 │
│    - Cases: *Smith v. Jones*, 456 F.3d 123 (D.C. Cir. 2012).                 │
│    - Statutes: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 8(a)(2); 28 U.S.C. §   │
│  1332 (diversity jurisdiction).                                              │
│    - Contracts: Cite as “Contract between [Party A] and [Party B], dated     │
│  [Date], at § [Clause Number].”                                              │
│  - **Font and Spacing**: Use 12-point Times New Roman, double-spaced text    │
│  for formal pleadings. While demand letters may be single-spaced, clarity    │
│  and readability are essential.                                              │
│  - **Margins**: 1-inch margins on all sides.                                 │
│  - **Page Numbers**: Required for documents exceeding five pages.            │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### III. RELEVANT STATUTES AND RULES                                        │
│                                                                              │
│  Key statutes and procedural rules governing breach of contract claims in    │
│  federal court include:                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP)**:                             │
│     - **Rule 8**: Requires a short and plain statement of the claim.         │
│     - **Rule 9**: Specificity required for allegations of fraud or mistake.  │
│     - **Rule 10**: Formatting of pleadings (headings, paragraphs,            │
│  captions).                                                                  │
│  2. **28 U.S.C. § 1332**: Provides diversity jurisdiction where parties are  │
│  from different states and the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000.        │
│  3. **Local Rules of U.S. District Courts**: Vary by district but often      │
│  impose formatting, filing, and certificate of service requirements. For     │

Dear Sirs:  

This law firm represents XYZ Corp (“XYZ”) in connection with your company’s material breach of the Supply Agreement entered into on January 15, 2023 (“Agreement”), a copy of which is attached hereto as Exhibit A. This letter serves as formal notice of such breach pursuant to Section 8.2 of the Agreement and demands immediate cure within fourteen (14) calendar days of receipt of this notice, as required under New York law and the terms of the Agreement.  

### 1. Statement of Facts  

Pursuant to the Agreement, ABC Ltd (“ABC”) agreed to deliver 10,000 units of industrial-grade sensors monthly to XYZ Corp at the agreed price of $120 per unit, with delivery terms FOB New York Harbor, Incoterms 2020. The Agreement further specified that all shipments must comply with U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) Part 15 standards and be accompanied by a Certificate of Conformity.  

Since October 2024, ABC has failed to meet its delivery obligations, shipping only 42% of the contracted volume. Furthermore, shipments received in November and December 2024 were found to contain non-compliant components, triggering a recall by XYZ’s downstream distributor under 16 C.F.R. § 1115. Independent testing confirmed that the sensors emitted radio frequency interference in violation of 47 U.S.C. § 302a and FCC rules.  

Despite written notice dated January 10, 2025, ABC has not rectified the non-conformity or resumed full-volume shipments. As of the date of this letter, ABC remains in arrears on 5,800 units, and XYZ has incurred $317,000 in compensatory damages, including lost profits, recall costs, and third-party claims.  

### 2. Legal Basis  

Under New York law, a party’s failure to perform its contractual obligations constitutes a material breach when, as here, the performance goes to the essence of the agreement. See *E. Allan Farnsworth, Farnsworth on Contracts § 8.16 (3d ed. 2023)*. The non-delivery and delivery of non-conforming goods constitute dual breaches under U.C.C. § 2-607(4)(a), adopted in New York (N.Y. U.C.C. § 2-607(4)(a)).  

The Agreement contains a valid liquidated damages clause, Section 5.3, which provides for $15,000 per month of delay due to supply failure. This clause is enforceable as a reasonable forecast of harm and not a penalty. See *Lake v. 9300 Realty, LLC*, 26 N.Y.3d 356, 364 (2015). Liquidated damages currently amount to $75,000 for the five-month shortfall.  

### 3. Demand for Cure and Damages  

Pursuant to Section 8.2 of the Agreement, XYZ hereby demands that ABC:  

a) Cure the breach by delivering the outstanding 5,800 units of FCC-compliant sensors within 14 calendar days of receipt of this letter;  

b) Provide written certification of compliance with FCC Part 15 and ISO 9001 standards for all future shipments;  

c) Pay $75,000 in liquidated damages and $317,000 in compensatory damages, for a total of $392,000, within 14 days of this notice;  

d) Confirm in writing its intention to resume full contractual performance beginning May 2025.
