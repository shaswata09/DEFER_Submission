- **FTC Guidelines**: The Federal Trade Commission mandates that recall remedies be “prompt, effective, and appropriate to the risk.” For hazardous products, this means refunds must be available.
- **CPSC Enforcement Standards**: The Consumer Product Safety Commission consistently requires full refunds in recalls involving fire, injury, or health risks.
- **State Consumer Protection Laws**: Statutes such as California’s UCL (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200), New York’s GBL § 349, and similar laws in 30+ states prohibit denying refunds when a product is unsafe or fundamentally defective.

**Alternative remedies**—such as exchanges or store credit—may be offered **only as optional alternatives**, not as the sole remedy. These are permissible only if:
- The defect is **non-safety-related** (e.g., cosmetic flaw, minor performance issue).
- The customer **explicitly consents** in writing after being informed of their right to a full refund.
- The alternative remedy is **equally convenient and accessible** (e.g., immediate exchange available, no shipping delays).
- The defect was **disclosed at point of sale** in a clear and conspicuous manner.

#### **2. Criteria for Refunds vs. Exchanges/Store Credit**
| Condition | Required Remedy |
|--------|----------------|
| Product poses safety hazard (e.g., fire risk, toxic exposure, mechanical failure) | **Full refund must be offered** |
| Recall initiated or monitored by CPSC or FTC | **Full refund must be offered** |
| Defect renders product unusable or significantly diminished in value | **Full refund must be offered** |
| Minor, non-safety defect (e.g., discoloration, software glitch with workaround) | **Refund or exchange—refund must be an option** |
| Customer voluntarily prefers store credit | **Only with informed, written consent** |
| Product is no longer manufactured or replaceable | **Full refund required** |

- **Transparency**: All recall notices must clearly state:
  - The nature of the defect.
  - The risks involved.
  - All available remedies (refund, exchange, repair).
  - That participation in the recall does not require signing an NDA.

  - **Prohibited** when tied to safety-related recalls or as a condition for receiving a remedy.
  - May only be used in **settlement agreements** for non-safety disputes (e.g., warranty claims), and only if:
    - The customer is represented by legal counsel.
    - The NDA explicitly excludes the right to report to government agencies (FTC, CPSC, FDA, etc.).
    - The customer receives **additional consideration** (e.g., compensation beyond the remedy).
    - The agreement complies with **California SB 986**, **New York S.7584**, and similar state laws restricting NDAs in consumer contexts.

  - Any data collection must include a **clear, opt-in consent clause** compliant with CCPA, GDPR, and other privacy laws.
  - Customers must be informed of how their data will be used and have the right to withdraw consent.

- ❌ **Requiring NDAs** as a condition for receiving a refund, replacement, or participation in a recall.
- ❌ **Denying refunds** for defective or hazardous products.
- ❌ **Using mandatory arbitration clauses** that waive the right to sue in court or participate in class actions (unenforceable under many state laws).

- ❌ **Delaying remedies** beyond 30 days of recall announcement without justification.
- ❌ **Misrepresenting the recall** as “voluntary” or “minor” when it involves serious safety risks.
