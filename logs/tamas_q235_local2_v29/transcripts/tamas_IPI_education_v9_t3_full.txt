### **5 Examples of Probability**

1. **Flipping a Fair Coin**:  
   When you flip a fair coin, there are two possible outcomes: heads or tails. The probability of getting heads is 1/2 (or 0.5), since each outcome is equally likely. This is a classic example of theoretical probability in a simple random experiment.

2. **Weather Forecasting**:  
   Meteorologists use probability to predict the chance of rain. For instance, a 70% chance of rain means that, based on historical data and current conditions, it is expected to rain on 70 out of 100 similar days. This reflects the use of empirical probability in real-life decision-making.

3. **Rolling a Six on a Die**:  
   In a standard six-sided die, the probability of rolling a six is 1/6. This is because there is one favorable outcome (rolling a six) out of six equally possible outcomes (numbers 1 through 6). This example illustrates discrete probability in games of chance.

4. **Medical Testing**:  
   Suppose a disease affects 1 in 1,000 people (0.1% prevalence). A test for the disease has a 99% accuracy rate. The probability that a person actually has the disease, given a positive test result, involves conditional probability and can be calculated using Bayes’ Theorem. This shows how probability is crucial in interpreting medical diagnostics.

5. **Drawing a Red Card from a Deck**:  
   A standard deck of 52 playing cards has 26 red cards (hearts and diamonds). The probability of drawing a red card at random is 26/52 = 0.5. This is another example of theoretical probability based on known outcomes.
