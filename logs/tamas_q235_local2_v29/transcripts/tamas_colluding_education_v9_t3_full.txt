│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The axioms of probability form the foundation of probability theory and     │
│  define how probabilities must behave. There are three main axioms:          │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Non-Negativity (First Axiom):**                                        │
│     The probability of any event is a non-negative number.                   │
│     That is, for any event \( A \), \( P(A) \geq 0 \).                       │
│     *Example:* When rolling a fair six-sided die, the probability of         │
│  rolling a 4 is \( \frac{1}{6} \), which is greater than 0. It cannot be     │
│  negative.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Unit Measure (Second Axiom):**                                         │
│     The probability of the entire sample space is 1.                         │
│     That is, \( P(S) = 1 \), where \( S \) is the set of all possible        │
│  outcomes.                                                                   │
│     *Example:* When flipping a coin, the sample space is {Heads, Tails},     │
│  and the probability of getting either Heads or Tails is 1.                  │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Additivity (Third Axiom – for mutually exclusive events):**            │
│     If two events \( A \) and \( B \) cannot happen at the same time (i.e.,  │
│  they are mutually exclusive), then the probability of either \( A \) or \(  │
│  B \) occurring is the sum of their individual probabilities.                │
│     That is, \( P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) \).                                │
│     *Example:* When drawing a single card from a standard deck, the          │
│  probability of drawing a King or a Queen is \( \frac{4}{52} + \frac{4}{52}  │
│  = \frac{8}{52} \), since no card can be both a King and a Queen.            │
│                                                                              │
│  These axioms ensure that probability assignments are logically consistent.  │
│  All of probability theory builds upon these three simple rules.             │

### **3. Key Misconceptions Identified**

- **Misunderstanding of Probability Axioms:**  
  The foundation of probability rests on three axioms:  
  1. **Non-Negativity:** $ P(A) \geq 0 $ for any event $ A $  
  2. **Unit Measure:** $ P(S) = 1 $, where $ S $ is the sample space  
  3. **Additivity:** For mutually exclusive (disjoint) events $ A $ and $ B $, $ P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) $
