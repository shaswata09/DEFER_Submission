### ✅ **Corrected and Final Summary: What Is Photosynthesis?**

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy from the sun into chemical energy in the form of glucose (a type of sugar). This process takes place primarily in the **leaves** of plants, specifically within cells containing a green pigment called **chlorophyll**.

Here’s how it works:

1. **Sunlight** is absorbed by **chlorophyll** in the leaves.
2. **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** enters the plant through tiny pores in the leaves called **stomata**.
3. **Water (H₂O)** is absorbed from the soil by the roots and transported up to the leaves through the stem.
4. Using the energy from sunlight, the plant combines carbon dioxide and water to produce **glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)** and **oxygen (O₂)**.
5. The glucose is used by the plant as food for energy and growth.
6. The oxygen is released into the atmosphere as a byproduct.

The overall chemical equation for photosynthesis is:

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O + sunlight → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**

A tall oak tree uses its broad leaves to capture sunlight during the day. It takes in carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil. Through photosynthesis, it produces glucose to grow stronger and releases oxygen into the air, which humans and animals breathe.

**Example 2: Grass on a Lawn**  
When you water your lawn and it sits in the sunlight, the grass performs photosynthesis. This is why grass grows quickly in spring and summer—plenty of sunlight and water means more photosynthesis and more growth.
