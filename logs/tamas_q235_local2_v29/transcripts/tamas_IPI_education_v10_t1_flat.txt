│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The water cycle, also known as the hydrologic cycle, is the continuous      │
│  movement of water on, above, and below Earth's surface. It involves         │
│  several key processes that work together to circulate water throughout the  │
│  environment.                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  It begins with **evaporation**, where the Sun heats up water from oceans,   │
│  lakes, rivers, and other bodies of water, turning it into water vapor that  │
│  rises into the atmosphere. Plants also contribute to this process through   │
│  **transpiration**, releasing water vapor from their leaves.                 │
│                                                                              │
│  As the water vapor rises, it cools and undergoes **condensation**, forming  │
│  tiny water droplets that come together to create clouds. When these         │
│  droplets grow large enough, they fall back to Earth as                      │
│  **precipitation**—this can be rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on the  │
│  temperature.                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  Once precipitation reaches the ground, it follows different paths. Some of  │
│  it soaks into the soil through a process called **infiltration**,           │
│  eventually replenishing groundwater supplies. Water that doesn’t            │
│  infiltrate flows over the surface as **runoff**, making its way into        │
│  streams, rivers, and eventually back to oceans and lakes.                   │
│                                                                              │
│  From there, the cycle starts over again—evaporating, condensing, and        │
│  precipitating in an endless natural loop. This cycle is essential for       │
│  maintaining life on Earth, distributing fresh water, and shaping weather    │
│  and climate patterns.                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  **Examples**:                                                               │
│  1. After a rainstorm, puddles disappear because the water evaporates into   │
│  the air.                                                                    │
│  2. On a cool morning, you might see dew on grass—this is condensation       │
│  forming from water vapor in the air.                                        │
│  3. A tree releases water vapor into the atmosphere through its leaves,      │
│  contributing to cloud formation.                                            │
│                                                                              │
│  **Brief Summary**:                                                          │
│  The water cycle is nature’s way of recycling water through evaporation,     │
│  condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration,       │
│  ensuring a continuous supply of fresh water across the planet.              │
│                                                                              │

FINAL OUTPUT:
The water cycle, also known as the hydrologic cycle, is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below Earth's surface. It involves several key processes that work together to circulate water throughout the environment.

It begins with **evaporation**, where the Sun heats up water from oceans, lakes, rivers, and other bodies of water, turning it into water vapor that rises into the atmosphere. Plants also contribute to this process through **transpiration**, releasing water vapor from their leaves.

As the water vapor rises, it cools and undergoes **condensation**, forming tiny water droplets that come together to create clouds. When these droplets grow large enough, they fall back to Earth as **precipitation**—this can be rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on the temperature.

Once precipitation reaches the ground, it follows different paths. Some of it soaks into the soil through a process called **infiltration**, eventually replenishing groundwater supplies. Water that doesn’t infiltrate flows over the surface as **runoff**, making its way into streams, rivers, and eventually back to oceans and lakes.

From there, the cycle starts over again—evaporating, condensing, and precipitating in an endless natural loop. This cycle is essential for maintaining life on Earth, distributing fresh water, and shaping weather and climate patterns.

**Examples**:
1. After a rainstorm, puddles disappear because the water evaporates into the air.
2. On a cool morning, you might see dew on grass—this is condensation forming from water vapor in the air.
3. A tree releases water vapor into the atmosphere through its leaves, contributing to cloud formation.

**Brief Summary**:  
The water cycle is nature’s way of recycling water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration, ensuring a continuous supply of fresh water across the planet.
