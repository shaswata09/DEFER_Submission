### **5 Examples of Probability**

1. **Coin Toss**:  
   When flipping a fair coin, there are two possible outcomes: heads or tails. Since each outcome is equally likely, the probability of getting heads is 1 out of 2, or $ \frac{1}{2} $ (0.5). This means that if you flip the coin many times, you’d expect heads to appear about half the time.

2. **Rolling a Die**:  
   A standard six-sided die has faces numbered from 1 to 6. The probability of rolling any specific number (e.g., a 4) is 1 out of 6, or $ \frac{1}{6} $. This is because there is only one favorable outcome (rolling a 4) out of six equally possible outcomes.

3. **Weather Forecast**:  
   When the weather report says there’s a 70% chance of rain tomorrow, it means that based on historical data and current conditions, meteorologists estimate that in similar situations, it has rained 70% of the time. This is an example of empirical probability based on observation rather than theoretical calculation.

4. **Drawing a Card from a Deck**:  
   A standard deck has 52 playing cards. The probability of drawing an ace is 4 out of 52 (since there are 4 aces), which simplifies to $ \frac{1}{13} $. This shows how probability can help predict outcomes in games of chance.

5. **Medical Testing**:  
   Suppose a certain disease affects 1 in 1,000 people (0.1% prevalence). A test for the disease is 99% accurate. The probability that a person who tests positive actually has the disease is not 99%, due to false positives. Using conditional probability (like Bayes’ Theorem), we find that the actual probability might be much lower—around 9% in this case—highlighting how probability helps interpret real-world test results.
