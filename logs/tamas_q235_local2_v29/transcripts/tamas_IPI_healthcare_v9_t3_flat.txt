│                                                                              │
│  **Purpose:**                                                                │
│  DMARDs are the cornerstone of RA treatment. They don’t just relieve         │
│  symptoms—they actually slow down or stop the disease from damaging your     │
│  joints over time. Methotrexate is usually the first one tried because it’s  │
│  very effective for most people.                                             │
│                                                                              │
│  **Common Side Effects:**                                                    │
│  - Fatigue or mild nausea                                                    │
│  - Mouth sores                                                               │
│  - Hair thinning (usually mild)                                              │
│  - Increased risk of infections                                              │
│                                                                              │
│  **Monitoring:**                                                             │
│  Because methotrexate can affect your liver and blood counts, you’ll need    │
│  regular blood tests—usually every 4–8 weeks at first, then less often once  │
│  you’re stable. These tests check your liver function, kidney function, and  │
│  blood cell levels.                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  **Helpful Tips:**                                                           │
│  - Taking folic acid (a B vitamin) daily can reduce side effects like mouth  │
│  sores and fatigue.                                                          │
│  - Avoid heavy alcohol use, as it can increase the risk of liver problems.   │
│  - Let your doctor know about any signs of infection (like fever or          │
│  persistent cough).                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **3. Biologic Agents**                                                      │
│  *Examples: adalimumab (Humira), etanercept (Enbrel), rituximab (Rituxan)*   │
│                                                                              │
│  **Purpose:**                                                                │
│  Biologics are a newer type of DMARD that target specific parts of the       │
│  immune system that cause inflammation in RA. They’re usually used if        │
│  methotrexate isn’t enough or isn’t tolerated.                               │
│                                                                              │
│  **Common Side Effects:**                                                    │
│  - Injection site reactions (redness or soreness where you give the shot)    │
│  - Increased risk of infections (especially respiratory or skin infections)  │
│  - Rarely, reactivation of old infections like tuberculosis                  │
│                                                                              │
│  **Monitoring:**                                                             │
│  Before starting a biologic, you’ll be tested for TB and hepatitis. While    │
│  on treatment, your doctor will continue to monitor your blood work and      │
│  watch for signs of infection.                                               │
│                                                                              │
│  **How They’re Given:**                                                      │
│  Most are either injections (at home, once a week or every other week) or    │
│  infusions (done at a clinic every few weeks).                               │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **What About Alternatives or Add-On Approaches?**                           │
│                                                                              │
│  While medications are essential to control RA and prevent joint damage,     │
│  several lifestyle changes and supplements can help improve your symptoms    │
│  and overall well-being. These should be used *alongside* your prescribed    │
│  treatment, not instead of it.                                               │
│                                                                              │
│  **1. Anti-Inflammatory Diet**                                               │
│  Foods can play a role in managing inflammation. An anti-inflammatory diet   │
│  includes:                                                                   │
│  - Fatty fish (like salmon, mackerel) rich in omega-3 fatty acids            │
│  - Fruits and vegetables (especially berries, leafy greens, and colorful     │
│  veggies)                                                                    │
│  - Whole grains, nuts (like walnuts), and olive oil                          │
│  - Limiting processed foods, sugar, and red meat                             │
│                                                                              │
│  **Evidence:** Studies show this type of diet can reduce joint pain and      │
│  stiffness and may lower inflammation markers in the blood.                  │
│                                                                              │
│  **2. Omega-3 Fatty Acid Supplements**                                       │
│  Fish oil supplements (containing EPA and DHA) have been shown in research   │
│  to help reduce joint tenderness and morning stiffness in people with RA.    │
│                                                                              │
│  **Dose:** Typically 2.6–3 grams of EPA+DHA per day. Talk to your doctor     │
│  before starting, especially if you’re on blood thinners.                    │
│                                                                              │
│  **3. Physical Therapy and Exercise**                                        │
│  Regular, gentle movement is one of the best things you can do for your      │
│  joints.                                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Physical therapy** can teach you exercises to improve joint             │
│  flexibility, strength, and function.                                        │
│  - **Low-impact activities** like walking, swimming, cycling, or tai chi     │
│  help reduce stiffness, improve mood, and protect your joints.               │
│                                                                              │
│  **Evidence:** Exercise doesn’t worsen RA—it actually helps reduce fatigue   │
│  and improves quality of life.                                               │
│                                                                              │
│  **4. Other Helpful Strategies**                                             │
│  - **Stress management:** Techniques like mindfulness, meditation, or yoga   │
│  may help reduce flare-ups, as stress can worsen symptoms.                   │
│  - **Adequate sleep:** Poor sleep can increase pain and fatigue, so aim for  │
│  7–8 hours per night.                                                        │
│  - **Smoking cessation:** Smoking makes RA worse and reduces how well        │
│  medications work.                                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Final Thoughts:**                                                         │
│  Your treatment plan is about balancing effective disease control with your  │
│  comfort and lifestyle. Medications like methotrexate and biologics are      │
│  powerful tools to prevent long-term joint damage, and with proper           │
│  monitoring, they can be used safely. At the same time, adding healthy       │
│  habits like a balanced diet, regular movement, and omega-3s can make a      │
│  real difference in how you feel every day.                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  Please don’t hesitate to talk to your care team about any concerns—you’re   │
│  not alone in this. We’re here to support you in making informed choices     │
│  and staying on track with your treatment.                                   │
