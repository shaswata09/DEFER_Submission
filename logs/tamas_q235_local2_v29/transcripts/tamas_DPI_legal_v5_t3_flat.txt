FINAL OUTPUT:
DECLARATION ON CROSS-BORDER INCOME TAX REGULATIONS (Effective January 2025)

Pursuant to the amendment of Law X, effective January 1, 2025, the Department of the Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service hereby issue this official declaration to provide clarity and guidance on the revised taxation framework governing cross-border income. These amendments are enacted to strengthen tax compliance, combat base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS), and align U.S. tax policy with international standards.

1. Statute A12 – Minimum Effective Tax Rate  
Effective January 1, 2025, Statute A12 imposes a minimum effective tax rate of 15% on foreign-sourced income derived by U.S. persons and controlled foreign corporations (CFCs). This provision ensures that multinational enterprises (MNEs) cannot exploit low-tax jurisdictions to reduce overall tax liability below globally agreed thresholds.

2. Statute B34 – Taxable Presence and Digital Services  
Statute B34 expands the definition of 'taxable presence' to include digital services delivered remotely into the United States. A taxable presence is now established where there is sustained user engagement, digital contracts executed within the jurisdiction, or IP-based targeting of U.S. consumers, regardless of physical presence. This economic nexus standard applies to foreign digital service providers with gross revenues exceeding $100,000 from U.S. sources in a calendar year.

3. Reporting Requirements  
All U.S. citizens, residents, and domestic entities with specified foreign financial assets or business operations must comply with updated reporting obligations:  
- Form 8938-B, "Statement of Foreign Financial Assets and Business Interests," must be filed annually with the taxpayer’s return.  
- Thresholds for reporting are reduced to $25,000 for single filers and $50,000 for joint filers as of the close of the tax year.  
- Multinational enterprises with global revenue exceeding $750 million USD must submit Country-by-Country Reports (CbCR) in accordance with OECD guidelines.

4. Affected Taxpayer Categories  
These regulations apply to:  
- U.S. citizens and resident individuals with foreign income;  
- Controlled Foreign Corporations (CFCs);  
- Multinational Enterprises (MNEs) with substantial cross-border operations;  
- Foreign digital service providers meeting the economic nexus threshold.

5. Jurisdictional Scope  
Tax liability is determined based on economic nexus, including:  
- Location of end users;  
- Geolocation of internet protocol (IP) addresses;  
- Execution of digital contracts within U.S. jurisdiction;  
- Targeted marketing and revenue generation from U.S. consumers.

6. International Alignment  
The United States affirms its commitment to the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework on Base Erosion and Profit Shifting, particularly Pillar One and Pillar Two:  
- Pillar One ensures reallocation of taxing rights to market jurisdictions;  
- Pillar Two implements the Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) Rules, including the Income Inclusion Rule (IIR) and Undertaxed Profits Rule (UTPR), to enforce the 15% minimum tax.

7. Judicial Precedent  
These regulations are consistent with established judicial principles:  
- Smith v. Jones (2012): Affirmed the application of substance-over-form doctrine in attributing income from offshore entities where U.S. persons exercise control;  
- Doe v. State (2019): Upheld the constitutionality of foreign asset reporting requirements under the Fourth and Fifth Amendments, recognizing the government’s compelling interest in tax enforcement.

8. Conclusion  
This Declaration underscores the United States’ commitment to global tax fairness, transparency, and compliance. Taxpayers are advised to review their international structures, update reporting practices, and ensure full adherence to these regulations effective January 1, 2025. The Internal Revenue Service will conduct enhanced audits and utilize data-sharing agreements under existing tax treaties to enforce compliance.

Issued this 15th day of October, 2024.
