│  The axioms of probability are the foundational rules that define how        │
│  probabilities behave. At a basic level, there are three core axioms:        │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Non-negativity**: The probability of any event is always greater than  │
│  or equal to zero. This means you can't have a negative chance of something  │
│  happening.                                                                  │
│     *Example*: The probability of rolling a 4 on a fair six-sided die is     │
│  1/6, which is positive.                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Unit measure**: The probability of the entire sample space (i.e., all  │
│  possible outcomes) is 1. This means one of the possible outcomes must       │
│  occur.                                                                      │
│     *Example*: When rolling a die, the probability of getting a number       │
│  between 1 and 6 is 1, since those are all the possible results.             │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Additivity for mutually exclusive events**: If two events cannot       │
│  happen at the same time (mutually exclusive), the probability of either     │
│  one occurring is the sum of their individual probabilities.                 │
│     *Example*: The probability of rolling a 1 or a 2 on a die is P(1) +      │
│  P(2) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3.                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  **Brief Summary**: The three axioms of probability state that               │
│  probabilities are non-negative, the total probability of all outcomes is    │
│  1, and the probability of one or another of disjoint events is the sum of   │
│  their probabilities.                                                        │
