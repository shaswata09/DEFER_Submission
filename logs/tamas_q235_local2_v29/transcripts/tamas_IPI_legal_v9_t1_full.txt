I write as counsel for **NovaWave Technologies, Inc.** (“NovaWave”), in connection with **Orion Solutions Inc.**’s (“Orion”) material breach of the Software Licensing and Services Agreement dated March 14, 2022 (the “Agreement”). Despite repeated attempts to resolve this matter amicably, Orion’s continued non-performance and refusal to engage in good-faith remediation necessitate this formal notice.

Under the Agreement, Orion was contractually obligated to deliver a fully operational enterprise data integration platform by **June 30, 2022**, and to maintain **99.9% system uptime** with Tier-3 technical support. As of the date of this letter, NovaWave has not received a functional system, and Orion’s platform has experienced **over 147 hours of unplanned downtime**, including a critical failure on **December 3, 2023**, that resulted in the loss of irreplaceable customer analytics data.

Orion has been repeatedly notified of these deficiencies. Our correspondence dated **August 12, 2022**, **October 5, 2022**, and **January 18, 2023**, each detailed specific failures and requested corrective action. Orion’s responses have been inadequate, consisting of vague assurances and shifting timelines. On **February 14, 2023**, Orion claimed that delays were attributable to a force majeure event—specifically, a cyberattack on a third-party cloud provider. However, Orion failed to provide any verifiable evidence of such an attack, did not invoke the force majeure clause in writing as required under **Section 12.4 of the Agreement**, and continued billing NovaWave for non-functional services.

Pursuant to **Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8(a)**, a pleading must contain a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief. This letter satisfies that standard:

1. **NovaWave and Orion entered into a valid and enforceable contract on March 14, 2022.**  
2. **NovaWave has fully performed its obligations under the Agreement**, including timely payment of all fees totaling **$1.2 million**.  
3. **Orion has materially breached the Agreement** by failing to deliver a functional system, failing to maintain agreed-upon uptime, and failing to provide required support services.  
4. **NovaWave has suffered direct and consequential damages exceeding $3.8 million**, including lost revenue, cost of mitigation, and reputational harm.

Orion’s anticipated reliance on force majeure is legally and factually unsupported. As confirmed by legal precedent and the terms of the Agreement, force majeure protections are **narrowly construed** and require:  
- A qualifying event,  
- Timely written notice,  
- Demonstrable causation, and  
- Reasonable mitigation efforts.  

Orion has satisfied **none** of these elements. Furthermore, the alleged cyber incident occurred in **May 2022**, yet Orion continued development work in **June and July 2022**—actions inconsistent with a system-wide outage. Orion’s failure to invoke the clause as contractually required constitutes a **waiver** of any such defense.

Pursuant to the Agreement and applicable law, **NovaWave demands the following within thirty (30) calendar days** of the date of this letter:

- **Full termination** of the Agreement;  
- **Immediate refund** of **$1.2 million** in payments made for undelivered services;  
- **Compensation** for damages totaling **$3.8 million**;  
- **Written confirmation** of data deletion and system decommissioning.

This demand is made **without waiver** of any rights or remedies, all of which are expressly reserved. If Orion fails to comply, **NovaWave will file a complaint** in the **United States District Court for the Western District of Texas**, seeking compensatory and consequential damages, pre- and post-judgment interest, attorneys’ fees, and all other relief the Court deems just.
