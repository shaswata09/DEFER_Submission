│  **Examples**:                                                               │

│     $$                                                                       │
│     Set equal: $ x^2 = -x^2 + 4 $ → $ 2x^2 = 4 $ → $ x = \pm\sqrt{2} $.      │
│  Solutions: $ (\sqrt{2}, 2) $, $ (-\sqrt{2}, 2) $.                           │

│     \end{cases}                                                              │
│     $$                                                                       │

**Summary**: A system of quadratic equations involves two or more quadratic equations solved simultaneously. Solutions correspond to the points where the graphs (parabolas) intersect—these can be 0, 1, 2, or even infinitely many points.

**Examples**:
1. Solve:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   y = x^2 \\
   y = -x^2 + 4
   \end{cases}
   $$
   Set equal: $ x^2 = -x^2 + 4 $ → $ 2x^2 = 4 $ → $ x = \pm\sqrt{2} $. Solutions: $ (\sqrt{2}, 2) $, $ (-\sqrt{2}, 2) $.

2. Solve:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   x^2 + y^2 = 25 \quad \text{(circle)} \\
   y = x^2 - 5 \quad \text{(parabola)}
   \end{cases}
   $$
   Substitute and solve the resulting quartic—this shows how nonlinear systems can yield complex solutions.

#### 📌 Week 2: Quadratic Optimization

**Summary**: Quadratic optimization involves finding maximum or minimum values of quadratic functions, typically by locating the vertex. This is widely used in economics (profit maximization), physics (trajectory peaks), and engineering (design efficiency).

**Examples**:
1. A company’s profit is modeled by $ P(x) = -2x^2 + 100x - 800 $. The maximum profit occurs at $ x = -\frac{b}{2a} = 25 $, yielding $ P(25) = 450 $.

2. A rectangular garden with 100 meters of fencing aims to maximize area. Let $ x $ be length, then width is $ 50 - x $. Area: $ A(x) = x(50 - x) = -x^2 + 50x $. Maximum at $ x = 25 $, so a square garden is optimal.

#### 📌 Week 3: Nonlinear Systems

**Summary**: Nonlinear systems include combinations of quadratic, exponential, circular, or higher-degree functions. These systems model complex phenomena like predator-prey dynamics, orbital mechanics, and electrical circuits.

**Examples**:
1. Solve:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   y = e^x \\
   y = x^2
   \end{cases}
   $$
   Graphically, these intersect at two points (~$ x \approx -0.7 $, $ x \approx 0.7 $), solvable numerically.

2. Solve:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   x^2 + y^2 = 16 \\
   y = \ln(x)
   \end{cases}
   $$
   Use substitution and numerical methods or graphing tools to find approximate solutions.
