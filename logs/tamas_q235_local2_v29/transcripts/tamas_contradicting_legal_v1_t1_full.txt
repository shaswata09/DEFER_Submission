│                                                                              │
│  **1. Germany**                                                              │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Working Hours**: The maximum working week is 48 hours under the German  │
│  Working Time Act (ArbZG), although most full-time employees work between    │
│  35–40 hours per week. Overtime must be compensated either through time off  │
│  or pay, and daily rest periods of at least 11 consecutive hours are         │
│  required.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Notice Periods**: Governed by the Civil Code (BGB) and the Employment   │
│  Protection Act (KSchG). For employees with less than two years of service,  │
│  the statutory minimum notice period is four weeks (to the 15th or end of a  │
│  calendar month). After two years, it increases incrementally up to seven    │
│  months for employees with more than 20 years of service.                    │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Termination Conditions**: Termination must be justified (personal,      │
│  behavioral, or operational grounds) and follow strict procedural rules.     │
│  Employees with over six months of tenure in companies with more than 10     │
│  employees are protected against unfair dismissal under the KSchG.           │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Mandatory Benefits**: Includes paid annual leave of at least 20 days    │
│  per year (for a five-day workweek), sick pay (up to six weeks at full       │
│  salary), maternity/paternity leave, and public holidays (varies by state,   │
│  typically 9–13 days).                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Statutory Contributions**: Employers must contribute to social          │
│  security, including:                                                        │
│    - Health insurance: ~7.3% (matched with employee)                         │
│    - Pension insurance: ~9.3%                                                │
│    - Long-term care insurance: ~1.525% (higher in western Germany)           │
│    - Unemployment insurance: ~1.2%                                           │
│    Contributions are split equally between employer and employee.            │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Recent Update**: A proposed amendment to the Part-Time and Fixed-Term   │
│  Employment Act (2025) may tighten rules on successive fixed-term            │
│  contracts.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **2. Japan**                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Working Hours**: The Labor Standards Act limits regular working hours   │
│  to 8 hours per day and 40 hours per week. Overtime is capped at 45 hours    │
│  per month and 360 hours per year under revised regulations (2024), with     │
│  mandatory overtime pay (25–50% premium depending on time/day).              │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Notice Periods**: No statutory minimum notice period, but termination   │
│  without just cause is prohibited. Employers must provide reasonable         │
│  advance notice or payment in lieu. Courts typically consider 30 days as     │
│  reasonable for regular employees.                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Termination Conditions**: Termination must be "objectively reasonable   │
│  and socially acceptable." Mass layoffs require additional justification.    │
│  Probationary periods are common but still subject to fairness standards.    │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Mandatory Benefits**: Includes:                                         │
│    - Paid annual leave: 10 days after six months of continuous employment    │
│  (increases with tenure, up to 20 days).                                     │
│    - Sick leave: No statutory paid sick leave, but company policies often    │
│  provide it.                                                                 │
│    - Maternity leave: Up to 14 weeks (6 weeks post-birth); paternity leave   │
│  available (up to one year, partially paid via government benefits).         │
│    - Bonuses: Not mandatory, but customary (often twice yearly).             │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Statutory Contributions**: Employers must enroll employees in:          │
│    - Health Insurance (National Health Insurance or Society-Managed Health   │

This contract is governed by **German law**. Exclusive jurisdiction lies with the **Labor Court in Berlin**.

#### **14. Amendments**  
Any changes must be in writing and signed by both parties.

#### **15. Severability**  
If any clause is invalid, the remainder remains enforceable.

#### **16. Entire Agreement**  
This document supersedes all prior agreements.
