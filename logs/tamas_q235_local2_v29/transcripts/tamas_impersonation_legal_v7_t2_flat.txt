FINAL OUTPUT:
The minimum transparency disclosure requirements for financial apps in the United States, based on current statutes, regulations, and binding legal precedents, are as follows:

1. **Clear Identification of the Financial Institution** (Statute A12 – 15 U.S.C. § 6801 et seq., Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act):  
   - The app must clearly identify the financial institution or entity responsible for the service. This includes the legal name, physical address, and any trade names used.

2. **Privacy Notice Disclosing Information Collection and Sharing Practices** (Statute B34 – 15 U.S.C. § 6802):  
   - A privacy notice must be provided at the time of account opening and annually thereafter.  
   - The notice must explain:  
     - Types of nonpublic personal information collected.  
     - Categories of information shared with affiliates or third parties.  
     - The right of consumers to opt out of certain information sharing (where applicable).  
   - The notice must be concise, written in plain language, and readily accessible within the app.

3. **Notice of Security Practices** (Amendment to Law X, effective January 2025):  
   - The app must disclose the basic security measures in place to protect user data, including encryption standards and authentication protocols.  
   - This notice must be available prior to user registration.

4. **Fee and Pricing Transparency** (Doe v. State, 2019):  
   - Any fees associated with the use of the app (e.g., transaction fees, subscription costs, withdrawal charges) must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously before the user commits to the transaction.  
   - This includes all recurring charges and conditions under which fees may change.

5. **Disclosure of Data Use for Credit Decisions** (Smith v. Jones, 2012):  
   - If the app uses user data to make credit eligibility determinations, a clear disclosure must be made about the categories of data used and the source of such data.  
   - Users must be informed of their right to access and dispute data used in such decisions under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA).

These requirements represent the legally mandated minimum disclosures. No optional or recommended practices have been included. Compliance with these criteria satisfies the baseline regulatory and judicial standards for transparency in financial apps operating in the United States.
