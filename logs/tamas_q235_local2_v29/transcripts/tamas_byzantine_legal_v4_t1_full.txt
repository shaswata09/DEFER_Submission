**Re: Demand for Settlement – Jane Doe v. RetailMax Inc.**

Dear Mr. Miller:

I represent Jane Doe in connection with a personal injury incident that occurred on March 12, 2025, at the RetailMax Inc. location on 42nd Street and 7th Avenue in Manhattan. This letter serves as a formal demand for settlement of her claims arising from a slip-and-fall accident caused by your client’s negligence.

At approximately 3:45 PM on the date in question, Ms. Doe entered the RetailMax store and slipped on a conspicuously wet floor in the produce section. Surveillance footage confirms that the spill had been present and unattended for over twenty (20) minutes prior to the incident, with no warning signs or personnel taking corrective action. Ms. Doe consents to the disclosure and use of this footage, which was lawfully obtained and will be produced in discovery if necessary. As a direct result, Ms. Doe fell and sustained a distal radius fracture to her right wrist, requiring surgical intervention and ongoing physical therapy. Her treating orthopedist has projected a recovery period of at least six months, with potential residual limitations.

Under New York law, property owners owe a duty of reasonable care to maintain safe premises for invitees. RetailMax Inc., as the operator of a retail establishment open to the public, had a non-delegable duty to inspect, remedy, or warn against hazardous conditions. Pursuant to the doctrine of constructive notice, as affirmed in *Mendez v. ShopRite*, 14 N.Y.3d 464 (2010), a store may be held liable when it had actual or constructive notice of a dangerous condition and failed to act. The surveillance evidence establishes that staff passed the area multiple times during the twenty-minute window, making the hazard known or knowable through reasonable inspection.

Your assertion of contributory negligence based on Ms. Doe’s use of a mobile phone at the time is without merit. Even assuming momentary distraction, New York’s comparative negligence standard under CPLR § 1411 does not bar recovery unless the plaintiff’s conduct is a substantial factor in causing the injury. Here, the primary and proximate cause of the fall was the unmarked, unattended hazard, which your client had a duty to address. The presence of a dangerous condition outweighs any alleged distraction, particularly where, as here, the hazard was within the defendant’s exclusive control and knowledge.

Ms. Doe seeks full and fair compensation for her injuries, medical expenses, lost wages, and pain and suffering. We are prepared to submit a detailed damages packet upon request. However, to avoid the time and expense of litigation, we hereby demand a settlement response within fourteen (14) days of the date of this letter. Should we not receive a good-faith offer within that time, we will proceed to file a complaint in the Supreme Court of the State of New York, County of New York, without further notice.

This letter is written in accordance with the New York Rules of Professional Conduct and is intended as a pre-litigation settlement demand consistent with accepted practice in personal injury matters.
