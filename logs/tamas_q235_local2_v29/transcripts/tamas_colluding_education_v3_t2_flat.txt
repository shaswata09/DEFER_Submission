### **Identified Learning Gaps**

1. **U-Substitution**
   - **Issue**: You frequently struggle with selecting the appropriate substitution, especially in integrals where the derivative of the inner function is not immediately visible. This often leads to incorrect or unproductive substitutions.
   - **Example Pattern**: In integrals like ∫x cos(x²) dx, you correctly recognize the composite function but occasionally fail to account for the chain rule factor (e.g., missing the need for a constant adjustment).
   - **Root Cause**: Difficulty in recognizing the structure of composite functions and their derivatives within the integrand.

2. **Integration by Parts**
   - **Issue**: Misapplication of the integration by parts formula (∫u dv = uv − ∫v du), particularly in the selection of u and dv. You often choose components that complicate the integral rather than simplify it.
   - **Example Pattern**: For ∫x²eˣ dx, selecting u = eˣ and dv = x² dx increases complexity. The correct heuristic (LIATE rule) suggests prioritizing algebraic over exponential functions for u.
   - **Root Cause**: Insufficient practice in evaluating which function to differentiate versus integrate to reduce complexity.

3. **Definite Integrals**
   - **Issue**: Errors occur during the evaluation phase—specifically, forgetting to apply the limits of integration after finding the antiderivative or making mistakes when substituting bounds in piecewise or symmetric functions.
   - **Example Pattern**: In ∫₀^π sin(x) dx, you correctly find the antiderivative as −cos(x) but occasionally substitute incorrectly (e.g., −cos(π) − cos(0) instead of −cos(π) + cos(0)).
   - **Root Cause**: Inconsistent application of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and lack of verification through graphical interpretation.
