FINAL OUTPUT:
Photosynthesis is a process that plants use to create energy by absorbing sunlight during the day. It primarily occurs in the leaves, where chlorophyll in chloroplasts captures light energy and converts carbon dioxide and water into glucose (a form of sugar) and oxygen. The main inputs are sunlight, carbon dioxide (from the air), and water (absorbed from the soil). The outputs are glucose, which the plant uses for energy and growth, and oxygen, which is released into the atmosphere.

This process is essential not only for plant survival but also for life on Earth, as it produces the oxygen we breathe and forms the base of the food chain.

**Real-World Examples:**

1. **Trees in a Forest:** During the day, trees absorb sunlight through their leaves, take in carbon dioxide from the air, and draw water from the ground through their roots. They use these to produce glucose for growth and release oxygen, which animals and humans breathe.

2. **Houseplants on a Sunny Windowsill:** A potted plant placed near a window grows better because it receives ample sunlight. The leaves turn toward the light to maximize photosynthesis, producing more energy for the plant to thrive.
