│  the EU context. In the U.S., the Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) of 2016    │
│  provides a federal civil cause of action consistent with TRIPS standards.   │
│                                                                              │
│  **2. U.S. Legal Framework: Trade Secret Protection and NDA                  │
│  Enforceability**                                                            │
│                                                                              │
│  In the United States, NDAs are generally enforceable if they meet basic     │

│  - **Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA)**: Allows for federal jurisdiction in   │
│  trade secret misappropriation cases and provides for injunctive relief,     │
│  damages, and, in exceptional cases, exemplary damages.                      │
│  - **Uniform Trade Secrets Act (UTSA)**: Adopted in some form by nearly all  │
│  U.S. states, offering consistent state-level protection.                    │
│                                                                              │
│  Recent updates (as of January 2025) to U.S. trade secret laws emphasize     │
│  the necessity of documenting reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy—such    │
│  as signed NDAs, access controls, and employee training—as a prerequisite    │
│  for legal protection. Courts have upheld NDAs where specificity is          │
│  present; vague or overbroad provisions may be struck down or narrowed       │
│  under the doctrine of overreach.                                            │
│                                                                              │
│  Case law such as *Whyte v. Schlage Lock Co.* (2002) confirms that           │
│  well-drafted NDAs are critical to establishing the existence of a           │
│  protectable trade secret.                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  **3. EU Legal Framework: GDPR and Data Confidentiality**                    │
│                                                                              │
│  In the European Union, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)        │
│  significantly impacts how confidentiality obligations are structured,       │
│  particularly when personal data is involved. While NDAs primarily govern    │
│  trade secrets and proprietary information, if the shared information        │
│  includes personal data (e.g., employee records, customer data), GDPR        │
│  compliance becomes mandatory.                                               │
│                                                                              │

│  - **Data Minimization**: Only the minimum necessary personal data should    │
│  be disclosed.                                                               │
│  - **Obligations of Data Processors**: If one party processes personal data  │
│  on behalf of the other, a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) must supplement   │

│  DPF where applicable.                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  Precedents such as *Schrems II* (C-311/18) underscore the need for          │
│  supplementary measures when transferring data to jurisdictions without an   │

│  **4. Bilateral and Multilateral Instruments**                               │
│                                                                              │
│  The U.S. and EU cooperate on IP protection through bilateral dialogues and  │

│  - **U.S.-EU Trade and Technology Council (TTC)**: Facilitates alignment on  │
│  emerging tech and IP standards.                                             │
│  - **Bilateral Investment Treaties (BITs)** and **Extradition Agreements**:  │

│  for international disputes due to enforceability under the New York         │
