│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The primary legal instruments governing international arbitration include   │
│  both international treaties and national legislation, which together form   │
│  a robust framework facilitating the recognition and enforcement of          │
│  arbitration agreements and awards across jurisdictions.                     │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral      │
│  Awards (1958) – The New York Convention**                                   │
│     - One of the most significant international treaties in the field of     │
│  arbitration.                                                                │
│     - Ratified by over 170 countries, including the United States, the       │
│  United Kingdom, Germany, China, and most major commercial jurisdictions.    │
│     - Requires contracting states to recognize written arbitration           │
│  agreements and to enforce foreign arbitral awards rendered in other         │
│  contracting states, subject to limited exceptions (e.g., incapacity, due    │
│  process violations, public policy).                                         │
│     - Status: In full force and widely implemented. Most jurisdictions have  │
│  incorporated its provisions into domestic law.                              │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (1985,      │
│  with 2006 amendments)**                                                     │
│     - Developed by the United Nations Commission on International Trade Law  │
│  (UNCITRAL).                                                                 │
│     - Designed to harmonize and modernize national arbitration laws.         │
│     - Adopted in whole or in part by over 80 countries, including Canada     │
│  (via provincial legislation), Australia, Singapore, India (as a guiding     │
│  framework), and several U.S. states.                                        │
│     - The 2006 amendments clarified provisions on interim measures, ad hoc   │
│  proceedings, and the scope of court intervention.                           │
│     - Status: Widely influential; jurisdictions that have adopted it use it  │
│  as the foundation for their arbitration statutes.                           │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **U.S. Federal Arbitration Act (FAA), 9 U.S.C. §§ 1–16 (Chapter 1) and   │
│  §§ 201–208 (Chapter 2 – Implementation of the New York Convention)**        │
│     - Governs arbitration in the United States.                              │
│     - Chapter 1 applies to both domestic and international arbitration       │
│  within the U.S.                                                             │
│     - Chapter 2 implements the New York Convention, enabling enforcement of  │
│  foreign awards in U.S. courts.                                              │
│     - U.S. courts generally uphold arbitration agreements and awards,        │
│  consistent with pro-arbitration policy.                                     │
│     - Status: Fully in force; regularly applied by federal and state         │
│  courts.                                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  4. **UK Arbitration Act 1996**                                              │
│     - Governs arbitration in England and Wales and Northern Ireland.         │
│     - Based on the UNCITRAL Model Law but with distinct features, such as    │
│  broad judicial discretion to appeal on points of law.                       │
│     - Emphasizes party autonomy, minimal court intervention, and finality    │
│  of awards.                                                                  │
│     - Includes specific provisions for international arbitrations seated in  │
│  England.                                                                    │
│     - Status: In full effect; considered one of the most                     │
│  arbitration-friendly legal regimes.                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  5. **International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes Convention  │
│  (1965) – ICSID Convention**                                                 │
│     - Administered by the World Bank.                                        │
│     - Applies to investor-state arbitration, not commercial arbitration      │
│  between private parties.                                                    │
│     - Provides a framework for conciliation and arbitration of investment    │
│  disputes between states and nationals of other states.                      │
│     - Over 160 member states; awards are binding and enforceable directly    │
│  in member jurisdictions without recourse to national courts.                │
│     - Status: Active and widely used in investment treaty arbitration.       │
│                                                                              │
│  6. **European Convention on International Commercial Arbitration (1961,     │
│  Geneva)**                                                                   │
│     - Pre-dates the New York Convention but remains in force for certain     │
│  European states.                                                            │
│     - Provides similar enforcement mechanisms but has largely been           │
│  superseded by the New York Convention in practice.                          │
│     - Signatories include France, Germany, Italy, and Switzerland.           │
│     - Status: Still applicable but secondary to the New York Convention.     │
│                                                                              │
│  7. **International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) Arbitration Rules**            │
│     - While not a statute or treaty, these institutional rules are widely    │
│  used in international arbitration.                                          │
│     - Provide a procedural framework for administering cross-border          │
│  disputes.                                                                   │
