**MEMORANDUM OF LAW IN SUPPORT OF LIABILITY**  

**I. FACTUAL BACKGROUND**  

On or about June 15, 2022, Plaintiff Jane Doe underwent an elective abdominal surgery at General Hospital performed by Defendant Dr. John Smith for the resection of a diagnosed gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST). Preoperative imaging and intraoperative findings indicated that complete tumor resection was medically feasible and within the standard of surgical care.  

Despite this, Dr. Smith failed to achieve complete resection, leaving residual tumor tissue in the abdominal cavity. This deviation from the accepted standard of care necessitated a second surgical intervention on July 10, 2022, during which significant infection and septic complications were discovered. Ms. Doe subsequently developed sepsis, requiring intensive care treatment for twelve (12) days. Her recovery was prolonged, and she incurred substantial medical expenses, lost wages, and ongoing physical and emotional distress.  

**II. LEGAL STANDARD: MEDICAL MALPRACTICE IN NEW YORK**  

To establish medical malpractice under New York law, a plaintiff must demonstrate: (1) a deviation or departure from the accepted standard of care by a healthcare provider; (2) that such deviation was a proximate cause of the patient’s injuries; and (3) resultant damages. *See Hohner v. Estate of Klein*, 159 A.D.3d 439, 441 (1st Dept. 2018); *Coppa v. Coppola*, 150 A.D.3d 859, 860 (2d Dept. 2017).  

**III. DUTY AND BREACH**  

Defendant Dr. Smith owed a duty of care to Plaintiff Jane Doe as her treating surgeon. This duty included adhering to the prevailing standard of care for abdominal tumor resection, which requires complete removal of resectable tumors to prevent recurrence and complications. The failure to perform a complete resection, when both feasible and medically indicated, constitutes a clear departure from that standard. *See Daly v. Bosque*, 266 A.D.2d 139 (1st Dept. 1999) (affirming liability where surgeon failed to remove entire lesion despite ability to do so).  

Defendant’s incomplete procedure falls squarely within recognized breaches of surgical duty. Expert testimony will confirm that complete resection was possible and expected under the circumstances.  

**IV. CAUSATION**  

The direct causal link between Dr. Smith’s incomplete surgery and Ms. Doe’s subsequent complications is well established. Residual tumor tissue created a nidus for infection, leading to peritonitis and sepsis. But-for the failure to fully excise the tumor, the second surgery and systemic infection would not have occurred. The temporal proximity and medical records support this causal chain.  

New York courts consistently recognize that a departure from the standard of care that directly leads to foreseeable harm satisfies proximate cause. *Hohner*, 159 A.D.3d at 441.  

**V. DAMAGES**  

As a direct and proximate result of Defendant’s negligence, Plaintiff has suffered:  

- $215,000 in past and future medical expenses;  
- $50,000 in lost earnings and diminished earning capacity;  
- Severe pain and suffering, mental anguish, and loss of enjoyment of life.  

Total compensatory damages are estimated at $300,000.  

**VI. CONCLUSION**  

Plaintiff respectfully requests that this Court find that Defendant Dr. John Smith breached the standard of care, that such breach proximately caused Plaintiff’s injuries, and that she is entitled to full and just compensation under New York law. Precedents including *Hohner*, *Coppa*, and *Daly* support the imposition of liability where, as here, a feasible and necessary surgical procedure is negligently left incomplete.
