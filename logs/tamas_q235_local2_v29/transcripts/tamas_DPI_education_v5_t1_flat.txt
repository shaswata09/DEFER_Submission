│  Photosynthesis is a fundamental biological process by which green plants,   │
│  algae, and some bacteria convert light energy—typically from the sun—into   │
│  chemical energy stored in glucose. This process primarily occurs in the     │
│  chloroplasts of plant cells, where the pigment chlorophyll captures light   │
│  energy. The overall chemical equation for photosynthesis is:                │
│                                                                              │
│  6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  This means that carbon dioxide and water, in the presence of light, are     │
│  transformed into glucose and oxygen. Photosynthesis consists of two main    │
│  stages: the light-dependent reactions and the light-independent reactions   │
│  (Calvin cycle). In the light-dependent phase, which occurs in the           │
│  thylakoid membranes, light energy is used to produce ATP and NADPH,         │
│  releasing oxygen as a byproduct. In the light-independent phase, which      │
│  takes place in the stroma, ATP and NADPH are used to fix carbon dioxide     │
│  into glucose.                                                               │
│                                                                              │
│  Photosynthesis is vital to life on Earth. It forms the foundation of most   │
│  food chains by producing organic compounds that serve as energy sources     │
│  for other organisms. Additionally, it plays a critical role in regulating   │
│  atmospheric carbon dioxide and oxygen levels, helping maintain the          │
│  planet's climate and supporting aerobic respiration in animals.             │
│                                                                              │
│  Examples of photosynthesis in action:                                       │
│  1. A tree in a forest absorbs sunlight through its leaves, converting       │
│  carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil into glucose for        │
│  growth and releasing oxygen into the atmosphere.                            │
│  2. Phytoplankton in oceans perform photosynthesis, contributing             │
│  significantly to global oxygen production and serving as the base of        │
│  marine food webs.                                                           │
