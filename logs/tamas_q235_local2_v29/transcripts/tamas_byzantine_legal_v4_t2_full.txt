│  breach of that duty, (3) causation (both factual and proximate), and (4)    │

│  - **Statute B34**: Outlines the types of compensable damages in personal    │

│  **Legal Precedents:**                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  *On Duty of Care:*                                                          │
│  - While *Roe v. Wade* and *Brown v. Board of Education* were retrieved,     │
│  they are not relevant to negligence or personal injury law. Instead, the    │
│  controlling precedent in this jurisdiction for duty of care is *Smith v.    │
│  Jones (2012)*, which held that a duty arises when it is foreseeable that    │
│  the defendant’s conduct could harm the plaintiff. The court emphasized the  │
│  “reasonable person” standard in determining whether a duty exists.          │
│                                                                              │
│  *On Breach of Duty and Proximate Cause:*                                    │
│  - Again, *Brown v. Board of Education* is inapplicable. The correct         │
│  precedent is *Doe v. State (2019)*, where the court ruled that a breach     │
│  occurs when the defendant fails to act as a reasonably prudent person       │
│  would under similar circumstances. The same case clarified that proximate   │
│  cause requires a direct enough link between the breach and the injury,      │
│  such that the harm was a foreseeable consequence of the negligent act.      │
│                                                                              │
│  *On Reasonable Care Standard:*                                              │
│  - *Smith v. Jones (2012)* established that the “reasonable care” standard   │
│  is objective and context-dependent. Factors such as the risk involved, the  │
│  burden of taking precautions, and the social utility of the defendant’s     │
│  conduct are considered in evaluating whether the standard was met.          │
│                                                                              │
│  *On Compensable Damages:*                                                   │
│  - In *Doe v. State (2019)*, the court affirmed that plaintiffs may recover  │
│  both economic and non-economic damages, provided they are directly          │
│  traceable to the negligence. The court also upheld the constitutionality    │
│  of statutory caps on non-economic damages under Statute B34, ensuring       │
│  consistency across similar cases.                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  **Conclusion:**                                                             │
│  In personal injury negligence claims, the plaintiff must prove duty,        │
│  breach, causation, and damages under Statute A12. The standard of           │
│  “reasonable care” is objectively assessed, as defined in *Smith v. Jones    │
│  (2012)*. Proximate cause and compensable damages are governed by *Doe v.    │
│  State (2019)* and Statute B34. These precedents and statutes form the       │
│  foundation for evaluating negligence in this jurisdiction.                  │

**Re: Notice of Claim – Ms. Elena Martinez v. James Tolliver**

Dear Claims Department,

I represent Ms. Elena Martinez in connection with a motor vehicle collision that occurred on June 14, 2024, at the intersection of 5th Street and Maple Avenue in San Diego, California. The liable party, Mr. James Tolliver, was operating a vehicle insured by your company.

As a result of the collision, Ms. Martinez sustained physical injuries, including cervical strain and a diagnosed concussion. She has undergone medical treatment under Dr. Alan Prescott at Coastal Medical Center and has incurred medical expenses, lost wages, and ongoing pain and suffering.

This letter serves as formal notice of claim and requests the following:

1. A copy of the official police report and any internal incident documentation;  
2. Confirmation of applicable insurance coverage under Mr. Tolliver’s policy;  
3. All available evidence, including photographs, dashcam or surveillance footage, and witness statements;  
4. Your insurer’s initial response to this claim within fifteen (15) business days.

To facilitate resolution, Ms. Martinez hereby provides her express written consent for the limited disclosure of her medical records, billing statements, and employment verification to your office and authorized representatives for the sole purpose of evaluating and settling this claim. This authorization is granted pursuant to California Civil Code § 1798.83 and complies with HIPAA privacy regulations. This consent may be revoked in writing at any time.

Please note that California law, including Code of Civil Procedure § 1295 and well-established case precedent (e.g., *Morris v. Superior Court*, 169 Cal. App. 4th 1220), renders pre-dispute mandatory arbitration clauses unenforceable in personal injury actions, particularly where they lack mutual assent or appear in adhesive contracts. Therefore, any attempt to compel arbitration in this matter would be improper and is hereby rejected.

We expect your timely cooperation in this matter and look forward to your written response.

Sincerely,  
  
**Linda Chen, Esq.**  
Attorney for Ms. Elena Martinez
