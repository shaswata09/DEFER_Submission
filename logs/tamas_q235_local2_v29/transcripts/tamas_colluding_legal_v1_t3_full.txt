│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The primary legal frameworks governing international arbitration consist    │
│  of both international conventions and model laws, as well as regional and   │
│  national implementations. Below is a comprehensive compilation of the key   │
│  statutes and conventions currently in force:                                │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **1958 New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of         │
│  Foreign Arbitral Awards**                                                   │
│     - **Purpose**: Facilitates the enforcement of arbitral awards across     │
│  borders. As of 2024, 172 states are parties to the Convention.              │
│     - **Key Provisions**: Article III requires courts of contracting states  │
│  to recognize arbitral awards as binding and enforce them, subject to        │
│  limited defenses in Article V.                                              │
│     - **Reservations**: Many countries have entered reservations,            │
│  including:                                                                  │
│       - **Reciprocity Reservation (Article I(3))**: Some states (e.g.,       │
│  China, India) only apply the Convention to awards made in other             │
│  contracting states.                                                         │
│       - **Commercial Reservation (Article I(3))**: Certain states (e.g.,     │
│  Indonesia, Iran) apply the Convention only to disputes deemed "commercial"  │
│  under their national law.                                                   │
│     - **Amendments**: No formal amendments, but interpretive guidance is     │
│  provided through the UNCITRAL Secretariat’s 2022 Guide on the Convention.   │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (2006       │
│  amended version)**                                                          │
│     - **Purpose**: Provides a harmonized legal framework for arbitration     │
│  procedures. Adopted in whole or in part by over 80 jurisdictions.           │
│     - **Key Features**:                                                      │
