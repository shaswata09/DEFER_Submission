### 1. **Chlorophyll and Light Absorption**
- **Explanation**: Chlorophyll captures light energy for photosynthesis, absorbing red and blue light most efficiently while reflecting green light—this is why plants appear green.
- **Example**: Under red light, photosynthesis is efficient because chlorophyll absorbs the energy. Under green light, photosynthesis slows because the light is mostly reflected, not absorbed.
- **Fall Leaves Analogy**: When chlorophyll breaks down in autumn, yellow and orange pigments (carotenoids) become visible, showing that chlorophyll was masking them during the growing season.

### 2. **Net Primary Productivity (NPP = GPP - R)**
- **Explanation**: 
  - **GPP** = total energy captured by photosynthesis.
  - **R** = energy used by the plant for respiration (life processes).
  - **NPP** = energy remaining for growth and available to consumers: **NPP = GPP - R**.
- **Paycheck Analogy**: GPP is like your salary; R is your bills; NPP is what’s left to save or spend. You subtract bills—you don’t add them.
- **Plant Growth Example**: A healthy plant with high GPP and moderate R has high NPP (lots of growth). A stressed plant may have high GPP but high R, resulting in low NPP.

### 3. **Limiting Factors in Photosynthesis**
- **Explanation**: The rate of photosynthesis is limited by the factor in shortest supply—light, CO₂, or temperature.
- **Forest Floor Example**: A plant under a dense canopy gets little light, even if CO₂ and temperature are ideal. Thus, **light intensity** is the limiting factor.
- **Greenhouse Example**: Increasing CO₂ or temperature won’t boost growth if lights are dim. Only increasing light increases photosynthesis—proving light is the bottleneck.
