### Explanation of Linear Equations

A **linear equation** is an algebraic equation in which each term is either a constant or the product of a constant and a single variable raised to the first power. These equations form straight lines when graphed on a coordinate plane.

The most common form of a linear equation in two variables is the **slope-intercept form**:
$$
y = mx + b
$$
where:
- $m$ represents the **slope** of the line (the rate at which $y$ changes with respect to $x$),
- $b$ is the **y-intercept** (the value of $y$ when $x = 0$, or where the line crosses the y-axis).

For example, consider a monthly phone plan that charges a flat fee of $20 plus $0.10 per minute of usage. The total cost ($C$) based on minutes used ($m$) can be modeled by the linear equation:
$$
C = 0.10m + 20
$$
If you use 100 minutes, the total cost would be:
$$
C = 0.10(100) + 20 = 10 + 20 = \$30
$$
This relationship is linear because the cost increases at a constant rate.

Linear equations are foundational in algebra and are used to model real-world relationships involving constant rates of change, such as speed, pricing, and growth trends.
