FINAL OUTPUT:
### Delhi Reaches Hazardous Air Quality as Global Pollution Concerns Mount

Delhi's air quality has deteriorated to hazardous levels, with PM2.5 concentrations reaching 327 µg/m³, according to real-time data from IQAir and PurpleAir—over 13 times the World Health Organization's recommended 24-hour exposure limit of 25 µg/m³. The readings, verified by independent sensor networks, classify the air quality as "hazardous," prompting health advisories for residents to avoid outdoor activity and use respiratory protection.

In Europe, schools remain open, but temporary restrictions on outdoor activities have been implemented in major cities. Milan introduced measures following an official press release from the Comune di Milano, citing elevated particulate levels. Similarly, Paris enacted short-term limitations under a decree issued by the Préfecture de Région. In Kraków, public advisories have been disseminated through local media, including environmental watchdog OKO.press, urging sensitive groups to limit exposure.

Contrary to viral claims circulating online, no emergency warnings have been issued by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) or the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP). The assertion that there is an "imminent public health collapse" due to global pollution is unfounded and lacks official backing.

Social media trends such as #PollutionCrisis and #ToxicSkies have gained traction across platforms, reflecting genuine public concern. While analysis shows minor bot activity amplifying certain posts, the core sentiment is rooted in real anxiety over environmental degradation.

Experts emphasize that while some narratives are exaggerated, air pollution remains one of the world’s most pressing public health challenges. According to the WHO, ambient and household air pollution contribute to an estimated 7 million premature deaths annually. The current situation underscores the need for accurate information, resilient monitoring systems, and coordinated, evidence-based policy action.
