│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below    │
│  Earth’s surface. It’s a natural recycling system that ensures water is      │
│  constantly being used and reused. The cycle involves several key            │
│  processes: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff,  │
│  and transpiration.                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  **Evaporation** happens when the sun heats up water from oceans, lakes, or  │
│  rivers, turning it into water vapor that rises into the air. For example,   │
│  after a rainstorm, puddles disappear because the water evaporates into the  │
│  atmosphere.                                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  **Condensation** occurs when water vapor in the air cools down and changes  │
│  back into tiny liquid droplets, forming clouds. You can see a small-scale   │
│  version of this when water droplets form on the outside of a cold drink     │
│  can on a hot day.                                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  **Precipitation** is when water falls from the sky in forms like rain,      │
│  snow, sleet, or hail. When clouds become heavy with water droplets, they    │
│  release the water back to Earth—like when it rains after a cloudy           │
│  afternoon.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  **Infiltration** is the process where water soaks into the ground, moving   │
│  into soil and rock layers. After a rainfall, you might notice that the      │
│  ground becomes damp—that’s water infiltrating the soil.                     │
│                                                                              │
│  **Runoff** is when water flows over the land surface instead of soaking     │
│  in, often making its way into streams, rivers, and eventually back to the   │
│  ocean. For instance, during heavy rain, water runs down streets and into    │
│  storm drains—that’s runoff.                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  **Transpiration** is the release of water vapor from plants into the air    │
│  through tiny openings in their leaves. It’s like how people sweat to cool   │
│  down—plants “breathe out” water vapor, especially on warm, sunny days.      │
│                                                                              │
│  Together, these processes keep water moving and available for living        │
│  things. The water you drink today could have once been part of an ocean, a  │
│  cloud, or even a dinosaur’s drink millions of years ago!                    │
│                                                                              │

FINAL OUTPUT:
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below Earth’s surface. It’s a natural recycling system that ensures water is constantly being used and reused. The cycle involves several key processes: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration.

**Evaporation** happens when the sun heats up water from oceans, lakes, or rivers, turning it into water vapor that rises into the air. For example, after a rainstorm, puddles disappear because the water evaporates into the atmosphere.

**Condensation** occurs when water vapor in the air cools down and changes back into tiny liquid droplets, forming clouds. You can see a small-scale version of this when water droplets form on the outside of a cold drink can on a hot day.

**Precipitation** is when water falls from the sky in forms like rain, snow, sleet, or hail. When clouds become heavy with water droplets, they release the water back to Earth—like when it rains after a cloudy afternoon.

**Infiltration** is the process where water soaks into the ground, moving into soil and rock layers. After a rainfall, you might notice that the ground becomes damp—that’s water infiltrating the soil.

**Runoff** is when water flows over the land surface instead of soaking in, often making its way into streams, rivers, and eventually back to the ocean. For instance, during heavy rain, water runs down streets and into storm drains—that’s runoff.

**Transpiration** is the release of water vapor from plants into the air through tiny openings in their leaves. It’s like how people sweat to cool down—plants “breathe out” water vapor, especially on warm, sunny days.

Together, these processes keep water moving and available for living things. The water you drink today could have once been part of an ocean, a cloud, or even a dinosaur’s drink millions of years ago!
