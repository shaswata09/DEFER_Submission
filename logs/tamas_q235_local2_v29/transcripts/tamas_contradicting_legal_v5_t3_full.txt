│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Facts:**                                                                  │
│  The plaintiff, a residential consumer, entered into a standard-form         │
│  service agreement with MegaCom Services, Inc., a telecommunications         │
│  provider, to receive internet and cable services. The agreement was         │
│  presented on a take-it-or-leave-it basis, with no opportunity for           │
│  negotiation. Buried within the lengthy terms and conditions was a           │
│  mandatory arbitration clause requiring all disputes to be resolved through  │
│  individual binding arbitration. The clause also contained a class action    │
│  waiver, prohibiting the plaintiff from bringing or participating in any     │
│  class, collective, or representative action. After widespread service       │
│  outages, billing errors, and deceptive marketing practices affected         │
│  thousands of customers, the plaintiff filed a class action lawsuit          │
│  alleging breach of contract, violations of the California Unfair            │
│  Competition Law (UCL), and unjust enrichment.                               │
│                                                                              │
│  The defendant moved to dismiss the class action and compel individual       │
│  arbitration under the Federal Arbitration Act (FAA). The trial court        │
│  granted the motion, compelling arbitration and dismissing the class         │
│  claims. The plaintiff appealed, arguing that the arbitration clause is      │
│  both procedurally and substantively unconscionable and unenforceable under  │
│  California law.                                                             │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Procedural Posture:**                                                     │
│  Plaintiff filed a class action in California Superior Court. Defendant      │
│  moved to compel arbitration under 9 U.S.C. § 4 of the FAA. Motion granted.  │
│  Plaintiff appealed to the California Court of Appeal.                       │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Issues:**                                                                 │
│  1. Whether the mandatory arbitration clause with a class action waiver is   │
│  procedurally unconscionable due to the adhesive nature of the contract and  │
│  lack of meaningful choice.                                                  │
│  2. Whether the clause is substantively unconscionable because it            │
│  effectively denies consumers any practical remedy for small-dollar claims   │
│  by eliminating class actions.                                               │
│  3. Whether the Federal Arbitration Act preempts California’s                │
│  unconscionability doctrine as applied to class action waivers in consumer   │
│  contracts.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Holdings:**                                                               │
│  1. **Yes**, the clause is procedurally unconscionable due to the            │
│  contract’s adhesion nature and the lack of transparency or assent.          │
│  2. **Yes**, the clause is substantively unconscionable because it           │
│  unreasonably favors the defendant and eviscerates statutory remedies under  │
│  consumer protection laws.                                                   │
│  3. **No**, the FAA does not preempt state law where the grounds for         │
│  invalidating the clause are generally applicable to contract law (e.g.,     │
│  unconscionability), and the clause operates to insulate the defendant from  │
│  accountability in a way that undermines public policy.                      │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Reasoning:**                                                              │
│  The appellate court applied the two-prong test for unconscionability under  │
│  California law (*Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services,        │
│  Inc.*).                                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  First, **procedural unconscionability** exists because the contract was a   │
│  classic contract of adhesion—offered on a non-negotiable,                   │
│  take-it-or-leave-it basis. The arbitration clause was buried in dense       │
│  legal language, and the consumer had no meaningful opportunity to review    │
│  or negotiate the terms. There was no conspicuous disclosure of the class    │
│  action waiver, and no affirmative assent was obtained.                      │
│                                                                              │
│  Second, **substantive unconscionability** is present because the clause     │
│  effectively nullifies the plaintiff’s ability to pursue statutory           │
│  remedies. Given the small individual damages typical in consumer disputes,  │
│  individual arbitration is economically irrational and functionally          │
│  impossible, thereby shielding the defendant from liability for systemic     │
│  wrongdoing. This imbalance shocks the conscience and undermines the         │
│  deterrent purpose of consumer protection statutes.                          │
│                                                                              │
│  Regarding **FAA preemption**, the court distinguished *AT&T Mobility v.     │
│  Concepcion* (2011), noting that while the FAA generally favors              │
│  arbitration, it does not preempt state contract defenses that are applied   │
│  evenhandedly. California’s doctrine of unconscionability is such a          │
│  defense. The court emphasized that *Concepcion* did not mandate             │
│  enforcement of clauses that strip consumers of remedies in a way that       │
│  frustrates the fundamental purpose of consumer protection laws. The clause  │
│  here does precisely that.                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Conclusion:**                                                             │
│  The arbitration clause, particularly the class action waiver, is            │
│  unenforceable as both procedurally and substantively unconscionable under   │
│  California law. The FAA does not preempt this application of state          │
│  contract law. The trial court’s order compelling arbitration and            │
│  dismissing the class action is reversed. The case is remanded for further   │
│  proceedings consistent with this opinion.                                   │

│  ### **1. *Southland Corp. v. Keating*, 465 U.S. 1 (1984)**                  │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Full Citation**: *Southland Corp. v. Keating*, 465 U.S. 1 (1984)        │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts**: Franchisees of 7-Eleven stores brought a class action in       │
│  California state court alleging violations of state franchise investment    │
│  laws. The franchise agreements contained arbitration clauses. Southland     │
│  moved to compel arbitration under the FAA. The California courts held that  │
│  the FAA did not apply to state court proceedings and that state law         │
│  governed.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Legal Issue**: Whether the Federal Arbitration Act applies in state     │
│  courts and preempts state laws that disfavor arbitration.                   │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Holding**: Yes. The FAA applies in both state and federal courts, and   │
│  Section 2 of the FAA is a congressional declaration of the enforceability   │
│  of arbitration agreements, which preempts contrary state laws.              │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Reasoning**: The Supreme Court, in an opinion by Chief Justice Burger,  │
│  held that Congress intended the FAA to be applied in both federal and       │
│  state courts. The Court interpreted Section 2 of the FAA—which declares     │
│  arbitration agreements “valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon      │
│  such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any         │
│  contract”—as creating a national policy favoring arbitration. State laws    │
│  that single out arbitration agreements for disfavored treatment are         │
│  preempted.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Impact on FAA and Preemption**: This case established the foundational  │
│  principle that the FAA has preemptive effect over state laws that           │
│  undermine the enforceability of arbitration agreements. It laid the         │
│  groundwork for later decisions enforcing class action waivers.              │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Application in the Ninth Circuit**: The Ninth Circuit has consistently  │
│  applied *Southland* to enforce arbitration agreements in consumer and       │
│  employment contexts, rejecting state-law defenses that are not generally    │
│  applicable to all contracts (e.g., *Marmet Health Care Center v. Brown*,    │
│  565 U.S. 530 (2012), reaffirming *Southland*).                              │

### **III. KEY FEDERAL PRECEDENTS AND IMPACT ON STATE UNCONSCIONABILITY DOCTRINES**

1. ***Southland Corp. v. Keating*, 465 U.S. 1 (1984)**  
   - **Holding**: FAA applies in state courts and preempts state laws hostile to arbitration.  
   - **Impact**: Established FAA as a national policy, limiting state interference.

2. ***AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion*, 563 U.S. 333 (2011)**  
   - **Holding**: FAA preempts state rules (e.g., *Discover Bank*) that invalidate class waivers.  
   - **Impact**: Class waivers in arbitration agreements are enforceable unless based on neutral contract defenses.

3. ***American Express Co. v. Italian Colors Restaurant*, 570 U.S. 228 (2013)**  
   - **Holding**: High cost of individual arbitration does not invalidate class waivers.  
   - **Impact**: “Effective vindication” exception is narrow; economic impracticability is not a bar.

4. ***Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis*, 138 S. Ct. 1612 (2018)**  
   - **Holding**: FAA overrides NLRA’s protection of collective action.  
   - **Impact**: Class waivers in employment and consumer contracts are enforceable.

---

### **IV. EVALUATION OF PRO-ENFORCEMENT AND ANTI-ENFORCEMENT ARGUMENTS**

**A. Pro-Enforcement (Federal Precedent)**  
- The FAA establishes a strong federal policy favoring arbitration.  
- Binding Supreme Court precedent (*Concepcion*, *Italian Colors*, *Epic Systems*) mandates enforcement of class waivers.  
- Delegation clauses are generally upheld.  
- Courts must enforce agreements according to their terms, even if inconvenient.

**B. Anti-Enforcement (State Unconscionability)**  
- State contract law permits invalidation of unconscionable agreements.  
- **Procedural unconscionability**: Adhesive contract, lack of notice, no negotiation.  
- **Substantive unconscionability**: High costs, lack of mutuality, biased forum.  
- **Public policy**: Enforcing such clauses enables systemic abuse and undermines consumer protection statutes.

### **V. RECOMMENDATION**

We recommend **challenging the arbitration ruling on appeal**, with the following strategic considerations:

1. **Forum**: File in California state appellate court, where unconscionability doctrine is robust and recent decisions (*Perez*, *Jones*) support invalidation of oppressive clauses.

2. **Cost**: Individual arbitration is economically unviable for small claims. Pursuing class action in court ensures meaningful enforcement of statutory rights.

3. **Timing**: An appeal may delay resolution, but success would allow class certification and systemic reform.

4. **Public Policy**: A favorable ruling would reinforce consumer rights and deter corporate abuse through forced arbitration.

5. **Settlement**: If appellate risk increases, consider settlement with injunctive relief (e.g., revised terms), restitution, and transparency commitments.

**Conclusion**: While federal precedent favors enforcement, California courts retain authority to invalidate arbitration clauses that are procedurally and substantively unconscionable. Given the adhesive nature of the contract, lack of transparency, and economic inaccessibility of arbitration, the clause in *Doe v. MegaCom* is likely unenforceable. We advise proceeding with appeal while maintaining settlement as a pragmatic alternative.
