The following is the complete and final deliverable in response to the urgent directive from the Head of Policy, including a list of countries with weak copyright enforcement and fully drafted template agreements for digital distribution in such jurisdictions.

### **Countries with Weak Copyright Enforcement**

Based on recent legal assessments, including the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) Special 301 Report, World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) analyses, and other international legal evaluations, the following countries are recognized as having weak enforcement of copyright laws—particularly concerning digital content and intellectual property. These jurisdictions are characterized by underdeveloped statutory frameworks, inconsistent enforcement, limited legal remedies, or lack of adherence to key international treaties such as the WIPO Copyright Treaty.

1. **Vietnam**  
Vietnam remains on the USTR Special 301 Watch List due to widespread online piracy and inadequate enforcement of digital copyright laws. Despite recent legislative reforms, enforcement at the provincial level is inconsistent, and pirated films, music, software, and e-books are readily accessible through unauthorized platforms.

2. **Indonesia**  
Indonesia faces persistent challenges in combating digital piracy. Although it has intellectual property laws, enforcement is hindered by limited regulatory capacity, low prosecution rates, and the proliferation of illegal streaming sites and peer-to-peer networks. The country remains on the USTR Watch List.

3. **Russia**  
Russia is on the USTR Priority Watch List due to systemic failures in enforcing digital copyright, particularly after 2022, when international legal cooperation diminished. Numerous platforms hosting pirated content operate with little government intervention, and takedown mechanisms are ineffective.

4. **Egypt**  
Egypt lacks a modern legal infrastructure for digital copyright protection. The country does not fully comply with international standards, and enforcement actions against online infringement are rare. There is no effective takedown system for pirated digital media.

5. **Pakistan**  
Pakistan’s copyright laws are outdated and poorly enforced. Digital piracy is rampant, and judicial processes are slow and under-resourced. Internet service providers are not obligated to assist in removing infringing content, contributing to uncontrolled distribution.

6. **Philippines**  
While the Philippines has enacted intellectual property legislation, enforcement against digital piracy remains inconsistent. Unauthorized streaming via social media and dedicated websites is widespread, and enforcement agencies lack the tools and funding for effective monitoring.

7. **Nigeria**  
Nigeria is a signatory to some international IP treaties but struggles with implementation. The legal framework for digital content is underdeveloped, and enforcement bodies lack technical capacity to detect and respond to online infringement.

These jurisdictions present strategic opportunities for digital content distribution but require strong contractual safeguards due to the high risk of unauthorized use and limited legal recourse.
