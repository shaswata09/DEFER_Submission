  $$
  \text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}
  $$
  It has magnitude only and does not depend on direction.

- **Velocity**: A *vector* quantity defined as the rate of change of displacement (change in position).  
  $$
  \text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}
  $$
  It includes both magnitude and direction.

---

#### **2. Key Differences**

| Aspect               | Speed                              | Velocity                            |
|----------------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Nature**           | Scalar (magnitude only)            | Vector (magnitude + direction)      |
| **Path Dependence**  | Depends on total distance traveled | Depends on net displacement         |
| **Direction**        | Ignored                            | Essential                           |

- *Displacement* = 0 m → Velocity = 0 / 1200 = **0 m/s**  
👉 *This reveals a critical misconception: motion does not guarantee non-zero velocity.*

**Example 3: Straight-Line Motion**
