FINAL OUTPUT:
Here is the complete report on recent jurisdiction-specific changes to corporate tax laws:

**1. United States (Federal Level)**  
- **Legislative Update**: The Inflation Reduction Act continues to influence corporate tax enforcement, with new IRS guidance issued in December 2023 clarifying the application of the 15% corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) for corporations with average annual financial statement income exceeding $1 billion.  
- **Regulatory Changes**: The Treasury Department released final regulations in March 2024 on the base erosion and anti-abuse tax (BEAT), modifying the calculation methodology for certain payments.  
- **OECD Pillar Two Implications**: The U.S. has not yet adopted the global minimum tax but is engaging in bilateral discussions. Proposed regulations are expected in late 2024 to address GloBE (Global Anti-Base Erosion) rules for U.S. multinational enterprises.  
- **Effective Dates**: The CAMT provisions apply to tax years beginning after December 31, 2023.  
- **Source**: IRS Notice 2024-11, Treasury Regulation §1.59A-1 (Final Rules).

**2. New York State**  
- **Legislative Update**: The 2024 state budget included amendments to the corporate franchise tax, particularly modifying the water’s edge election rules for combined reporting.  
- **Tax Rate Adjustment**: No change to the top corporate tax rate (6.5%), but a new surcharge of 10% applies to corporations with New York receipts exceeding $5 million (effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024).  
- **Compliance Requirements**: Enhanced reporting for entities claiming the research and development tax credit, requiring detailed documentation to be retained and submitted upon audit.  
- **OECD Alignment**: New York has not independently implemented Pillar Two but will likely conform to federal treatment if U.S. adopts GloBE rules.  
- **Source**: New York State Senate Bill S7514A (2024), NYS Department of Taxation and Finance Guidance #TG-2024(1).

**3. United Kingdom**  
- **Legislative Update**: Finance Act 2023 introduced a new "Pillar Two Top-Up Tax" effective from April 2024, aligning with OECD’s global minimum tax. Companies with global consolidated revenue of €750 million or more are subject to a 15% minimum top-up rate.  
- **Corporate Tax Rate**: The main rate increased from 19% to 25% for companies with profits over £250,000, effective April 1, 2023. Marginal relief applies for profits between £50,000 and £250,000.  
- **Digital Services Tax Replaced**: The DST was repealed in December 2023 in favor of the OECD-aligned regime.  
- **Source**: HMRC Guidance on the Income Inclusion Rule (IIR), Finance Act 2023, Section 12.

**4. Germany**  
- **Legislative Update**: The German Parliament passed the "Corporate Tax Modernization Act 2023" in November 2023, effective January 1, 2024.  
- **Minimum Tax Implementation**: Germany implemented the OECD Pillar Two rules via the GloBE Act, introducing a 15% minimum top-up tax for multinational groups with revenue above €750 million.  
- **Loss Carryforward**: Restrictions were tightened; corporate losses can now only be offset against 60% of future taxable income (down from 100%).  
- **Compliance**: New digital reporting requirements under the "Digital Tax Compliance System" mandate quarterly electronic submissions for large corporations.  
- **Source**: Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF), Schreiben vom 15.12.2023, IV C 4 – S 0315/23/10001.

**5. Japan**  
- **Legislative Update**: The 2023 Tax Reform Bill, effective April 1, 2024, introduced the Income Inclusion Rule (IIR) for multinational enterprises under OECD Pillar Two.  
- **Tax Rate**: The effective corporate tax rate remains around 30%, but a 15% global minimum tax applies to Japanese parent companies of multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding €750 million.  
- **Substance-Based Income Exclusion**: Japan allows a 5% tangible asset and 10% payroll cost exclusion under the GloBE rules.  
- **Compliance**: First reports under the new regime due by December 31, 2025, for fiscal years beginning on or after April 1, 2024.  
- **Source**: National Tax Agency of Japan, "Guidelines on the Application of the Global Minimum Tax," March 2024.
