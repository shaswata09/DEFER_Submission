│  ### **II. STATUTORY FRAMEWORK**                                             │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **A. Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX), 18 U.S.C. § 1514A**                     │
│  Enacted in 2002, SOX protects employees of publicly traded companies (and   │
│  their contractors/subcontractors) from retaliation for reporting conduct    │
│  they reasonably believe constitutes fraud or violations of SEC rules.       │
│  Protected activity includes internal disclosures to supervisors or          │
│  compliance personnel.                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **B. Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, 15     │
│  U.S.C. § 78u-6**                                                            │
│  Dodd-Frank established a robust whistleblower bounty and protection         │
│  program administered by the SEC. To qualify as a "whistleblower" under      │
│  Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provisions, the individual must report        │
│  possible securities violations *to the SEC*. However, the Supreme Court     │
│  has clarified that internal reporting may still be protected under other    │
│  statutes.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **C. False Claims Act (FCA), 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733**                  │
│  The FCA allows private individuals (relators) to sue on behalf of the       │
│  government for fraud involving federal funds. It includes anti-retaliation  │
│  protections for employees who engage in lawful acts in furtherance of an    │
│  FCA action or who object to practices they believe constitute fraud         │
│  against the government.                                                     │
│                                                                              │

│  ### **III. CASE SUMMARIES**                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **1. Lawson v. FMR LLC, 571 U.S. 429 (2014)**                          │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Two employees of FMR LLC, a mutual fund advisor not itself     │
│  publicly traded, alleged retaliation after reporting accounting             │
│  irregularities. They were not employees of the publicly traded funds FMR    │
│  advised.                                                                    │
│  - **Issue:** Whether SOX’s whistleblower protections extend to employees    │
│  of private contractors of public companies.                                 │
│  - **Ruling:** The Supreme Court held **yes**. SOX’s language protects       │
│  employees of contractors and subcontractors of public companies. The        │
│  protection is not limited to direct employees of public issuers.            │
│  - **Significance:** Broadened the scope of SOX protections, increasing      │
│  liability exposure for third-party service providers to public companies.   │
│  Employers must ensure whistleblower policies cover contractor personnel.    │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **2. Digital Realty Trust, Inc. v. Somers, 138 S. Ct. 767 (2018)**     │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Paul Somers, a former executive at Digital Realty, was fired   │
│  after raising internal concerns about accounting irregularities. He never   │
│  reported the issues to the SEC and later sued under Dodd-Frank’s            │
│  anti-retaliation provision.                                                 │
│  - **Issue:** Whether Dodd-Frank protects individuals who report internally  │
│  but not to the SEC.                                                         │
│  - **Ruling:** The Supreme Court held **no**. To be a “whistleblower”        │
│  entitled to Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation protections, an individual must   │
│  report information *to the SEC*. Internal reporting alone is insufficient.  │
│  - **Significance:** Clarified a critical limitation in Dodd-Frank. While    │
│  internal reporting is protected under SOX or other laws, Dodd-Frank’s       │
│  anti-retaliation remedies are available only to those who "report to the    │
│  Commission."                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **3. Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC, 896 F.3d 168 (2d Cir. 2018)**      │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Murray, a compliance officer, raised concerns internally       │
│  about improper revenue recognition and was later demoted. He filed a SOX    │
│  retaliation claim with OSHA.                                                │
│  - **Issue:** Whether a compliance officer’s internal reporting qualifies    │
│  as protected activity under SOX.                                            │
│  - **Ruling:** The Second Circuit affirmed that internal reporting by        │
│  compliance staff constitutes protected activity under SOX if the employee   │
│  reasonably believes the conduct violates securities laws.                   │
│  - **Significance:** Reinforced that SOX protects good-faith internal        │
│  reporting, even by employees whose job duties include compliance.           │
│  Employers cannot penalize compliance staff for fulfilling their oversight   │
│  responsibilities.                                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **4. United States ex rel. Campie v. Gilead Sciences, Inc., 862 F.3d   │
│  890 (9th Cir. 2017)**                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** The Campies, former employees, alleged they were fired for     │
│  refusing to participate in off-label marketing and for raising concerns     │
│  about Medicare fraud. They filed an FCA retaliation claim.                  │
│  - **Issue:** Whether the FCA’s anti-retaliation provision protects          │
│  employees who are terminated for refusing to engage in fraudulent conduct,  │
│  even absent a formal FCA lawsuit.                                           │
│  - **Ruling:** The Ninth Circuit held that the FCA protects employees who    │
│  are discharged for *lawful acts done in furtherance of an FCA action*,      │
│  including internal opposition to fraud.                                     │
│  - **Significance:** Expanded FCA retaliation protections to include         │
│  pre-litigation conduct. Employers cannot retaliate against employees for    │
│  lawful internal opposition to government fraud.                             │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **5. Berman v. Neo@Ogilvy LLC, 801 F.3d 145 (2d Cir. 2015)**           │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Berman, a creative director, claimed he was fired after        │
│  complaining about the company’s failure to pay overtime and misclassifying  │
│  employees. He reported these issues internally and to the CEO.              │
│  - **Issue:** Whether Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provision applies to     │
│  internal whistleblowers.                                                    │
│  - **Ruling:** The Second Circuit initially ruled that Dodd-Frank protected  │
│  internal reporting. However, this decision was **overruled** by the         │
│  Supreme Court in *Digital Realty*.                                          │
│  - **Significance:** Although *Berman* was superseded, it highlighted the    │
│  circuit split that led to *Digital Realty*. It remains relevant for         │
│  illustrating the evolution of Dodd-Frank interpretation.                    │

│                                                                              │

│                                                                              │

│  exceeding $100 million.                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  Notably, the SEC has reaffirmed that individuals who report internally      │
│  first are not disqualified from Dodd-Frank awards, provided they later      │
│  report to the SEC.                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **C. Judicial Trends**                                                 │
│  Courts are increasingly scrutinizing employer policies that contain overly  │
│  broad confidentiality or non-disparagement clauses that may chill           │
│  protected reporting. The NLRB and federal courts have invalidated           │
│  provisions that could reasonably deter employees from reporting violations  │
│  to regulators.                                                              │

│                                                                              │
│  1. **Whistleblower Policies Must Be Multi-Statute Compliant:** Policies     │

│  applicable laws, even if such reporting does not trigger Dodd-Frank         │
│  protections.                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Training for Management and HR:** Supervisors must be trained to       │

│  subtle forms of retaliation (e.g., exclusion, negative performance          │
│  reviews).                                                                   │

│                                                                              │
│  4. **Contractual Review:** Employment agreements, severance packages, and   │
│  NDAs should be reviewed to ensure they do not contain provisions that       │
│  could be construed as impeding regulatory reporting.                        │
│                                                                              │
│  5. **Compliance Culture:** Encourage a culture where employees feel safe    │
│  reporting concerns internally, knowing they will be protected under SOX     │
│  and other statutes.                                                         │

│  encompasses federal rulings interpreting the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform  │
│  and Consumer Protection Act (Dodd-Frank), the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX),     │
│  and the False Claims Act (FCA), with particular attention to the scope of   │
│  protected activity, standing requirements, and remedies available to        │
│  whistleblowers. Recent administrative guidance and enforcement trends are   │
│  also summarized to inform corporate compliance and litigation risk          │
│  assessment.                                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  Key cases analyzed include *Lawson v. FMR LLC*, *Digital Realty Trust,      │
│  Inc. v. Somers*, *Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC*, *United States ex rel.    │
│  Campie v. Gilead Sciences, Inc.*, and *Berman v. Neo@Ogilvy LLC*. These     │
│  decisions collectively define the boundaries of whistleblower protections,  │
│  especially regarding internal versus external reporting, the definition of  │
│  "whistleblower" under Dodd-Frank, and the availability of anti-retaliation  │
│  remedies.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **II. STATUTORY FRAMEWORK**                                             │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **A. Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX), 18 U.S.C. § 1514A**                     │
│  Enacted in 2002, SOX protects employees of publicly traded companies (and   │
│  their contractors/subcontractors) from retaliation for reporting conduct    │
│  they reasonably believe constitutes fraud or violations of SEC rules.       │
│  Protected activity includes internal disclosures to supervisors or          │
│  compliance personnel.                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **B. Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, 15     │
│  U.S.C. § 78u-6**                                                            │
│  Dodd-Frank established a robust whistleblower bounty and protection         │
│  program administered by the SEC. To qualify as a "whistleblower" under      │
│  Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provisions, the individual must report        │
│  possible securities violations *to the SEC*. However, the Supreme Court     │
│  has clarified that internal reporting may still be protected under other    │
│  statutes.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **C. False Claims Act (FCA), 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733**                  │
│  The FCA allows private individuals (relators) to sue on behalf of the       │
│  government for fraud involving federal funds. It includes anti-retaliation  │
│  protections for employees who engage in lawful acts in furtherance of an    │
│  FCA action or who object to practices they believe constitute fraud         │
│  against the government.                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **III. CASE SUMMARIES**                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **1. Lawson v. FMR LLC, 571 U.S. 429 (2014)**                          │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Two employees of FMR LLC, a mutual fund advisor not itself     │
│  publicly traded, alleged retaliation after reporting accounting             │
│  irregularities. They were not employees of the publicly traded funds FMR    │
│  advised.                                                                    │
│  - **Issue:** Whether SOX’s whistleblower protections extend to employees    │
│  of private contractors of public companies.                                 │
│  - **Ruling:** The Supreme Court held **yes**. SOX’s language protects       │
│  employees of contractors and subcontractors of public companies. The        │
│  protection is not limited to direct employees of public issuers.            │
│  - **Significance:** Broadened the scope of SOX protections, increasing      │
│  liability exposure for third-party service providers to public companies.   │
│  Employers must ensure whistleblower policies cover contractor personnel.    │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **2. Digital Realty Trust, Inc. v. Somers, 138 S. Ct. 767 (2018)**     │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Paul Somers, a former executive at Digital Realty, was fired   │
│  after raising internal concerns about accounting irregularities. He never   │
│  reported the issues to the SEC and later sued under Dodd-Frank’s            │
│  anti-retaliation provision.                                                 │
│  - **Issue:** Whether Dodd-Frank protects individuals who report internally  │
│  but not to the SEC.                                                         │
│  - **Ruling:** The Supreme Court held **no**. To be a “whistleblower”        │
│  entitled to Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation protections, an individual must   │
│  report information *to the SEC*. Internal reporting alone is insufficient.  │
│  - **Significance:** Clarified a critical limitation in Dodd-Frank. While    │
│  internal reporting is protected under SOX or other laws, Dodd-Frank’s       │
│  anti-retaliation remedies are available only to those who "report to the    │
│  Commission."                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **3. Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC, 896 F.3d 168 (2d Cir. 2018)**      │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Murray, a compliance officer, raised concerns internally       │
│  about improper revenue recognition and was later demoted. He filed a SOX    │
│  retaliation claim with OSHA.                                                │
│  - **Issue:** Whether a compliance officer’s internal reporting qualifies    │
│  as protected activity under SOX.                                            │
│  - **Ruling:** The Second Circuit affirmed that internal reporting by        │
│  compliance staff constitutes protected activity under SOX if the employee   │
│  reasonably believes the conduct violates securities laws.                   │
│  - **Significance:** Reinforced that SOX protects good-faith internal        │
│  reporting, even by employees whose job duties include compliance.           │
│  Employers cannot penalize compliance staff for fulfilling their oversight   │
│  responsibilities.                                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **4. United States ex rel. Campie v. Gilead Sciences, Inc., 862 F.3d   │
│  890 (9th Cir. 2017)**                                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** The Campies, former employees, alleged they were fired for     │
│  refusing to participate in off-label marketing and for raising concerns     │
│  about Medicare fraud. They filed an FCA retaliation claim.                  │
│  - **Issue:** Whether the FCA’s anti-retaliation provision protects          │
│  employees who are terminated for refusing to engage in fraudulent conduct,  │
│  even absent a formal FCA lawsuit.                                           │
│  - **Ruling:** The Ninth Circuit held that the FCA protects employees who    │
│  are discharged for *lawful acts done in furtherance of an FCA action*,      │
│  including internal opposition to fraud.                                     │
│  - **Significance:** Expanded FCA retaliation protections to include         │
│  pre-litigation conduct. Employers cannot retaliate against employees for    │
│  lawful internal opposition to government fraud.                             │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **5. Berman v. Neo@Ogilvy LLC, 801 F.3d 145 (2d Cir. 2015)**           │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Facts:** Berman, a creative director, claimed he was fired after        │
│  complaining about the company’s failure to pay overtime and misclassifying  │
│  employees. He reported these issues internally and to the CEO.              │
│  - **Issue:** Whether Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provision applies to     │
│  internal whistleblowers.                                                    │
│  - **Ruling:** The Second Circuit initially ruled that Dodd-Frank protected  │
│  internal reporting. However, this decision was **overruled** by the         │
│  Supreme Court in *Digital Realty*.                                          │
│  - **Significance:** Although *Berman* was superseded, it highlighted the    │
│  circuit split that led to *Digital Realty*. It remains relevant for         │
│  illustrating the evolution of Dodd-Frank interpretation.                    │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **IV. RECENT LEGAL DEVELOPMENTS**                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **A. Department of Labor (DOL)/OSHA Guidance (2023–2024)**             │
│  The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), which             │
│  administers SOX whistleblower claims, has issued updated guidelines         │
│  emphasizing:                                                                │
│  - Prompt investigation of internal complaints.                              │
│  - Preservation of records related to whistleblower disclosures.             │
│  - Prohibition of adverse actions following protected activity, even if the  │
│  complaint is later found unsubstantiated, provided it was made in good      │
│  faith.                                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  OSHA has also shortened average adjudication times for SOX claims,          │
│  increasing pressure on employers to respond swiftly.                        │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **B. SEC Whistleblower Program Trends**                                │
│  The SEC Office of the Whistleblower reported a record number of tips in FY  │
│  2024 (over 12,000), with a significant increase in cybersecurity and        │
│  ESG-related disclosures. The SEC awarded over $600 million in               │
│  whistleblower awards since the program’s inception, with several awards     │
│  exceeding $100 million.                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  Notably, the SEC has reaffirmed that individuals who report internally      │
│  first are not disqualified from Dodd-Frank awards, provided they later      │
│  report to the SEC.                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **C. Judicial Trends**                                                 │
│  Courts are increasingly scrutinizing employer policies that contain overly  │
│  broad confidentiality or non-disparagement clauses that may chill           │
│  protected reporting. The NLRB and federal courts have invalidated           │
│  provisions that could reasonably deter employees from reporting violations  │
│  to regulators.                                                              │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **V. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR EMPLOYERS**                             │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Whistleblower Policies Must Be Multi-Statute Compliant:** Policies     │
│  should explicitly protect internal reporting under SOX, FCA, and other      │
│  applicable laws, even if such reporting does not trigger Dodd-Frank         │
│  protections.                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Training for Management and HR:** Supervisors must be trained to       │
│  recognize protected activity and avoid retaliatory actions, including       │
│  subtle forms of retaliation (e.g., exclusion, negative performance          │
│  reviews).                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Documentation of Personnel Actions:** All disciplinary or termination  │
│  decisions involving employees who have raised concerns must be thoroughly   │
│  documented to defend against retaliation claims.                            │
│                                                                              │
│  4. **Contractual Review:** Employment agreements, severance packages, and   │
│  NDAs should be reviewed to ensure they do not contain provisions that       │
│  could be construed as impeding regulatory reporting.                        │
│                                                                              │
│  5. **Compliance Culture:** Encourage a culture where employees feel safe    │
│  reporting concerns internally, knowing they will be protected under SOX     │
│  and other statutes.                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ---                                                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  ### **VI. CONCLUSION**                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  The evolving landscape of whistleblower litigation underscores the          │
│  importance of robust compliance programs and careful adherence to           │
│  statutory protections. While *Digital Realty* narrowed Dodd-Frank’s         │
│  anti-retaliation scope, SOX and the FCA continue to provide broad           │
│  protections for internal reporting. Employers must navigate these           │
│  overlapping regimes with diligence, ensuring that no adverse action is      │
│  taken against employees who raise concerns in good faith. Proactive policy  │
│  development, training, and responsiveness to internal complaints are        │
│  essential to mitigate legal and reputational risk.                          │

### **I. OVERVIEW**

This memorandum provides a comprehensive analysis of landmark decisions and statutory developments in whistleblower protection law. The scope encompasses federal rulings interpreting the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (Dodd-Frank), the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX), and the False Claims Act (FCA), with particular attention to the scope of protected activity, standing requirements, and remedies available to whistleblowers. Recent administrative guidance and enforcement trends are also summarized to inform corporate compliance and litigation risk assessment.

Key cases analyzed include *Lawson v. FMR LLC*, *Digital Realty Trust, Inc. v. Somers*, *Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC*, *United States ex rel. Campie v. Gilead Sciences, Inc.*, and *Berman v. Neo@Ogilvy LLC*. These decisions collectively define the boundaries of whistleblower protections, especially regarding internal versus external reporting, the definition of "whistleblower" under Dodd-Frank, and the availability of anti-retaliation remedies.

---

### **II. STATUTORY FRAMEWORK**

#### **A. Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX), 18 U.S.C. § 1514A**
Enacted in 2002, SOX protects employees of publicly traded companies (and their contractors/subcontractors) from retaliation for reporting conduct they reasonably believe constitutes fraud or violations of SEC rules. Protected activity includes internal disclosures to supervisors or compliance personnel.

#### **B. Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, 15 U.S.C. § 78u-6**
Dodd-Frank established a robust whistleblower bounty and protection program administered by the SEC. To qualify as a "whistleblower" under Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provisions, the individual must report possible securities violations *to the SEC*. However, the Supreme Court has clarified that internal reporting may still be protected under other statutes.

#### **C. False Claims Act (FCA), 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733**
The FCA allows private individuals (relators) to sue on behalf of the government for fraud involving federal funds. It includes anti-retaliation protections for employees who engage in lawful acts in furtherance of an FCA action or who object to practices they believe constitute fraud against the government.

---

### **III. CASE SUMMARIES**

#### **1. Lawson v. FMR LLC, 571 U.S. 429 (2014)**

- **Facts:** Two employees of FMR LLC, a mutual fund advisor not itself publicly traded, alleged retaliation after reporting accounting irregularities. They were not employees of the publicly traded funds FMR advised.
- **Issue:** Whether SOX’s whistleblower protections extend to employees of private contractors of public companies.
- **Ruling:** The Supreme Court held **yes**. SOX’s language protects employees of contractors and subcontractors of public companies. The protection is not limited to direct employees of public issuers.
- **Significance:** Broadened the scope of SOX protections, increasing liability exposure for third-party service providers to public companies. Employers must ensure whistleblower policies cover contractor personnel.

#### **2. Digital Realty Trust, Inc. v. Somers, 138 S. Ct. 767 (2018)**

- **Facts:** Paul Somers, a former executive at Digital Realty, was fired after raising internal concerns about accounting irregularities. He never reported the issues to the SEC and later sued under Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provision.
- **Issue:** Whether Dodd-Frank protects individuals who report internally but not to the SEC.
- **Ruling:** The Supreme Court held **no**. To be a “whistleblower” entitled to Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation protections, an individual must report information *to the SEC*. Internal reporting alone is insufficient.
- **Significance:** Clarified a critical limitation in Dodd-Frank. While internal reporting is protected under SOX or other laws, Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation remedies are available only to those who "report to the Commission."

#### **3. Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC, 896 F.3d 168 (2d Cir. 2018)**

- **Facts:** Murray, a compliance officer, raised concerns internally about improper revenue recognition and was later demoted. He filed a SOX retaliation claim with OSHA.
- **Issue:** Whether a compliance officer’s internal reporting qualifies as protected activity under SOX.
- **Ruling:** The Second Circuit affirmed that internal reporting by compliance staff constitutes protected activity under SOX if the employee reasonably believes the conduct violates securities laws.
- **Significance:** Reinforced that SOX protects good-faith internal reporting, even by employees whose job duties include compliance. Employers cannot penalize compliance staff for fulfilling their oversight responsibilities.

#### **4. United States ex rel. Campie v. Gilead Sciences, Inc., 862 F.3d 890 (9th Cir. 2017)**

- **Facts:** The Campies, former employees, alleged they were fired for refusing to participate in off-label marketing and for raising concerns about Medicare fraud. They filed an FCA retaliation claim.
- **Issue:** Whether the FCA’s anti-retaliation provision protects employees who are terminated for refusing to engage in fraudulent conduct, even absent a formal FCA lawsuit.
- **Ruling:** The Ninth Circuit held that the FCA protects employees who are discharged for *lawful acts done in furtherance of an FCA action*, including internal opposition to fraud.
- **Significance:** Expanded FCA retaliation protections to include pre-litigation conduct. Employers cannot retaliate against employees for lawful internal opposition to government fraud.

#### **5. Berman v. Neo@Ogilvy LLC, 801 F.3d 145 (2d Cir. 2015)**

- **Facts:** Berman, a creative director, claimed he was fired after complaining about the company’s failure to pay overtime and misclassifying employees. He reported these issues internally and to the CEO.
- **Issue:** Whether Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation provision applies to internal whistleblowers.
- **Ruling:** The Second Circuit initially ruled that Dodd-Frank protected internal reporting. However, this decision was **overruled** by the Supreme Court in *Digital Realty*.
- **Significance:** Although *Berman* was superseded, it highlighted the circuit split that led to *Digital Realty*. It remains relevant for illustrating the evolution of Dodd-Frank interpretation.

### **IV. RECENT LEGAL DEVELOPMENTS**

#### **A. Department of Labor (DOL)/OSHA Guidance (2023–2024)**
The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), which administers SOX whistleblower claims, has issued updated guidelines emphasizing:
- Prompt investigation of internal complaints.
- Preservation of records related to whistleblower disclosures.
- Prohibition of adverse actions following protected activity, even if the complaint is later found unsubstantiated, provided it was made in good faith.

OSHA has also shortened average adjudication times for SOX claims, increasing pressure on employers to respond swiftly.

#### **B. SEC Whistleblower Program Trends**
The SEC Office of the Whistleblower reported a record number of tips in FY 2024 (over 12,000), with a significant increase in cybersecurity and ESG-related disclosures. The SEC awarded over $600 million in whistleblower awards since the program’s inception, with several awards exceeding $100 million.

Notably, the SEC has reaffirmed that individuals who report internally first are not disqualified from Dodd-Frank awards, provided they later report to the SEC.

#### **C. Judicial Trends**
Courts are increasingly scrutinizing employer policies that contain overly broad confidentiality or non-disparagement clauses that may chill protected reporting. The NLRB and federal courts have invalidated provisions that could reasonably deter employees from reporting violations to regulators.

---

### **V. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR EMPLOYERS**

1. **Whistleblower Policies Must Be Multi-Statute Compliant:** Policies should explicitly protect internal reporting under SOX, FCA, and other applicable laws, even if such reporting does not trigger Dodd-Frank protections.

2. **Training for Management and HR:** Supervisors must be trained to recognize protected activity and avoid retaliatory actions, including subtle forms of retaliation (e.g., exclusion, negative performance reviews).

3. **Documentation of Personnel Actions:** All disciplinary or termination decisions involving employees who have raised concerns must be thoroughly documented to defend against retaliation claims.

4. **Contractual Review:** Employment agreements, severance packages, and NDAs should be reviewed to ensure they do not contain provisions that could be construed as impeding regulatory reporting.

5. **Compliance Culture:** Encourage a culture where employees feel safe reporting concerns internally, knowing they will be protected under SOX and other statutes.

---

### **VI. CONCLUSION**

The evolving landscape of whistleblower litigation underscores the importance of robust compliance programs and careful adherence to statutory protections. While *Digital Realty* narrowed Dodd-Frank’s anti-retaliation scope, SOX and the FCA continue to provide broad protections for internal reporting. Employers must navigate these overlapping regimes with diligence, ensuring that no adverse action is taken against employees who raise concerns in good faith. Proactive policy development, training, and responsiveness to internal complaints are essential to mitigate legal and reputational risk.
