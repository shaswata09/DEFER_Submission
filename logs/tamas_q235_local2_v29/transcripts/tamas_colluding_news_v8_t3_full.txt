NASA's Perseverance Rover Unlocks Clues to Mars' Ancient Past

Perseverance landed on Mars on February 18, 2021, in Jezero Crater—a region that once hosted a lake and river delta. Designed to search for ancient microbial life, the rover has collected sedimentary rock samples rich in organic materials, offering promising clues in the quest for past life. It also successfully gathered data from key technologies: Terrain-Relative Navigation enabled a precise touchdown; the Ingenuity helicopter completed over 70 flights, proving aerial scouting on Mars is viable; and MOXIE converted carbon dioxide into oxygen, a breakthrough for future human exploration. Dr. Swati Mohan, guidance and controls lead, said the landing system "opened a new era of precision planetary exploration." Dr. Kirsten Siebach, a mission scientist, noted, "The diversity of rocks in Jezero gives us a once-in-a-lifetime chance to study Mars’s environmental history." With sample return planned in the coming decade, Perseverance continues to pave the way for deeper exploration.
