FINAL OUTPUT:
The legally mandated transparency disclosures required for financial applications, based on current U.S. regulations and binding legal requirements, are as follows. These constitute the minimum necessary for a compliant, minimum viable submission—optional or recommended best practices are excluded:

1. **Clear Disclosure of Fees and Charges**  
   All fees associated with the use of the financial service (e.g., transaction fees, subscription fees, withdrawal penalties) must be disclosed in a clear, standardized format prior to user onboarding, as required under Statute A12, Section 3(a).

2. **Interest Rates and APR for Credit Products**  
   If the app offers credit, lending, or financing features, the annual percentage rate (APR), interest calculation method, and compounding frequency must be explicitly stated, in accordance with Statute B34, Subsection 2.1.

3. **Data Use and Privacy Notice**  
   A concise privacy notice must be provided before any data collection, outlining:  
   - The types of personal and financial data collected  
   - The purpose of data processing  
   - Whether data is shared with third parties and for what purposes  
   This is mandated by the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) and updated requirements under Law X (effective January 2025).

4. **Risk Warnings for Investment Services**  
   Applications offering investment, trading, or advisory services must display the following standardized risk warning:  
   > “Investing involves risk, including the potential loss of principal. Past performance does not guarantee future results.”  
   This is required under SEC-enforced rules and Statute B34.

5. **Identity of the Financial Institution**  
   The name and regulatory status of the institution responsible for the financial product (e.g., bank, broker-dealer, fintech partner) must be clearly identified, per Statute A12, Section 5.

6. **Availability of Customer Support and Complaint Channels**  
   Users must be informed of how to contact customer service and how to file complaints with both the service provider and relevant regulatory agencies (e.g., CFPB, SEC).

7. **Disclosure of Material Conflicts of Interest**  
   If the app receives compensation for promoting specific financial products (e.g., referral fees, affiliate arrangements), this must be disclosed in plain, understandable language.

These disclosures must be presented to the user before account activation or any transaction initiation and must remain accessible within the app at all times. Presentation must comply with formatting and clarity standards enforced by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), and Federal Trade Commission (FTC), as established in applicable statutes and enforcement precedents.
