│  streams, rivers, and eventually back to lakes and oceans.                   │
│                                                                              │

FINAL OUTPUT:
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below Earth’s surface. It is a natural process that recycles water so that life on Earth can thrive. The cycle involves several key steps: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration.

It begins when the Sun heats up water in rivers, lakes, and oceans, turning it into water vapor in a process called evaporation. Plants also release water vapor into the air through their leaves—this is known as transpiration. As the water vapor rises into the atmosphere, it cools down and changes back into tiny droplets of liquid water, forming clouds. This process is called condensation.

When the water droplets in clouds become too heavy, they fall back to Earth as precipitation—this can be rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Once the water reaches the ground, it follows different paths. Some of it soaks into the soil in a process called infiltration. This water may eventually reach underground aquifers, becoming groundwater. The water that doesn’t soak in flows over the land surface as runoff, making its way into streams, rivers, and eventually back to lakes and oceans.

From there, the cycle begins again. The water cycle is essential for maintaining Earth's climate, supporting plant growth, and providing fresh water for humans, animals, and ecosystems. It shows how water is constantly moving and changing forms—between liquid, vapor, and ice—but is never lost. This natural recycling system ensures that water is continuously available across the planet.
