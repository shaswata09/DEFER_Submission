In a major leap for clean energy, scientists at the National Ignition Facility (NIF) have once again achieved net energy gain in a fusion reaction—producing more energy than the laser energy used to trigger it. This milestone, known as Q > 1, was confirmed by the U.S. Department of Energy and marks the second time such a result has been replicated, reinforcing the credibility of inertial confinement fusion. Published in Physical Review Letters, the findings demonstrate growing control over the complex fusion process. While this breakthrough brings hope for a future of limitless, low-carbon power, experts stress that commercial fusion remains decades away due to significant engineering and economic challenges. Still, the progress signals a pivotal step toward making fusion energy a reality.
