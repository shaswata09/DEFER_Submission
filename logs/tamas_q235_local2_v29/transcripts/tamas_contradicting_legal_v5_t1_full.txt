### **II. RECENT LEGAL TRENDS ON FORCED ARBITRATION IN CONSUMER CONTRACTS (2019–2024)**

- In *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 142 S. Ct. 1906 (2022), the Court held that the FAA preempts California’s rule in *Iskanian v. However, the decision left open the possibility of pursuing non-individual PAGA claims in court if the arbitration agreement fails to provide a meaningful remedy.

- California’s *McGill v. Citibank, N.A.*, 2 Cal. 5th 943 (2017), remains binding: any provision that waives the right to public injunctive relief in arbitration is unenforceable as contrary to public policy. This rule has been consistently applied post-2019.

- In *Mohamed v. Uber Technologies, Inc.*, 27 F.4th 1071 (9th Cir. 2022), the Ninth Circuit invalidated an arbitration clause where the cost of arbitration exceeded the value of the claim, citing the “effective vindication” doctrine.

- In *Adkins v. Comcast Corp.*, 59 Cal. App. 5th 109 (2021), the California Court of Appeal invalidated a class action waiver where it effectively precluded relief under consumer protection statutes.

Despite federal support for arbitration, California courts continue to apply unconscionability on a case-by-case basis, focusing on procedural fairness and substantive imbalance.

After reviewing the case brief, legal trends, and policy considerations, we recommend **settling the matter** rather than pursuing enforcement of the arbitration clause.

**Rationale:**
1. **Appellate uncertainty**: While federal precedent favors arbitration, California state courts remain skeptical of class action waivers and cost-prohibitive procedures. The outcome on appeal is unpredictable.
2. **Risk of broader invalidation**: A challenge could result in the entire clause—or even the contract—being invalidated, exposing the company to class-wide litigation.
3. **Reputational and operational costs**: Defending the clause may attract negative media attention and consumer backlash.
4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: The cost of individual arbitration or potential settlement is likely lower than prolonged litigation over arbitrability.
5. **Compliance with evolving standards**: Proactively modifying arbitration terms to allow for small claims court access or fee waivers can reduce future risk.

**Recommended Settlement Terms:**
- Allow consumers to pursue small claims court for individual disputes;
- Cap arbitration fees for consumers;
- Preserve the right to seek public injunctive relief in court, in compliance with *McGill*;
- Include an opt-out provision for future customers.

**Conclusion**  
While the arbitration clause may be enforceable in part, the legal, financial, and reputational risks of enforcement outweigh the benefits. We recommend settlement and contractual reform to mitigate future exposure and align with emerging legal and ethical standards.
