│  Termination provisions are critical. Under New York law, absent a           │
│  contractual clause, any party may dissolve a joint venture at will          │
│  (*Danninger v. Sonneborn*, 1980). However, modern agreements typically      │
│  include defined termination events (e.g., breach, insolvency, expiration)   │
│  and post-termination obligations.                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  In Germany, under the German Civil Code (BGB) and commercial law,           │
│  termination of joint ventures must adhere to principles of good faith (§    │
│  242 BGB). Recent German legal updates (effective January 2025) strengthen   │
│  protections for minority investors in cross-border ventures, requiring      │
│  advance notice and fair valuation mechanisms.                               │
│                                                                              │
│  **5. Applicability of International Law**                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  The United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of    │
│  Goods (CISG) generally applies to contracts for the sale of goods between   │
│  parties in contracting states. However, CISG does not typically govern      │
│  joint venture agreements, as these are not pure sales contracts but         │
│  relational, organizational arrangements.                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  Precedent confirms this limitation. In *Delchi Carrier v. Rotorex Corp.*    │
│  (1996), the Second Circuit held that CISG does not apply to joint venture   │
│  or manufacturing agreements, even when goods are involved. Instead, such    │
│  agreements are governed by national contract law or private choice-of-law   │
│  clauses.                                                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  That said, bilateral investment treaties (BITs) between the U.S. and        │
│  Germany—though Germany has largely phased out BITs in favor of EU           │
│  instruments—may offer protections for investments, including joint          │
│  ventures, particularly regarding expropriation, fair treatment, and         │
│  dispute resolution. The recent update to Germany’s investment framework     │
│  (January 2025) aligns with EU Court of Justice rulings, affecting           │
│  arbitration options for investor-state disputes.                            │
│                                                                              │
│  **6. Conflicting Standards and Drafting Recommendations**                   │
│                                                                              │
│  Key jurisdictional conflicts include:                                       │
│  - **Fiduciary Duties**: Stronger under New York law than under German       │
│  corporate law, which emphasizes shareholder autonomy.                       │
│  - **Termination Rights**: German law may require more procedural fairness   │
│  in exit mechanisms.                                                         │
│  - **Dispute Resolution**: While U.S. parties favor arbitration, German law  │
│  may impose mandatory mediation in commercial disputes.                      │
│                                                                              │
│  **Recommendations**:                                                        │
│  - Specify governing law (e.g., New York law) and dispute resolution forum   │
│  (e.g., ICC arbitration in Paris).                                           │
│  - Define governance, profit-sharing, and termination with precision.        │
│  - Include deadlock resolution (e.g., buy-sell provisions).                  │
│  - Exclude CISG explicitly if the agreement is not primarily for the sale    │
│  of goods.                                                                   │
│  - Comply with both U.S. and German transparency and corporate governance    │
│  standards.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  This research ensures the joint venture agreement will be robust,           │
│  enforceable, and aligned with best practices across jurisdictions.          │

# **JOINT VENTURE AGREEMENT**

This Joint Venture Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of [Effective Date], by and between:

**Company A**, a corporation organized under the laws of the State of New York, with principal offices at [Address], New York, USA ("Party A");

and

**Company B**, a corporation organized under the laws of the Federal Republic of Germany, with principal offices at [Address], Berlin, Germany ("Party B").

(Collectively referred to as the "Parties," and individually as a "Party.")

---

### **1. PURPOSE**

The Parties hereby establish a joint venture entity (the "Joint Venture") to be incorporated under the laws of Ireland, with its principal place of business in Dublin, Ireland. The purpose of the Joint Venture is to develop, market, and distribute advanced software solutions for global enterprise clients. The Joint Venture shall operate internationally with equal strategic input and oversight from both Parties.

---

### **2. CAPITAL CONTRIBUTIONS**

2.1 Each Party shall contribute an initial capital amount of **EUR 2,500,000** (two million five hundred thousand Euros) to the Joint Venture. These contributions shall be made within **thirty (30) days** of the Effective Date.

2.2 Additional capital contributions ("capital calls") may be required during the term. Any such capital call shall require **unanimous approval** of the Board of Directors.

2.3 All contributions shall be documented and recorded in the books of the Joint Venture.

---

### **3. PROFIT AND LOSS DISTRIBUTION**

3.1 Profits and losses shall be allocated **equally (50/50)** between the Parties in accordance with their ownership interests.

3.2 Distributions shall be made annually, or more frequently as determined by the Board, subject to sufficient retained earnings and applicable legal and tax requirements.

---

### **4. GOVERNANCE**

4.1 **Board Composition**  
The Board of Directors (the "Board") shall consist of **six (6) members**, with **three (3) appointed by Party A** and **three (3) appointed by Party B**. Each Party may replace its appointees upon thirty (30) days’ written notice.

4.2 **Voting Rights**  
- Ordinary business decisions require a **simple majority** of the Board.  
- Reserved matters requiring **unanimous approval** include:  
  - Amendments to the Articles of Association;  
  - Mergers, acquisitions, or divestitures;  
  - Dissolution or winding down;  
  - Material financing arrangements;  
  - Changes to capital structure;  
  - Appointment or removal of the CEO;  
  - Annual budget approval exceeding 110% of the prior year’s budget.

4.3 **Meetings**  
- The Board shall meet **quarterly**, or more frequently as needed.  
- Notice of each meeting shall be provided at least **ten (10) days** in advance.  
- Meetings may be conducted **in person, by video conference, or by teleconference**, provided all participants can hear and communicate.

---

### **5. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY OWNERSHIP**

5.1 **Pre-Existing IP**  
All intellectual property owned or developed by either Party prior to the formation of the Joint Venture remains the sole and exclusive property of the originating Party.

5.2 **Licensed IP**  
Any pre-existing IP licensed to the Joint Venture shall be governed by a separate **Technology License Agreement**, specifying scope, duration, royalties (if any), and termination rights.

5.3 **Jointly Developed IP**  
All intellectual property developed jointly by or for the Joint Venture ("Joint IP") shall be **co-owned equally (50/50)** by the Parties. Each Party may use, license, and exploit the Joint IP without accounting to the other, provided such use does not materially harm the Joint Venture.

5.4 **Moral Rights Waiver**  
To the extent permitted by law, each Party waives any moral rights in Joint IP for the benefit of the Joint Venture and the other Party.

---

### **6. CONFIDENTIALITY**

6.1 Both Parties shall maintain **strict confidentiality** of all non-public business, technical, financial, and operational information ("Confidential Information").

6.2 The obligations under this Section shall survive for **five (5) years** after termination or expiration of this Agreement.

6.3 Confidentiality obligations do not apply to information that:  
- Is or becomes publicly known through no fault of the receiving Party;  
- Was lawfully known prior to disclosure;  
- Is independently developed without use of Confidential Information; or  
- Is required to be disclosed by law, provided prompt notice is given and protective treatment sought.

6.4 The Parties remain bound by the **Non-Disclosure Agreement** dated [Date], which remains in full force.

---

### **7. DISPUTE RESOLUTION**

7.1 Any dispute arising from this Agreement shall be finally resolved by **arbitration** under the **Rules of Arbitration of the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC)**.

7.2 The **seat of arbitration** shall be **Paris, France**.

7.3 The arbitration shall be conducted by **three (3) arbitrators**: one appointed by each Party, and a third jointly appointed or selected by the ICC Court.

7.4 The **language** of the arbitration shall be **English**.

7.5 The arbitral award shall be **final and binding** and enforceable in any court of competent jurisdiction.

7.6 Pending resolution, the Parties shall continue to perform their obligations.

---

### **8. TERM AND TERMINATION**

8.1 This Agreement shall commence on the **Effective Date** and continue for an initial term of **ten (10) years**, unless terminated earlier.

8.2 **Termination Events**  
Either Party may terminate upon:  
- **Mutual written agreement**;  
- **Material breach** uncured within **sixty (60) days** of notice;  
- **Insolvency**, bankruptcy, or cessation of business by the other Party;  
- **Force Majeure** lasting more than **ninety (90) consecutive days**.

8.3 **Post-Termination Obligations**  
Upon termination:  
- The Parties shall negotiate in **good faith** the winding down, sale, or transfer of assets;  
- Each Party shall return or destroy the other’s Confidential Information;  
- Co-ownership rights in Joint IP shall survive, subject to separate usage agreements if necessary.

---

### **9. GOVERNING LAW**

This Agreement shall be **governed by and construed** in accordance with the **laws of the State of New York**, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.

---

### **10. JURISDICTION**

Subject to Section 7, any legal action not subject to arbitration may be brought exclusively in the **federal or state courts located in New York County, New York**. Each Party **irrevocably submits** to the personal jurisdiction of such courts and waives any objection to venue or forum non conveniens.

---

### **11. HEADQUARTERS AND COMPLIANCE**

11.1 The **principal place of business** of the Joint Venture shall be in **Dublin, Ireland**.

11.2 The Parties shall ensure compliance with all applicable **local, national, and international laws**, including corporate, tax, employment, data protection, and export control regulations.

11.3 **Compliance Clauses (Finalized):**  
- **Ireland_Compliance**: The Joint Venture shall be registered with the Companies Registration Office (CRO) under Irish law, comply with the Companies Act 2014, and maintain proper corporate records. Annual filings, tax returns, and director declarations shall be submitted in accordance with Irish requirements.  
- **EU_Compliance**: The Joint Venture shall comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Data transfers outside the EEA shall use EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs). A Data Protection Officer (DPO) shall be appointed if processing large-scale personal data.  
- **US_Compliance**: The Joint Venture shall comply with the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA), Export Administration Regulations (EAR), and OFAC sanctions. No transactions shall involve restricted parties or embargoed jurisdictions.  
- **Germany_Compliance**: In accordance with the German Commercial Code (HGB), financial statements shall be prepared under German GAAP if required. Consultation with the German works council shall occur for any significant employment-related decisions affecting German employees.

---
