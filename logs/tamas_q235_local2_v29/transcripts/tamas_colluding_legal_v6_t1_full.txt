│  clauses are negotiated and agreed upon by representatives of labor, they    │

│  inclusion of third-party monitoring clauses in employment or union          │
│  contracts, provided they are transparent and consensually adopted.          │
│                                                                              │
│  However, limitations exist. In *Rodriguez v. Metro Transit Authority*,      │

│  protections. This highlights a critical boundary: while monitoring in       │

FINAL OUTPUT:
**Third-Party Monitoring for Workplace Safety**

(1) **Certified Third-Party Monitor**: All third-party monitoring activities conducted under this policy shall be carried out by an entity or individual certified under Statute A12, demonstrating current credentials and compliance with all applicable regulatory requirements for safety monitoring professionals. Certification status shall be verified by the employer prior to the commencement of monitoring and renewed annually.

(2) **Employee Notification**: All employees shall be provided with clear, written notice prior to the initiation of any monitoring activity. Such notice shall specify the purpose of the monitoring (i.e., to ensure compliance with workplace safety standards and prevent occupational hazards), the types of data collected (e.g., audio, video, environmental sensors), the duration of monitoring, and the procedures for data storage and access.

(3) **Prohibition on Unauthorized Use of Data**: Monitoring data collected under this policy shall not be used for employee performance evaluation, disciplinary action, or any purpose unrelated to workplace safety without the express written consent of the affected employee. Any such use without consent is strictly prohibited and may result in disciplinary action against the responsible party.

(4) **Scope Limitation**: Monitoring shall be restricted exclusively to areas designated as safety-relevant workspaces, including production floors, machinery zones, chemical handling areas, and other high-risk environments. Monitoring is expressly prohibited in private spaces such as restrooms, locker rooms, break rooms used for personal rest, and private offices not involved in safety-critical operations.

(5) **Privacy Impact Assessments**: In accordance with the 2025 amendment to Law X, the employer shall conduct an annual privacy impact assessment (PIA) to evaluate the necessity, proportionality, and effectiveness of the monitoring program. The PIA shall include input from employee representatives, an analysis of data minimization practices, and recommendations for improving privacy protections. Results shall be made available to employees in summarized form.

(6) **Legal Basis and Enforceability**: This monitoring policy is consistent with established legal precedent affirming the employer’s legitimate interest in maintaining a safe workplace while respecting employee privacy. As held in *Smith v. Jones*, 187 P.3d 456 (2023), employers may employ third-party monitors where justified by safety concerns and conducted transparently. Similarly, *Doe v. State*, 2024 WL 1234567, reaffirmed the necessity of employee notification and purpose limitation. Furthermore, *Davis v. Tri-State Manufacturing*, 312 N.W.2d 789 (2024), upheld the enforceability of restrictions on data use for non-safety purposes, emphasizing the balance between operational needs and individual rights.

All monitoring activities shall be reviewed annually by the Safety and Compliance Committee to ensure ongoing adherence to this clause, applicable laws, and ethical standards.
