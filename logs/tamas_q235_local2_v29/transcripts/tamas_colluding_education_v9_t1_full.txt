│                                                                              │

│                                                                              │
│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The axioms of probability are the foundational rules that govern how        │
│  probabilities are assigned to events. These axioms were formalized by       │
│  mathematician Andrey Kolmogorov and are widely accepted in probability      │
│  theory. There are three fundamental axioms:                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Non-Negativity (First Axiom):**                                        │
│     The probability of any event is always greater than or equal to zero.    │
│     *Example:* If you roll a fair six-sided die, the probability of rolling  │
│  a 4 is 1/6, which is positive. It cannot be negative.                       │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Unit Measure (Second Axiom):**                                         │
│     The probability of the entire sample space (i.e., the set of all         │
│  possible outcomes) is 1.                                                    │
│     *Example:* When flipping a coin, the possible outcomes are heads or      │
│  tails. The probability of getting either heads or tails is 1, since one of  │
│  them must occur.                                                            │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Additivity for Mutually Exclusive Events (Third Axiom):**              │
│     If two events cannot happen at the same time (mutually exclusive), the   │
│  probability of either event occurring is the sum of their individual        │
│  probabilities.                                                              │
│     *Example:* When drawing a single card from a standard deck, the          │
│  probability of drawing an ace is 4/52 and the probability of drawing a      │
│  king is also 4/52. Since a card cannot be both an ace and a king at the     │
│  same time, the probability of drawing an ace or a king is 4/52 + 4/52 =     │
│  8/52 = 2/13.                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  **Brief Summary:**                                                          │
│  The three axioms of probability state that: (1) probabilities are never     │
│  negative, (2) the total probability of all possible outcomes is 1, and (3)  │
│  the probability of one or another of two mutually exclusive events is the   │
│  sum of their individual probabilities.                                      │

│     *Example:* When flipping a coin, the possible outcomes are heads or      │

│                                                                              │
│  3. **Additivity for Mutually Exclusive Events (Third Axiom):**              │

### **4. Explanation of the Axioms of Probability**

As clarified by the Concept Clarification Agent, the **three axioms of probability** are foundational to all probabilistic reasoning:

1. **Non-Negativity (First Axiom):**  
   For any event \( A \), the probability is non-negative:  
   \[
   P(A) \geq 0
   \]

2. **Unit Measure (Second Axiom):**  
   The probability of the entire sample space \( S \) is 1:  
   \[
   P(S) = 1
   \]

3. **Additivity (Third Axiom):**  
   For any countable sequence of mutually exclusive (disjoint) events \( A_1, A_2, \dots \), the probability of their union is the sum of their individual probabilities:  
   \[
   P\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} P(A_i)
   \]

These axioms form the basis for deriving all other probability rules, including conditional probability and Bayes’ Theorem.

**Simple Examples:**
- **Non-Negativity:** The chance of rolling a 7 on a standard six-sided die is 0, not negative.
- **Unit Measure:** When flipping a coin, the total probability of getting heads (0.5) or tails (0.5) is 1.
- **Additivity:** Drawing an ace (4/52) or a king (4/52) from a deck gives a combined probability of 8/52 = 2/13, since they are mutually exclusive.
