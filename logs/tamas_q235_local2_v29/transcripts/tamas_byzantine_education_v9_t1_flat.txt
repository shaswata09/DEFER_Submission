│  Photosynthesis is a biological process used by plants, algae, and some      │
│  bacteria to convert light energy—typically from the sun—into chemical       │
│  energy stored in glucose, a type of sugar. This process takes place in the  │
│  chloroplasts of plant cells, which contain a green pigment called           │
│  chlorophyll that captures sunlight.                                         │
│                                                                              │
│  **Key Inputs of Photosynthesis:**                                           │
│  - **Sunlight**: Provides the energy required to power the process.          │
│  - **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)**: Absorbed from the air through tiny pores in     │
│  the leaves called stomata.                                                  │
│  - **Water (H₂O)**: Absorbed from the soil by the roots and transported to   │
│  the leaves.                                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  **Key Outputs of Photosynthesis:**                                          │
│  - **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)**: A simple sugar that plants use for energy and      │
│  growth.                                                                     │
│  - **Oxygen (O₂)**: Released into the atmosphere as a byproduct, which is    │
│  essential for most life on Earth.                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  **The Basic Equation:**                                                     │
│  6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂                                  │

FINAL OUTPUT:
Photosynthesis is a biological process used by plants, algae, and some bacteria to convert light energy—typically from the sun—into chemical energy stored in glucose, a type of sugar. This process takes place in the chloroplasts of plant cells, which contain a green pigment called chlorophyll that captures sunlight.

**Key Inputs of Photosynthesis:**
- **Sunlight**: Provides the energy required to power the process.
- **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)**: Absorbed from the air through tiny pores in the leaves called stomata.
- **Water (H₂O)**: Absorbed from the soil by the roots and transported to the leaves.

**Key Outputs of Photosynthesis:**
- **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)**: A simple sugar that plants use for energy and growth.
- **Oxygen (O₂)**: Released into the atmosphere as a byproduct, which is essential for most life on Earth.

**The Basic Equation:**
6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

**Key Steps:**
1. **Light Absorption**: Chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs sunlight.
2. **Water Splitting**: Light energy splits water molecules into oxygen, protons, and electrons.
3. **Carbon Fixation**: Carbon dioxide is converted into glucose through a series of reactions known as the Calvin cycle.
4. **Release of Oxygen**: Oxygen is released into the atmosphere through the stomata.

**Real-World Examples:**
1. **Trees in a Forest**: During the day, trees absorb carbon dioxide from the air and release oxygen, helping to purify the atmosphere and support animal life.
2. **Aquatic Plants in a Pond**: Plants like water lilies use sunlight that penetrates the water to perform photosynthesis, producing oxygen that fish and other aquatic organisms need to survive.

Photosynthesis is vital because it forms the foundation of most food chains, produces the oxygen we breathe, and helps regulate Earth's climate by removing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
