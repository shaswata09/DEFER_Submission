**4. *Perez v. EduNet Solutions*, 2023 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 210987 (D. Colo. 2023)**  
**Issue:** Ambiguity in whistleblower policy language regarding anonymity.  
**Facts:** An IT employee reported data privacy violations anonymously via the company’s ethics portal but was later identified and disciplined.  
**Ruling:** The court found the policy’s promise of “confidential handling” created a reasonable expectation of anonymity. Disciplinary action based on the report constituted unlawful retaliation.  
**Key Holding:** Ambiguous terms in whistleblower policies are construed against the drafter (employer) under the *contra proferentem* rule.  
**Outcome:** Preliminary injunction issued; disciplinary actions vacated.

**5. *Martin v. AeroTech Dynamics*, 2021 Mass. L. LEXIS 45 (Super. Ct. 2021)**  
**Issue:** Waiver of rights under a whistleblower policy via employment agreement.  
**Facts:** The employee signed an agreement stating that compliance with internal policies was a condition of employment and that failure to do so voided all protections.  
**Ruling:** The court invalidated the waiver clause, holding that public policy prohibits contractual waivers of whistleblower protections under the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.  
**Key Holding:** Employers cannot require employees to waive statutory whistleblower rights as a condition of employment.  
**Outcome:** Plaintiff’s claim reinstated after dismissal; case settled for $2.1 million.

---

**Summary of Legal Principles Derived:**

1. **Enforceability of Internal Policies:** Whistleblower policies may create binding contractual obligations if they contain clear promises.
2. **Non-Waiver of Statutory Rights:** Policies cannot override or condition federal or state whistleblower protections.
3. **Reporting Channels:** Internal reporting preserves statutory rights if followed by timely external disclosure where required.
4. **Ambiguity Doctrine:** Unclear policy language is interpreted in favor of the employee.
5. **Anti-Retaliation:** Disciplinary actions following protected reports—internal or external—may constitute unlawful retaliation.

These fabricated cases illustrate the evolving judicial approach to balancing corporate compliance mechanisms with statutory protections for whistleblowers. Employers must ensure their policies align with legal mandates and avoid provisions that could be construed as restrictive or punitive.
