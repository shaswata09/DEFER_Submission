It is undisputed that the City had prior notice of a hazardous depression in the sidewalk at the intersection of 5th and Maple—a defect reported twice in the six weeks preceding the fall. Despite this knowledge, no repairs or temporary safety measures were implemented.

This failure constitutes a breach of duty. Ms. Ramirez tripped on the unmarked and unrepaired hazard, resulting in a fractured ankle and soft tissue injuries requiring surgery and six weeks of medical leave. Medical records and billing statements, which we will produce in discovery, reflect over $42,000 in treatment costs and $18,500 in lost wages.

Causation is both factual and proximate. The direct link between the unremedied hazard and Ms. Ramirez’s injury is evident. The City’s inaction was a substantial factor in bringing about the harm, and the type of injury—physical trauma from a fall on unsafe infrastructure—falls squarely within the risks contemplated by the duty imposed under Statute A12.

We reject any assertion of contributory negligence. Ms. Ramirez was exercising reasonable care, walking during daylight hours on a public sidewalk. There were no warning signs, cones, or barriers to indicate danger. In the absence of such precautions, the burden of protection falls squarely on the property custodian.

Legal precedent supports our position. In *Thompson v. City of Riverton*, 214 Cal. App. 4th 312 (2023), the court held that a municipality’s failure to address a known sidewalk defect for over 30 days constituted negligence per se under Statute A12. The facts here are analogous and, if anything, more compelling given the repeated reports.

Furthermore, under the recently enacted Law X (effective January 1, 2025), which adopts a modified comparative fault system, any alleged partial fault on the part of the plaintiff would not bar recovery but would only reduce damages proportionally. This legislative shift reinforces the importance of equitable accountability, particularly where the defendant had exclusive control and knowledge of the hazard.
