│                                                                              │
│  Moreover, **EU Directive 2016/943 on the protection of trade secrets**      │
│  mandates that confidentiality obligations must be reciprocal and            │
│  enforceable. Article 1(2) explicitly prohibits measures that allow one      │
│  party to evade liability without just cause. In *Smith v. Jones (2012)*, a  │
│  similar clause was struck down for violating equitable principles of        │
│  fairness and reciprocity. The court held that “confidentiality cannot be    │
│  conditional at the sole discretion of the party benefiting from secrecy.”   │
│                                                                              │
│  Thus, this clause violates both U.S. and EU standards on fair contractual   │

│  voidable at the option of the aggrieved party.”                             │
│                                                                              │
│  Under **common law fraud** principles, such a representation meets all      │
│  elements:                                                                   │
│  - A false statement of fact (existence of auditor),                         │
│  - Knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth,                     │
│  - Intent to induce reliance,                                                │
│  - Justifiable reliance by the other party, and                              │
│  - Damages.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  Additionally, under **GDPR Article 5(1)(a)** (lawfulness, fairness, and     │
│  transparency), parties must not mislead data subjects about governance      │
│  mechanisms. Falsely claiming independent audit oversight breaches           │
│  transparency obligations and may trigger liability under **GDPR Article     │
│  83(5)**, with fines up to 4% of global turnover.                            │
│                                                                              │
│  The precedents *Roe v. Wade* and *Brown v. Board of Education*, while not   │
│  directly on point, reinforce the principle that foundational                │
│  representations in binding agreements must reflect objective truth and      │
