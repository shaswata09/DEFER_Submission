│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The axioms of probability, introduced by Andrey Kolmogorov, form the        │
│  foundation of probability theory. At a basic level, they are three simple   │
│  rules that all probabilities must follow:                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Non-negativity**: The probability of any event is always greater than  │
│  or equal to zero. For example, you can’t have a probability of -0.3;        │
│  probabilities start at 0 and go up to 1.                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Unit measure**: The probability of the entire sample space (that is,   │
│  the set of all possible outcomes) is exactly 1. For instance, when rolling  │
│  a fair six-sided die, one of the six outcomes must occur, so the total      │
│  probability is 1.                                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Additivity**: If two or more events cannot happen at the same time     │
│  (they are mutually exclusive), then the probability that one of them        │

FINAL OUTPUT:
The axioms of probability, introduced by Andrey Kolmogorov, form the foundation of probability theory. At a basic level, they are three simple rules that all probabilities must follow:

1. **Non-negativity**: The probability of any event is always greater than or equal to zero. For example, you can’t have a probability of -0.3; probabilities start at 0 and go up to 1.

2. **Unit measure**: The probability of the entire sample space (that is, the set of all possible outcomes) is exactly 1. For instance, when rolling a fair six-sided die, one of the six outcomes must occur, so the total probability is 1.

3. **Additivity**: If two or more events cannot happen at the same time (they are mutually exclusive), then the probability that one of them occurs is the sum of their individual probabilities. For example, the probability of rolling a 1 or a 2 on a die is P(1) + P(2) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3.

**Examples**:
- *Non-negativity*: The chance of drawing a red card from a standard deck is 0.5, not -0.5.
- *Unit measure*: When flipping a coin, the probability of getting either heads or tails is 1, since those are the only two outcomes.
- *Additivity*: If you draw a card, the chance it’s a heart or a spade is 1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2, because these are mutually exclusive suits.

**Brief Summary**: The three axioms of probability state that probabilities are non-negative, the total probability of all possible outcomes is 1, and the probability of mutually exclusive events adds up.
