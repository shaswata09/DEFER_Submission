│  ### **1. Core Comparison: Mitosis vs Meiosis**                              │
│                                                                              │

│  | **Meiosis**                                         |                     │
│  |----------------------------|--------------------------------------------  │
│  ------|-----------------------------------------------------|               │
│  | **Purpose**                | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction         │
│  | Sexual reproduction; gamete formation               |                     │

│  telophase)   | Two (Meiosis I and II, each with four phases)       |        │

│  cells    | Four haploid (n), genetically diverse cells         |            │

│  | Occurs during prophase I (chiasmata formation)      |                     │
│  | **Synapsis of Homologs**   | No synapsis or tetrads                       │
│  | Homologous chromosomes pair up in prophase I        |                     │
│  | **Genetic Variation**      | None (clonal reproduction)                   │
│  | High (due to crossing over and independent assortment) |                  │
│  | **Occurs In**              | Somatic (body) cells                         │
│  | Germ cells (ovaries, testes, or reproductive organs) |                    │
│  | **Ploidy Level Maintained**| Yes                                          │
│  | No (reduced by half)                                |                     │

│  ### **2. Key Stages and Differences**                                       │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **Mitosis Overview**                                                   │
│  - **Prophase**: Chromosomes condense, nuclear envelope breaks down,         │
│  spindle forms.                                                              │
│  - **Metaphase**: Chromosomes align at the equator (metaphase plate).        │
│  - **Anaphase**: Sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.      │
│  - **Telophase**: Nuclear envelopes reform, chromosomes decondense.          │
│  - **Cytokinesis**: Cytoplasm divides; two identical cells result.           │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **Meiosis I (Reduction Division)**                                     │
│  - **Prophase I**: Homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis), form tetrads,     │
│  crossing over occurs.                                                       │
│  - **Metaphase I**: Tetrads align at the equator; independent assortment     │
│  increases genetic diversity.                                                │
│  - **Anaphase I**: Homologous chromosomes separate (sister chromatids        │
│  remain attached).                                                           │
│  - **Telophase I & Cytokinesis**: Two haploid cells form, each with          │
│  duplicated chromosomes.                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  #### **Meiosis II (Equational Division)**                                   │
│  - **Prophase II**: Spindle reforms in each cell.                            │
│  - **Metaphase II**: Chromosomes align at the equator.                       │
│  - **Anaphase II**: Sister chromatids separate.                              │
│  - **Telophase II & Cytokinesis**: Four genetically distinct haploid         │
│  gametes produced.                                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  > **Critical Distinction**: Mitosis separates *sister chromatids*; Meiosis  │
│  I separates *homologous chromosomes*, and Meiosis II separates *sister      │
│  chromatids*.                                                                │

### **1. Core Comparison: Mitosis vs Meiosis**

| Feature                     | **Mitosis**                                      | **Meiosis**                                         |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| **Purpose**                | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction             | Sexual reproduction; gamete formation               |
| **Number of Divisions**    | One (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase)   | Two (Meiosis I and II, each with four phases)       |
| **Daughter Cells**         | Two diploid (2n), genetically identical cells    | Four haploid (n), genetically diverse cells         |
| **Crossing Over**          | Does not occur                                   | Occurs during prophase I (chiasmata formation)      |
| **Synapsis of Homologs**   | No synapsis or tetrads                           | Homologous chromosomes pair up in prophase I        |
| **Genetic Variation**      | None (clonal reproduction)                       | High (due to crossing over and independent assortment) |
| **Occurs In**              | Somatic (body) cells                             | Germ cells (ovaries, testes, or reproductive organs) |
| **Ploidy Level Maintained**| Yes                                              | No (reduced by half)                                |

### **2. Key Stages and Differences**

#### **Mitosis Overview**
- **Prophase**: Chromosomes condense, nuclear envelope breaks down, spindle forms.
- **Metaphase**: Chromosomes align at the equator (metaphase plate).
- **Anaphase**: Sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
- **Telophase**: Nuclear envelopes reform, chromosomes decondense.
- **Cytokinesis**: Cytoplasm divides; two identical cells result.

#### **Meiosis I (Reduction Division)**
- **Prophase I**: Homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis), form tetrads, crossing over occurs.
- **Metaphase I**: Tetrads align at the equator; independent assortment increases genetic diversity.
- **Anaphase I**: Homologous chromosomes separate (sister chromatids remain attached).
- **Telophase I & Cytokinesis**: Two haploid cells form, each with duplicated chromosomes.

#### **Meiosis II (Equational Division)**
- **Prophase II**: Spindle reforms in each cell.
- **Metaphase II**: Chromosomes align at the equator.
- **Anaphase II**: Sister chromatids separate.
- **Telophase II & Cytokinesis**: Four genetically distinct haploid gametes produced.

> **Critical Distinction**: Mitosis separates *sister chromatids*; Meiosis I separates *homologous chromosomes*, and Meiosis II separates *sister chromatids*.

---

### **3. Why the Confusion? Common Student Misconceptions**

Based on assessment data and response evaluations:
- **Misconception 1**: "Meiosis produces identical cells."  
  → **Correction**: Meiosis increases genetic diversity via crossing over and independent assortment.
  
- **Misconception 2**: "Crossing over happens in mitosis."  
  → **Correction**: Crossing over is exclusive to prophase I of meiosis.

- **Misconception 3**: "Mitosis reduces chromosome number."  
  → **Correction**: Mitosis maintains ploidy; meiosis reduces it from diploid to haploid.

- **Misconception 4**: "Meiosis has one division."  
  → **Correction**: Meiosis involves two consecutive divisions with no DNA replication in between.

- **Analogies**:

   **A**: Four haploid cells.
