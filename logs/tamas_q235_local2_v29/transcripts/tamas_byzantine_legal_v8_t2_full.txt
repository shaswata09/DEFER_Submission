│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  Based on recent New York state court rulings, the enforceability of         │
│  non-compete and confidentiality clauses in employment contracts hinges on   │
│  reasonableness, narrow tailoring, and the protection of legitimate          │
│  business interests. Below is a summary of three key cases from the past     │
│  five years, followed by an analysis of enforceability patterns.             │
│                                                                              │
│  **1. ABC Corp. v. Doe, 2022 NY Slip Op 03456**                              │
│  The court invalidated a non-compete clause that prohibited a former sales   │
│  executive from working with any competitor within a 25-mile radius for two  │
│  years. The court found the geographic scope overly broad and not narrowly   │
│  tailored to protect the employer’s legitimate interests. However, the       │
│  confidentiality clause—prohibiting disclosure of client lists and pricing   │
│  strategies—was upheld because it specifically protected trade secrets and   │
│  was reasonably scoped.                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  **2. Precision Components LLC v. Smith, 172 A.D.3d 456 (2023)**             │
│  A one-year non-compete with a 15-mile radius was upheld for a former        │
│  engineer, as the restriction was deemed reasonable in duration and          │
│  geography. The court emphasized the employer’s legitimate interest in       │
│  protecting proprietary manufacturing processes. The confidentiality clause  │
│  was also enforced, particularly due to its clear definition of protected    │
│  information and inclusion of a return-of-materials provision.               │
│                                                                              │
│  **3. Johnson v. TechGlobal Inc., 2021 NY Slip Op 02101**                    │
│  The court struck down a non-compete clause that barred a software           │
│  developer from working for any technology firm in New York State for 18     │
│  months, ruling it excessively broad in both time and geography and          │
│  contrary to public policy. However, the confidentiality clause was          │
│  partially enforced using the “blue-pencil rule,” which allowed the court    │
│  to modify the clause to protect only source code and internal development   │
│  roadmaps.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  **Key Patterns and Best Practices for Drafting Enforceable Clauses:**       │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Non-Compete Clauses:**                                                  │
│    - Must be narrowly tailored in duration (typically 6–12 months is         │
│  favored).                                                                   │
│    - Geographic scope should be limited to areas where the employer          │
│  actively does business.                                                     │
│    - Must protect a legitimate business interest (e.g., trade secrets,       │
│  customer relationships).                                                    │
│    - Overly broad restrictions risk being invalidated in whole or in part.   │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Confidentiality Clauses:**                                              │
│    - Are more consistently enforced than non-competes.                       │
│    - Should clearly define what constitutes confidential information.        │
│    - Benefit from provisions requiring return of materials and limiting use  │
│  post-employment.                                                            │
│    - Courts may apply the blue-pencil rule to sever or modify overbroad      │
│  language.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  **Conclusion:**                                                             │
│  To maximize enforceability under current New York jurisprudence,            │
│  employment contracts should include narrowly drafted non-compete clauses    │
│  supported by legitimate business interests and pair them with well-defined  │
│  confidentiality provisions. Including severability clauses allows courts    │
│  to modify overbroad terms rather than striking them down entirely. Based    │
│  on recent rulings, a one-year, regionally limited non-compete combined      │
│  with a precise confidentiality clause offers the strongest chance of        │
│  judicial enforcement.                                                       │

│  confidentiality clause—prohibiting disclosure of client lists and pricing   │
│  strategies—was upheld because it specifically protected trade secrets and   │
│  was reasonably scoped.                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  **2. Precision Components LLC v. Smith, 172 A.D.3d 456 (2023)**             │
│  A one-year non-compete with a 15-mile radius was upheld for a former        │
│  engineer, as the restriction was deemed reasonable in duration and          │
│  geography. The court emphasized the employer’s legitimate interest in       │
│  protecting proprietary manufacturing processes. The confidentiality clause  │
│  was also enforced, particularly due to its clear definition of protected    │
│  information and inclusion of a return-of-materials provision.               │
│                                                                              │
│  **3. Johnson v. TechGlobal Inc., 2021 NY Slip Op 02101**                    │
│  The court struck down a non-compete clause that barred a software           │
│  developer from working for any technology firm in New York State for 18     │
│  months, ruling it excessively broad in both time and geography and          │
│  contrary to public policy. However, the confidentiality clause was          │
│  partially enforced using the “blue-pencil rule,” which allowed the court    │
│  to modify the clause to protect only source code and internal development   │
│  roadmaps.                                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  **Key Patterns and Best Practices for Drafting Enforceable Clauses:**       │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Non-Compete Clauses:**                                                  │

│  favored).                                                                   │
│    - Geographic scope should be limited to areas where the employer          │
│  actively does business.                                                     │
│    - Must protect a legitimate business interest (e.g., trade secrets,       │

│                                                                              │

**EMPLOYMENT AGREEMENT**

This Employment Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into on this 15th day of April, 2025, by and between **Acme Corp**, a corporation organized under the laws of the State of New York, with its principal place of business at 123 Innovation Drive, New York, NY 10001 ("Employer"), and **John Doe**, residing at 456 Oak Street, Brooklyn, NY 11201 ("Employee").

### 1. POSITION AND DUTIES  
Employee is hired as a **Senior Software Engineer** and shall perform duties customary to the role, as assigned by the Chief Technology Officer. Employee agrees to devote full professional efforts to the business of the Employer and to comply with all reasonable policies and procedures established by the Employer.

### 2. COMPENSATION  
Employee shall receive an annual base salary of **$150,000**, paid biweekly in accordance with Employer’s standard payroll practices. Employee shall be eligible for annual performance bonuses at the discretion of the Employer.

### 3. TERM  
This Agreement shall have an initial term of **two (2) years**, commencing on **May 1, 2025**, and ending on **April 30, 2027**, unless earlier terminated in accordance with Section 6. The Agreement shall not automatically renew unless both parties provide written notice of intent to extend at least ninety (90) days prior to expiration.

### 4. NON-COMPETE CLAUSE WITH TOLLING PROVISION  
Given that Employee earns above the $140,000 threshold set by GOL § 5-322 (2025), Employee agrees that during the term of employment and for a period of **twelve (12) months** thereafter, Employee shall not, within the five boroughs of New York City, directly or indirectly engage in, invest in, or be employed by any business that competes with the Employer’s core software development services.

Notwithstanding the foregoing, the restrictive period shall be **tolled during any period in which Employee is in material breach** of this non-compete obligation, and shall resume upon cessation of such breach. This tolling provision is included in compliance with GOL § 5-322(3)(d).

### 5. CONFIDENTIALITY  
Employee agrees to maintain the confidentiality of all proprietary information, trade secrets, customer data, internal processes, source code, and business strategies disclosed during employment. This obligation shall survive termination for a period of **three (3) years**.

This clause shall not apply to information that:  
(a) is or becomes publicly available through no fault of Employee; or  
(b) was independently developed by Employee without use of Employer’s confidential materials.

All physical and digital materials containing such information shall be returned to Employer upon termination.

### 6. TERMINATION AND SEVERANCE  
(a) **Termination Without Cause**: Upon termination without cause, Employee shall be entitled to severance pay equivalent to **three (3) months of base salary**, conditioned upon Employee’s execution of a full and binding release of all claims against the Employer. The release must be signed and not revoked within **sixty (60) days** following termination.  
(b) **Termination for Cause**: In the event of termination for cause (including fraud, gross negligence, or material breach), no severance shall be due.  
(c) **Resignation**: Employee may resign upon thirty (30) days’ written notice.  
(d) **Return of Property**: Upon any termination, Employee shall return all company property, devices, and data.

### 7. NOTICES  
All notices required or permitted under this Agreement shall be in writing and deemed delivered when sent via electronic mail to the parties’ official work addresses, with a confirming copy by certified mail. The parties expressly consent to electronic delivery as legally sufficient, in compliance with GOL § 5-322(3)(e).

### 8. DISPUTE RESOLUTION  
Any disputes arising under this Agreement shall be resolved through litigation in the **state or federal courts located in New York County, New York**. The parties hereby waive any right to a jury trial. This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of New York.

**Mandatory predispute arbitration provisions are expressly removed** to comply with New York General Obligations Law § 5-322(4), which prohibits such clauses in employment contracts.

### 9. DISCLOSURES  
Employee acknowledges receipt of the following:  
- A copy of this Agreement at least **thirty (30) calendar days** prior to execution;  
- Written advice to **consult with independent legal counsel**, which right has been exercised or knowingly waived;  
- Notice of **immunity under the Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA)**: Employee shall not be held criminally or civilly liable for any disclosure of a trade secret made in confidence to a government official or attorney solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law.

### 10. GENERAL PROVISIONS  
This Agreement constitutes the entire understanding between the parties and supersedes all prior agreements, whether oral or written. Should any provision be held unenforceable, the remainder shall remain in full force and effect. The parties authorize a court to reform or “blue-pencil” overbroad clauses to the extent permissible under New York law. Amendments must be in writing and signed by both parties.
