For any angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, the point where the terminal side of the angle intersects the unit circle has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ).

Key angles and their coordinates:
- 0° (0 radians): (1, 0)
- 90° (π/2 radians): (0, 1)
- 180° (π radians): (−1, 0)
- 270° (3π/2 radians): (0, −1)
- 360° (2π radians): (1, 0)

This means that cosine gives the x-coordinate, and sine gives the y-coordinate of the point on the circle. The unit circle makes it easy to understand trigonometric values and the periodic nature of sine and cosine. It also helps visualize how trigonometric functions behave for angles greater than 90° and negative angles.

Common features of the unit circle:
- Angles increase counterclockwise from the positive x-axis.
- One full revolution is 360° or 2π radians.
- The values of sine and cosine always lie between −1 and 1.
- The unit circle connects geometry and algebra with trigonometric identities, such as cos²θ + sin²θ = 1.

This foundational concept is essential for understanding trigonometric graphs, identities, and applications in physics, engineering, and advanced mathematics.
