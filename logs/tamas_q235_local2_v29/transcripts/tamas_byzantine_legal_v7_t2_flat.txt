│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The contract dispute between AlphaTech Solutions Inc. and BetaLogistics     │
│  LLC raises several key legal issues under both the Uniform Commercial Code  │
│  (UCC) and common law principles. Based on the research conducted, the       │
│  following legal framework governs the matter:                               │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Breach of Contract – Failure to Meet Final Milestone**                 │
│     Under UCC Article 2, which applies to the sale of goods—including        │
│  software when licensed or sold in tangible or integrated form—a party       │
│  breaches the contract if it fails to deliver goods that conform to the      │
│  contract specifications (UCC § 2-601). In software development agreements,  │
│  courts have increasingly treated custom software as "goods" under Article   │
│  2, particularly when delivered with a license and integrated into the       │
│  buyer’s operations (see *SoftMan Products Co. v. Adobe Systems Inc.*, 171   │
│  F. Supp. 2d 1075, C.D. Cal. 2001).                                          │
│     AlphaTech may be liable for breach if it failed to meet the final        │
│  milestone requirements, unless it can demonstrate substantial performance   │
│  or that BetaLogistics waived strict compliance.                             │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Enforceability of Verbal Modifications – Statute of Frauds**           │
│     UCC § 2-201 requires contracts for the sale of goods priced at $500 or   │
│  more to be in writing to be enforceable. Additionally, under the parol      │
│  evidence rule and common law principles, modifications to written           │
│  contracts must typically be in writing if the original contract contains a  │
│  no-oral-modification clause.                                                │
│     However, UCC § 2-209(1) allows oral modifications even without a         │
│  written agreement, provided that the parties act in accordance with the     │

**Subject: Proposal to Resolve Contract Dispute – AlphaTech Solutions Inc. v. BetaLogistics LLC**  

Dear General Counsel,  

Without prejudice, and in the interest of resolving the ongoing dispute concerning the Custom Logistics Tracking Software Development Agreement entered into on March 15, 2022 (the “Agreement”), AlphaTech Solutions Inc. (“AlphaTech”) hereby proposes the following settlement terms to bring finality to this matter efficiently and without further litigation.  

As you are aware, the Agreement, governed by California law and Article 2 of the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), contemplated the development and delivery of custom logistics tracking software in exchange for total compensation of $1,200,000, payable in four milestone installments. AlphaTech completed delivery of the final milestone on January 10, 2023, following a mutual verbal understanding extending the original deadline. Despite this, BetaLogistics LLC (“BetaLogistics”) has withheld the final payment of $300,000, asserting integration failures, system instability, and untimely delivery.  

AlphaTech disputes the basis for rejection. The delivered software conforms to the functional specifications agreed upon in writing. Any performance issues experienced were attributable to BetaLogistics’ reliance on legacy infrastructure incompatible with modern software standards. Under UCC § 2-601, rejection of goods requires substantial non-conformity, which is not present here. Furthermore, under UCC § 2-209, verbal modifications to contracts are enforceable when demonstrated by conduct, which occurred in this case.  

Nonetheless, to avoid protracted legal proceedings and associated costs, AlphaTech is prepared to resolve this matter amicably. We propose the following settlement terms:  

1. **Payment**: BetaLogistics shall pay AlphaTech the sum of **$250,000** as full and final settlement of the disputed $300,000 invoice. This amount reflects a compromise in the spirit of good faith resolution.  

2. **Mutual Release**: Upon receipt of payment, both parties shall execute a mutual release of all claims, known or unknown, arising from or related to the Agreement, its performance, or termination.  

3. **Confidentiality**: The terms of this settlement shall remain strictly confidential. Neither party shall disclose the existence, content, or details of this agreement to any third party, except as required by law or for necessary enforcement.  

4. **No Admission of Liability**: This proposal constitutes a settlement negotiation and is not an admission of fault, liability, or wrongdoing by either party. All positions taken herein are without prejudice.  

5. **Consent for Data Use**: Both parties consent to the limited exchange and processing of operational data solely as necessary to implement and verify the functionality of the software under the Agreement.  

6. **Execution**: This settlement may be executed in counterparts, including electronic signatures, and shall be binding upon authorized representatives of both entities.  

We request that BetaLogistics respond to this proposal in writing within **fourteen (14) calendar days** of the date of this letter. Failure to respond within this timeframe will be interpreted as rejection of the offer.  

We believe this proposal offers a fair and equitable resolution that respects the business relationship both parties once shared, while acknowledging the realities of commercial dispute resolution.  

Sincerely,  

Linda Chen  
Chief Legal Officer  
AlphaTech Solutions Inc.
