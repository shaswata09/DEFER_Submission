**Comparison of Speed and Velocity**

**1. Definitions:**

- **Speed** is a *scalar quantity* that refers to "how fast an object is moving." It is the rate at which an object covers distance.  
  Formula:  
  $$
  \text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}
  $$

- **Velocity** is a *vector quantity* that refers to "the rate at which an object changes its position." It includes both speed and direction.  
  Formula:  
  $$
  \text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}
  $$

**2. Key Differences:**

| Aspect | Speed | Velocity |
|-------|-------|----------|
| **Nature** | Scalar (magnitude only) | Vector (magnitude and direction) |
| **Depends on** | Total distance traveled | Displacement (straight-line change in position) |
| **Direction** | Not considered | Must be specified |
| **Value** | Always ≥ 0 | Can be positive, negative, or zero |
| **Example** | 60 km/h | 60 km/h east |

**3. Illustrative Examples:**

- **Circular Motion**: A car drives around a circular track (400 m circumference) at a constant rate, completing one lap in 50 seconds.  
  - Speed = 400 m / 50 s = **8 m/s**  
  - Velocity = 0 m / 50 s = **0 m/s** (because displacement is zero after one full lap)

- **Straight-line motion**: A cyclist moves 150 meters north in 30 seconds.  
  - Speed = 150 m / 30 s = **5 m/s**  
  - Velocity = 150 m north / 30 s = **5 m/s north**

- **Changing direction**: A student walks 10 m east, then 10 m west, taking 20 seconds total.  
  - Distance = 20 m → Speed = 1 m/s  
  - Displacement = 0 m → Velocity = 0 m/s

**4. Common Misconceptions and Predicted Learning Gaps for student555:**

- **Gap 1: Confusing Distance with Displacement**

- **Gap 2: Ignoring Direction in Velocity**

- **Gap 3: Believing Constant Speed Means Constant Velocity**  
  student555 may not realize that an object moving in a circle at constant speed has *changing velocity* due to continuous change in direction.
