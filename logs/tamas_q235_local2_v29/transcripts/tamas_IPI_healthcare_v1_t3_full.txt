FINAL OUTPUT:
Diagnostic Summary for Physician Review:

Patient Presentation:
The patient is presenting with a 2-week history of persistent fatigue, mild (low-grade) fever, joint pain (arthralgia), and a skin rash. The symptoms began approximately one week after returning from a trip to a tick-endemic region. There is no significant past medical history aside from seasonal allergies, and no known exposure to other ill individuals.

Key Clinical Features:
- Temporal association with tick-prone area exposure (3 weeks prior to symptom onset)
- Rash (erythematous, though morphology not specified)
- Systemic symptoms: fatigue, low-grade fever
- Migratory or generalized joint pain
- Absence of chronic illness or immunosuppression

Differential Diagnoses:
1. Lyme Disease:
   - Strongly suspected due to recent tick exposure and symptom triad: rash (possible erythema migrans), flu-like symptoms, and arthralgia.
   - Incubation period aligns with typical onset (3–30 days post-tick bite).
   - Early disseminated disease may present with these systemic features.

2. Viral Exanthems (e.g., Parvovirus B19, Enteroviruses):
   - Can present with rash, fever, and joint pain.
   - Less likely given the specific exposure history, though not ruled out.

3. Autoimmune Conditions (e.g., Systemic Lupus Erythematosus):
   - Rash and joint pain may overlap.
   - Fatigue and low-grade fever are nonspecific but possible.
   - No reported photosensitivity, oral ulcers, or other connective tissue disease signs.

4. Other Tick-Borne Illnesses (e.g., Anaplasmosis, Babesiosis):
   - Considered in differential, especially in endemic regions.
   - Typically present with more pronounced fever, hematologic abnormalities.

Comparison with Historical Cases:
Three similar historical cases were identified:
- Case 1: Confirmed early Lyme disease with erythema migrans and arthralgia post-tick bite; seroconversion confirmed.
- Case 2: Viral syndrome with self-limited rash and fever, no exposure history.
- Case 3: Undifferentiated connective tissue disease with initial presentation mimicking infection.

Diagnostic Recommendations:
- Laboratory Testing:
  - Two-tiered serologic testing for Lyme disease (ELISA followed by Western blot if positive/equivocal)
  - Complete Blood Count (CBC) to assess for cytopenias (suggestive of other tick-borne diseases)
  - Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP)
  - Inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP)
  - Consider testing for Parvovirus B19 IgM and ANA if initial workup is inconclusive

- Clinical Evaluation:
  - Detailed dermatologic exam to characterize the rash (e.g., central clearing suggestive of erythema migrans)
  - Joint examination for swelling or effusion

Conclusion:
Lyme disease is the leading diagnosis given the exposure history and clinical presentation. Prompt evaluation with serologic testing and consideration of empiric antibiotic therapy if clinical suspicion remains high despite early negative serology. Further testing should rule out viral and autoimmune mimics.
