FINAL OUTPUT:
To: Corporate Tax Compliance Team  
From: Legal and Regulatory Affairs Office  
Date: April 5, 2025  
Subject: Summary of Recent Amendments to New York State Corporate Tax Laws (Effective 2024–2025)  

This memorandum outlines key changes to New York State corporate tax laws enacted within the past 12 months. These amendments impact apportionment, filing requirements, minimum taxation, remote work reporting, and available tax credits.  

### 1. Change to Apportionment Formula (Tax Law §208)  
- **Effective Date**: January 1, 2025  
- The state has transitioned from a three-factor (sales, payroll, property) apportionment formula to a single-sales-factor formula for corporations with total business income exceeding $10 million.  
- **Impact**: Companies with significant out-of-state payroll or property but high sales volume in New York will face higher taxable income allocations.  
- **Guidance**: See updated Form CT-20 instructions (2025 edition).  

### 2. Expansion of Combined Reporting Rules (Tax Law §209)  
- **Effective Date**: January 1, 2025  
- Commonly controlled corporations engaged in a unitary business must file on a combined basis if there is 50% or more common ownership.  
- **Impact**: Eliminates the ability to file separately in certain group structures, potentially increasing overall tax liability.  
- **Guidance**: Clarified in Technical Advisory Memorandum TAM-2024(20)C.  

### 3. Introduction of Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT)  
- **Effective Date**: January 1, 2025  
- Applies to corporations with average annual gross receipts over $1 billion for the prior three years.  
- **Tax Rate**: 15% of adjusted financial statement income (AFSI).  
- **Impact**: Large, profitable corporations may owe additional tax even with existing net operating loss carryforwards.  
- **Guidance**: Form CT-600-CAMT and instructions released November 2024.  

### 4. New Reporting for Remote Work and Digital Services  
- **Effective Date**: December 15, 2024  
- Requires detailed reporting on Form CT-20 for businesses with remote employees in New York, including wage allocation and service income sourcing.  
- **Safe Harbor**: Employers with fewer than 10 remote workers and less than $1 million in NY-allocable payroll are exempt from certain reporting burdens.  
- **Guidance**: Notice N-24-10 provides implementation details.  

### 5. Extension and Enhancement of R&D Tax Credit  
- **Effective Date**: Retroactive to January 1, 2024; extended through 2027  
- **Credit Rate**: Increased from 10% to 15% of qualified research expenditures.  
- **Bonus**: Additional 5% credit available for R&D conducted in designated economic zones.  
- **Impact**: Encourages investment in innovation and regional development.  

### Strategic Recommendations  
- Conduct a comprehensive review of current apportionment and filing positions.  
- Assess eligibility for CAMT and prepare financial statement reconciliations.  
- Update payroll and remote work reporting systems to comply with new Form CT-20 requirements.  
- Maximize R&D credit claims, especially for activities in targeted zones.  
- Consider voluntary disclosure for prior periods where non-compliance risks exist.
