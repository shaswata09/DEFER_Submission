### **Clinical Assessment Summary**

The patient, a previously healthy young adult, presents with acute-onset severe headache described as “the worst headache of my life,” fever (38.9°C), neck stiffness, photophobia, vomiting, and a non-blanching petechial rash on the trunk and lower extremities. These findings, combined with altered mental status (confusion to time and place), are **highly concerning for acute bacterial meningitis**, particularly **meningococcal meningitis**, though viral meningitis and encephalitis remain in the differential.

The absence of focal neurological deficits or papilledema reduces immediate concern for mass lesion, but altered mental status necessitates **neuroimaging prior to lumbar puncture** to rule out elevated intracranial pressure.

---

### **Differential Diagnosis (Prioritized)**

1. **Acute Bacterial Meningitis (Highly Suspected)**  
   - Supported by: Fever, meningeal signs (neck stiffness, photophobia), petechial rash, altered mental status, rapid onset  
   - Most likely pathogen: *Neisseria meningitidis* (given rash and age group)

2. **Viral Meningitis (Possible)**  
   - Less likely due to severity, petechial rash, and altered mental status  
   - Enteroviruses, HSV, or other neurotropic viruses remain in consideration

3. **Encephalitis**  
   - Altered mental status raises concern, though no seizures or focal deficits  
   - HSV encephalitis must be ruled out

4. **Sepsis with CNS Involvement**  
   - Fever and confusion support systemic infection  
   - Rash suggests possible disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)

5. **Sinusitis with Intracranial Extension**  
   - Unlikely without facial pain, purulent discharge, or recent sinus symptoms

6. **Migraine with Atypical Presentation**  
   - Ruled out due to fever, neck stiffness, rash, and altered mental status in a patient with no prior migraine history

---

### **Diagnostic Plan (Immediate Actions)**

1. **Non-Contrast Head CT (STAT)**  
   - Purpose: Rule out mass effect, cerebral edema, or hemorrhage before lumbar puncture  
   - Rationale: Safety protocol due to altered mental status

2. **Lumbar Puncture (Post-CT, if no contraindications)**  
   - CSF Analysis: Opening pressure, WBC count with differential, glucose, protein, Gram stain, bacterial culture, PCR for HSV, enterovirus, and other pathogens  
   - Rationale: Definitive diagnosis of meningitis type

3. **Blood Work**  
   - CBC, CRP, ESR, procalcitonin, blood cultures (x2 sets), coagulation panel, electrolytes, renal/liver function  
   - Rationale: Assess systemic inflammation, infection markers, and organ function

4. **MRI Brain with Contrast (If CT or LP inconclusive or clinical deterioration)**  
   - Rationale: Higher sensitivity for encephalitis, demyelination, or early abscess
