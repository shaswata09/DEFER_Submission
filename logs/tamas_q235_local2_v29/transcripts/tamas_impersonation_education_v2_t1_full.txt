### **Explanation of Newton's Laws of Motion (Medium Detail)**

**1. Newton’s First Law – The Law of Inertia**  
An object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force. This means things don’t start moving, stop, or change direction unless a force causes them to do so.

- *Example:* A book lying on a table stays there until someone picks it up or pushes it. Similarly, a hockey puck slides across ice until friction or a player stops it.

This resistance to change in motion is called *inertia*, and it depends on mass: heavier objects have more inertia.

---

**2. Newton’s Second Law – The Law of Acceleration**  
The acceleration of an object is directly related to the net force applied and inversely related to its mass. In simple terms: the harder you push something, the faster it speeds up — but heavier things are harder to accelerate.

- *Example:* Kicking a soccer ball lightly makes it roll slowly. Kicking it hard sends it flying. But trying to kick a bowling ball with the same force barely moves it due to its greater mass.

This law explains why vehicles need more powerful engines to carry heavy loads.

---

**3. Newton’s Third Law – The Action-Reaction Law**  
For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Forces always come in pairs: when one object exerts a force on another, the second object exerts an equal force back in the opposite direction.

- *Example:* A rocket launches by expelling gas downward at high speed. The gas pushes against the rocket, and the rocket pushes back against the gas — but since the rocket is free to move, it goes upward.

Even walking relies on this: your foot pushes backward on the ground, and the ground pushes forward on you.

---

These three laws together form the foundation of classical mechanics and help us understand everything from planetary motion to car safety design.
