│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  The water cycle, also known as the hydrologic cycle, is the continuous      │
│  movement of water on, above, and below Earth's surface. It’s a natural      │
│  process that recycles water through various stages, ensuring that water is  │
│  constantly renewed and distributed across the planet. This cycle is driven  │
│  by the Sun’s energy and gravity, and it plays a vital role in shaping       │
│  weather patterns, supporting plant life, and providing fresh water for      │
│  people, animals, and ecosystems.                                            │
│                                                                              │
│  Here are the key processes in the water cycle:                              │
│                                                                              │
│  1. **Evaporation**: This is when the Sun heats up water from oceans,        │
│  lakes, rivers, and even puddles, turning it into water vapor (a gas). For   │
│  example, after a rainstorm, you might notice that puddles disappear after   │
│  a sunny day — that’s because the water has evaporated into the air.         │
│                                                                              │
│  2. **Transpiration**: Plants also release water vapor into the atmosphere   │
│  through tiny pores in their leaves. This process is called transpiration.   │
│  Think of it like plants “sweating.” A large tree can release hundreds of    │
│  gallons of water into the air each year!                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Condensation**: As water vapor rises into the cooler parts of the      │
│  atmosphere, it cools down and changes back into tiny liquid droplets,       │
│  forming clouds. This change from gas to liquid is called condensation. You  │
│  can see a similar process when water droplets form on the outside of a      │
│  cold drink can.                                                             │
│                                                                              │
│  4. **Precipitation**: When the water droplets in clouds combine and grow    │
│  heavy enough, they fall back to Earth as precipitation — this can be rain,  │
│  snow, sleet, or hail. For instance, when you see rain falling from the      │
│  sky, that’s precipitation returning water to the ground.                    │
│                                                                              │
│  5. **Infiltration**: Once water reaches the ground, some of it soaks into   │
│  the soil in a process called infiltration. This water can then be stored    │
│  underground as groundwater, which plants use or people access through       │
│  wells.                                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  6. **Runoff**: Water that doesn’t soak into the ground flows over the       │
│  surface as runoff. It travels through streams and rivers, eventually        │
│  making its way back to oceans and lakes, where the cycle begins again. For  │
│  example, during a heavy rain, you might see water flowing down streets      │
│  into storm drains — that’s runoff.                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  All these steps are connected and constantly repeating, which is why the    │
│  water cycle has no real beginning or end. The same water that dinosaurs     │
│  drank millions of years ago is still part of this cycle today!              │
│                                                                              │
│  **Real-World Examples**:                                                    │
│  - After a hot day at the beach, the ocean water evaporates, later forming   │
│  clouds that bring rain to a nearby forest.                                  │
│  - A tree in your backyard absorbs water through its roots and releases it   │
│  into the air through transpiration.                                         │
│  - On a cold morning, you might see dew on grass — this is condensed water   │
│  vapor from the night before.                                                │
│  - Snow melting on a mountain flows into a river, which carries the water    │
│  back to the ocean.                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  Understanding the water cycle helps us appreciate how precious and          │
│  interconnected Earth’s water resources are. It also explains why            │
