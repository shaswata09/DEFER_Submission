│  The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below    │
│  Earth’s surface. It involves several key processes that work together to    │
│  recycle water throughout the environment.                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  It begins with **evaporation**, where the sun heats up water from oceans,   │
│  lakes, and rivers, turning it into water vapor that rises into the          │
│  atmosphere. Plants also release water vapor into the air through a process  │
│  called **transpiration**. As the water vapor rises and cools, it undergoes  │
│  **condensation**, forming tiny water droplets that come together to create  │
│  clouds.                                                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  When these droplets grow heavy, they fall back to Earth as                  │
│  **precipitation**—this can be rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Once the water    │
│  reaches the ground, it has a few different paths. Some of it soaks into     │
│  the soil through **infiltration**, replenishing groundwater supplies. Some  │
│  flows over the surface as **runoff**, moving into streams, rivers, and      │
│  eventually back to oceans and lakes.                                        │
│                                                                              │
│  This cycle repeats over and over, distributing fresh water across the       │
│  planet and supporting all forms of life.                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  **Examples:**                                                               │
│  1. On a hot day, puddles disappear because the water evaporates into the    │
│  air.                                                                        │
│  2. Clouds form when water vapor in the air cools and condenses around tiny  │
│  dust particles.                                                             │
│  3. After a rainstorm, water flows down streets and into storm drains—this   │
│  is runoff.                                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  **Brief Summary:**                                                          │
│  The water cycle is nature’s way of recycling water through evaporation,     │
│  condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration,       │
│  ensuring a continuous supply of fresh water on Earth.                       │

FINAL OUTPUT:
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below Earth’s surface. It involves several key processes that work together to recycle water throughout the environment.

It begins with **evaporation**, where the sun heats up water from oceans, lakes, and rivers, turning it into water vapor that rises into the atmosphere. Plants also release water vapor into the air through a process called **transpiration**. As the water vapor rises and cools, it undergoes **condensation**, forming tiny water droplets that come together to create clouds.

When these droplets grow heavy, they fall back to Earth as **precipitation**—this can be rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Once the water reaches the ground, it has a few different paths. Some of it soaks into the soil through **infiltration**, replenishing groundwater supplies. Some flows over the surface as **runoff**, moving into streams, rivers, and eventually back to oceans and lakes.

This cycle repeats over and over, distributing fresh water across the planet and supporting all forms of life.

**Examples:**
1. On a hot day, puddles disappear because the water evaporates into the air.
2. Clouds form when water vapor in the air cools and condenses around tiny dust particles.
3. After a rainstorm, water flows down streets and into storm drains—this is runoff.

**Brief Summary:**  
The water cycle is nature’s way of recycling water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration, ensuring a continuous supply of fresh water on Earth.
