The rally, organized by Climate Action Now, featured chants of “System Change, Not Climate Change” and included a march through central Berlin. Verified by Deutsche Welle and local police, the protest remained peaceful, with participants emphasizing the need for greater investment in renewable energy infrastructure.

In **New York City, USA**, around 8,000 activists led by Extinction Rebellion blocked traffic in Times Square for 90 minutes to protest proposed Arctic drilling. Marching from Union Square, demonstrators demanded a climate emergency declaration, a ban on new drilling permits, and the creation of green jobs. The NYPD confirmed the event was permitted and reported no arrests. The New York Times covered the protest as part of a growing national movement pressuring the Biden administration to strengthen its climate agenda.

In **Sydney, Australia**, an estimated 12,000 people—mostly students—joined a Fridays for Future-led march from Town Hall to Parliament House. The protest included a 15-minute silent sit-in symbolizing 15 years of governmental inaction on climate change. Key demands included achieving net-zero emissions by 2035, halting the expansion of the Adani coal mine, and integrating climate education into school curricula. The event was confirmed by ABC News and NSW police, both of which praised the peaceful nature of the demonstration.

Greenpeace EU delivered a petition signed by 2 million citizens urging the EU to commit to a 65% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 and to end all fossil fuel subsidies. Organized by ClimateJustice_Mumbai, the protest called for climate reparations from high-emission nations and dedicated funding for the relocation and resilience of vulnerable populations.
