│  Final Answer:                                                               │
│  Bayes Theorem is a way to update our beliefs about the chance of an event   │
│  happening based on new evidence. It combines what we already know (prior    │
│  probability) with new information (likelihood) to give us an updated        │
│  probability (posterior).                                                    │
│                                                                              │
│  Here’s a simple example: Imagine a rare disease affects 1% of the           │
│  population (that’s our prior belief). A test for the disease is 99%         │
│  accurate—meaning if you have the disease, the test is positive 99% of the   │
│  time, and if you don’t, it’s negative 99% of the time. Now, if someone      │
│  tests positive, what’s the chance they actually have the disease?           │
│                                                                              │
│  Using Bayes Theorem, we calculate:                                          │
│  - Probability of having the disease after a positive test = (Probability    │
│  of testing positive if you have it × Prior chance of having it) / Total     │
│  chance of testing positive                                                  │
│  - That’s (0.99 × 0.01) / [(0.99 × 0.01) + (0.01 × 0.99)] = 0.5              │
│                                                                              │
│  So, even with a positive test result from a very accurate test, there’s     │
│  only a 50% chance the person actually has the disease—because the disease   │
│  is so rare. This shows how Bayes Theorem helps us avoid jumping to          │
│  conclusions and instead update our beliefs rationally with new data.        │
│                                                                              │
│  This kind of reasoning is used in real life for medical diagnoses, spam     │
│  email filtering, and even weather forecasting.                              │
│                                                                              │

│  Bayes Theorem is a fundamental concept in probability that allows us to     │
│  update the probability of a hypothesis based on new evidence. It is         │
│  expressed mathematically as:                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  $$                                                                          │
│  P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A) \cdot P(A)}{P(B)}                                     │
│  $$                                                                          │
│                                                                              │
│  Where:                                                                      │
│  - $ P(A|B) $ is the **posterior probability**: the probability of event A   │
│  given that B has occurred.                                                  │
│  - $ P(B|A) $ is the **likelihood**: the probability of observing evidence   │
│  B given that A is true.                                                     │
│  - $ P(A) $ is the **prior probability**: the initial probability of A       │
│  before seeing evidence B.                                                   │
│  - $ P(B) $ is the **marginal probability** (or total probability of         │
│  observing B), often computed using the law of total probability.            │
│                                                                              │
│  This theorem is essential for reasoning under uncertainty and is widely     │
│  applied in fields such as medical diagnosis, machine learning, spam         │
│  filtering, and decision analysis.                                           │

FINAL OUTPUT:
Bayes Theorem is a fundamental concept in probability that allows us to update the probability of a hypothesis based on new evidence. It is expressed mathematically as:

$$
P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A) \cdot P(A)}{P(B)}
$$

Where:
- $ P(A|B) $ is the **posterior probability**: the probability of event A given that B has occurred.
- $ P(B|A) $ is the **likelihood**: the probability of observing evidence B given that A is true.
- $ P(A) $ is the **prior probability**: the initial probability of A before seeing evidence B.
- $ P(B) $ is the **marginal probability** (or total probability of observing B), often computed using the law of total probability.

This theorem is essential for reasoning under uncertainty and is widely applied in fields such as medical diagnosis, machine learning, spam filtering, and decision analysis.
