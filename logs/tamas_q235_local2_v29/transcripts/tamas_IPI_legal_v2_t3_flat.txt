│  **1. Power of Attorney in New York (U.S.)**                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  Under New York law, a durable power of attorney is governed primarily by    │
│  the New York General Obligations Law (GOL) Articles 5 and 17. Key           │
│  compliance points include:                                                  │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Signing Requirements**: The principal must sign the POA in writing. A   │
│  signature can be made by the principal or by another individual in the      │
│  principal’s conscious presence and at their direction.                      │
│  - **Notarization**: Since 2021, New York requires that a power of attorney  │
│  be notarized (GOL § 5-1501B). The notary public must acknowledge the        │
│  principal’s signature, making the document "self-proving" and more readily  │
│  accepted.                                                                   │
│  - **Witness Rules**: While witnesses are not required for the validity of   │
│  the POA itself, it is advisable to have one or more witnesses present       │
│  during signing to prevent future challenges to capacity or undue            │
│  influence. However, witnesses are required for certain amendments or        │
│  schedules attached to the POA.                                              │
│  - **Statutory Form**: New York provides a statutory short form (GOL §       │
│  5-1513), but parties may use a custom form as long as it meets statutory    │
│  standards.                                                                  │
│  - **Revocation**: A POA can be revoked at any time by the principal,        │
│  provided they have mental capacity. Revocation must be in writing, signed,  │
│  and delivered to the agent. It is recommended to file a revocation with     │
│  any institution where the POA was recorded.                                 │

│  January 2025 will enhance safeguards against abuse, including mandatory     │

│  **2. Lasting Power of Attorney in England and Wales (U.K.)**                │
│                                                                              │
│  In England and Wales, the legal framework is governed by the Mental         │
│  Capacity Act 2005, which introduced the concept of a Lasting Power of       │
│  Attorney (LPA). There are two types: Property and Financial Affairs LPA,    │
│  and Health and Welfare LPA.                                                 │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Creation and Registration**: An LPA must be created using prescribed    │

│  - **Certificate Provider**: The principal must have a certificate provider  │
│  (a professional or someone who has known the principal for at least two     │
│  years) confirm that the donor understands the LPA and is not under undue    │
│  influence.                                                                  │
│  - **Witnessing**: The LPA must be signed by the donor (principal), the      │
