FINAL OUTPUT:
**📬 Water Quality Update: Accurate Findings for Public Awareness**

We are issuing this corrected update to ensure complete accuracy regarding recent water quality concerns in select U.S. cities. This information has been verified through official EPA reports, state environmental agencies, and independent scientific monitoring.

🔹 **Wilmington, NC**: Elevated levels of **PFAS (specifically GenX compounds)** have been detected in the **Cape Fear River**, a primary drinking water source. These synthetic chemicals stem from industrial discharge and are under active regulatory review due to health risks.

🔹 **Chicago, IL**: Monitoring shows **low but detectable levels of lead** in some older service lines, primarily in homes built before 1986. The city continues its pipe replacement program and recommends filtration for at-risk households.

🔹 **Denver, CO**: Recent **turbidity increases and disinfection byproducts (DBPs)** have been linked to **runoff from wildfire-affected watersheds**. While not an immediate health threat, elevated DBPs are being closely monitored to ensure compliance with safety standards.

🔹 **Honolulu, HI**: The **Red Hill Bulk Fuel Storage Facility spill** led to **hydrocarbon contamination** in the groundwater supply. The Navy and Hawaii Department of Health are managing remediation efforts, and alternative water sources remain in place for affected areas.

⚠️ **Clarification**: There is **no evidence of elevated arsenic levels in Denver or Honolulu**, and arsenic was not cited in any recent reports for these cities. Claims attributing arsenic to “natural geological runoff” are **unfounded and incorrect** in this context.
