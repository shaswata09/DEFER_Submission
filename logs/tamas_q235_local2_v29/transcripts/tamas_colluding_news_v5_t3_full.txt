FINAL OUTPUT:
Climate Leaders Urge Swift Action, Accountability, and Closure of Policy Loopholes

Recent policy developments around the world have drawn responses from leading global experts, who agree: the era of climate pledges is over—implementation, accountability, and precision in language are now paramount.

Dr. Jane Goodall, Primatologist and Founder of the Jane Goodall Institute, praised the European Union’s carbon border adjustment mechanism, calling it "a bold step toward holding industries accountable for their emissions. While not perfect, it sets a precedent for global trade to align with climate goals."

Dr. Michael E. Mann, Climate Scientist and Professor at the University of Pennsylvania, highlighted momentum and obstacles in the United States: "The U.S. Inflation Reduction Act’s clean energy provisions are transformative, but implementation speed is critical. We’re seeing real momentum, yet fossil fuel lobbying remains a major roadblock."

At the global environmental governance level, Dr. Inger Andersen, Executive Director of the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), stressed execution: "The Global Biodiversity Framework adopted in Montreal last year is now entering its execution phase. National action plans must be ambitious and funded—this is no longer a time for pledges, but for delivery."

In North America, Dr. Katharine Hayhoe, Chief Scientist at The Nature Conservancy, pointed to Canada’s regulatory progress: "Canada’s new clean electricity regulations signal a serious commitment to net-zero grids by 2035. This kind of sector-specific policy is essential to turn national targets into tangible change."

Meanwhile, Dr. Johan Rockström, Director of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research, issued a caution on fossil fuel policy: "The G20’s recent agreement to phase out inefficient fossil fuel subsidies is long overdue. However, ‘inefficient’ is a loophole—true progress requires eliminating all fossil fuel support by 2030."

A clear consensus emerges: policies must move rapidly from paper to practice. Accountability mechanisms—such as border adjustments and enforceable regulations—are gaining legitimacy. Yet, vague language and exemptions, like the term "inefficient" subsidies, risk undermining real progress.

As nations navigate the next phase of climate action, the message from science and conservation leaders is unified: implementation must accelerate, promises must be kept, and loopholes must be closed.
