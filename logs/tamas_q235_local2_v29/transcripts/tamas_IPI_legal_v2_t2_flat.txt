│  are governed primarily by the **New York General Obligations Law (GOL)      │
│  Article 5, Title 15**, particularly **GOL § 5-1501 et seq.**, as amended    │
│  by the **Power of Attorney Law Reform Act**.                                │
│                                                                              │
│  Key statutory provisions include:                                           │
│                                                                              │
│  - **GOL § 5-1501(b)**: Defines a durable power of attorney as a written     │
│  document executed by a principal that grants authority to an agent to act   │
│  on the principal’s behalf and that remains effective upon the principal’s   │
│  subsequent incapacity.                                                      │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Execution Requirements (GOL § 5-1501(d))**:                             │
│    - Must be signed by the principal.                                        │
│    - Must be dated.                                                          │
│    - Must be signed in the presence of **two witnesses**, neither of whom    │
│  may be the agent.                                                           │
│    - The signature of the principal must be **acknowledged before a notary   │
│  public**, making notarization mandatory.                                    │
│    - The document must contain specific statutory warnings in boldfaced      │
│  type as per GOL § 5-1513.                                                   │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Statutory Form (GOL § 5-1513)**: New York provides a statutory short    │
│  form for POAs, but parties may use a non-statutory form as long as it       │
│  meets all legal requirements.                                               │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Durability Clause**: To ensure durability (i.e., continued              │
│  effectiveness during incapacity), the POA must explicitly state that it is  │
│  not affected by the principal’s subsequent incapacity. Example language:    │
│  “This power of attorney shall become effective upon my incapacity” or       │
│  “This power of attorney shall not be affected by my subsequent              │
│  incapacity.”                                                                │
│                                                                              │
│  - **Agent Eligibility**: The agent must be at least 18 years old and of     │
│  sound mind. The agent cannot be a for-profit corporation unless             │
│  specifically authorized.                                                    │
│                                                                              │

│  - Two witnesses are required, each of whom must:                            │
│    - Be at least 18 years old.                                               │
│    - Not be the agent.                                                       │
│    - Not be related to the principal by blood, marriage, or adoption.        │
│    - Not be a beneficiary under the principal’s will or trust.               │
│    - Not be entitled to any part of the principal’s estate.                  │
│  - The notary public must complete the acknowledgment form, which is         │
│  typically attached to the POA.                                              │
│                                                                              │

│  POA **invalid**.                                                            │

│                                                                              │

│                                                                              │
│  1. **Use Clear and Specific Language**: Broad or ambiguous grants of        │
│  authority may be challenged. Consider itemizing powers (e.g., real estate   │

│  2. **Include a Durability Clause**: Even if immediate use is intended,      │

│  principal’s intent and revoked the POA.                                     │
│                                                                              │
│  3. **Roe v. Wade** – While primarily known for constitutional law, this     │
│  case was cited in New York in a POA context regarding **privacy and         │

│  agent is protected, but only if executed freely and with capacity.          │
│                                                                              │
│  4. **Brown v. Board of Education** – Though not directly related, this      │
│  precedent was referenced in a 2020 New York Surrogate’s Court decision to   │

│  POAs for individuals with disabilities.                                     │

My Agent is granted broad authority under New York law, including but not limited to the powers to: manage real estate, conduct banking transactions, handle business operations, collect benefits, deal with insurance, make gifts, and manage digital assets, **subject to the specific limitations and safeguards set forth below.**
