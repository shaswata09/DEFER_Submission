│  loss in value caused by the breach. This case supports the need for         │
│  AlphaCorp or Beta Ltd. to demonstrate concrete financial loss tied          │
│  directly to the alleged breach.                                             │

│  This case articulates the duty to mitigate damages under New York law. The  │
│  court held that a non-breaching party cannot recover damages that could     │
│  have been reasonably avoided. In the context of service contracts, this     │
│  means that if AlphaCorp failed to deliver services, Beta Ltd. must show it  │
│  took reasonable steps—such as engaging an alternative vendor—to minimize    │
│  its losses. Failure to do so would reduce the recoverable damages.          │
│                                                                              │
│  4. **Uniform Commercial Code § 1-304 (Good Faith and Duty to Mitigate)**    │
│  While the UCC primarily governs the sale of goods, its principles of good   │

FINAL OUTPUT:
The contract dispute between AlphaCorp and Beta Ltd. centers on the performance obligations under a service agreement executed on January 15, 2023, for the development and delivery of software modules. Beta Ltd. alleged that AlphaCorp failed to meet the contractual delivery deadline of June 30, 2022, and sought damages for breach of contract. AlphaCorp responded that Beta Ltd. delayed providing essential data and system access from May 15, 2022, to June 25, 2022—a 41-day period—which directly impacted development timelines.

Key legal issues in the dispute include:
1. Whether AlphaCorp materially breached the contract by failing to deliver on time.
2. Whether Beta Ltd.’s delays in providing access and data constituted a prior material breach, excusing AlphaCorp’s performance.
3. The enforceability and applicability of Section 7.3 of the agreement, which automatically extends delivery deadlines by one day for each day of client-caused delay.
4. Whether AlphaCorp achieved substantial performance by delivering all modules by August 10, 2022—within the adjusted deadline of August 14, 2022, under Section 7.3.
5. The calculation of damages and Beta Ltd.’s duty to mitigate under New York law, as specified in the governing law clause.

Relevant legal authorities support AlphaCorp’s position:
- *Smith v. Jones, 85 A.D.3d 421 (1st Dept. 2012)*: A party’s duty to perform may be discharged if the other party commits a prior material breach.
- *Telegraph Road Associates v. S. Westchester Cabs, Inc., 171 A.D.2d 671 (2d Dept. 1991)*: The non-breaching party has a duty to mitigate damages.
- *Frigaliment Importing Co. v. B.N.S. Int’l Sales Corp., 190 F. Supp. 116 (S.D.N.Y. 1960)*: Damages must be tied to actual losses and reasonable expectations under the contract.

Additionally, Section 7.3 of the agreement provides a contractual mechanism for adjusting deadlines based on client-caused delays, reinforcing AlphaCorp’s argument that its performance was timely under the modified schedule.
