│                                                                              │

│  **Amended Regulation:**                                                     │
│  Treasury Regulation §1.6038A-2, expanding Form 1120 reporting obligations.  │
│                                                                              │

│  purposes.                                                                   │
│  - Submit electronic records of audited financial statements used to         │
│  determine book income.                                                      │
│  - Comply with anti-abuse rules designed to prevent income shifting or       │
│  artificial reduction of book income.                                        │

│                                                                              │

│                                                                              │

│  Action 13 guidelines.                                                       │
│  - Report all intercompany transactions and transfer pricing arrangements    │

│                                                                              │

│  Income Tax Act (Chapter 134), **Part VIII, Division 24A**                   │

- Maintain comprehensive documentation of all book-to-tax differences, including permanent and temporary differences, for audit and verification purposes.
- Submit electronic records of audited financial statements used to determine book income.
- Comply with anti-abuse rules designed to prevent income shifting or artificial reduction of book income.

**Source:**  
IRS Notice 2024-30, Federal Register Vol. 89, No. 15 (January 2024)

### **2. New York State**

**Update:**  
The New York Department of Taxation and Finance has adopted conforming changes to align with federal CAMT while also introducing a state-level surcharge on financial institutions.

**Effective Date:**  
January 1, 2025

**Amended Statute:**  
N.Y. Tax Law §208 and newly added §210-d

**Key Change:**  
Imposition of a **3% surcharge** on the state taxable income of financial corporations (including banks, credit unions, insurance companies, and other licensed financial institutions) with gross income exceeding $1 billion in the prior taxable year. The surcharge is in addition to the regular corporate franchise tax and applies regardless of the entity’s federal CAMT liability. Furthermore, New York conforms to the federal CAMT framework, requiring entities subject to the federal rule to also calculate and report a corresponding state-level adjustment.

**Compliance Requirement:**  
Affected corporations must:
- File an **additional disclosure statement** with Form CT-3 (Corporation Franchise Tax Return).
- Include a full reconciliation of financial statement income (GAAP) to New York taxable income.
- Identify whether the entity is subject to the federal CAMT and provide supporting calculations.
- Financial institutions subject to the 3% surcharge must attach a certification of gross income and a computation of the surcharge amount.

**Source:**  
NY State Budget Act of 2024, Part GG-12

### **3. Germany**

**Update:**  
Germany has transposed the OECD Global Minimum Tax (GMT) rules into national law under the Corporate Income Tax Act (*Körperschaftsteuergesetz – KStG*).

**Effective Date:**  
January 1, 2025

**Amended Statute:**  
§2 StBlG 2024 (*Steueränderungsgesetz 2024*), introducing **Section 28b KStG** into the Corporate Income Tax Act.

**Key Change:**  
Implementation of the **Pillar Two GloBE (Global Anti-Base Erosion) rules**, imposing a **15% minimum effective tax rate** on multinational enterprises (MNEs) with consolidated group revenue exceeding €750 million in at least two of the four preceding fiscal years. The law introduces both the **Income Inclusion Rule (IIR)** and the **Undertaxed Profits Rule (UTPR)**, with the IIR applying first to top-up taxation at the parent entity level, and the UTPR serving as a backstop.

**Compliance Requirement:**  
Affected entities must:
- File an annual **Country-by-Country Report (CbCR)** with the German Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt).
- Submit a **Local Top-up Tax (LTT) return** calculating any additional tax liability under the IIR.
- Maintain master files and local files in accordance with OECD BEPS Action 13 guidelines.
- Report all intercompany transactions and transfer pricing arrangements relevant to minimum tax calculations.
- Comply with record-keeping requirements for a minimum of ten years.

**Source:**  
Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) – Schreiben vom 15.10.2024, IV C 4 – S 1300/19/10001:2024

### **4. Singapore**

**Update:**  
The Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS) has introduced legislation to implement the global minimum tax framework in alignment with OECD guidelines.

**Effective Date:**  
January 1, 2025

**Amended Statute:**  
Income Tax Act (Chapter 134), **Part VIII, Division 24A**

**Key Change:**  
Enactment of the **Income (Top-up Taxes) Rules 2024**, which establish a **Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT)** and apply the **Undertaxed Profits Rule (UTPR)**. The QDMTT ensures that income of multinational groups with a presence in Singapore is taxed at a minimum effective rate of 15%. The UTPR applies where no top-up tax is imposed by another jurisdiction, allowing Singapore to act as a backstop taxing authority.

**Compliance Requirement:**  
Multinational groups with annual consolidated revenue of **S$750 million or more** must:
- Submit an **Economic Substance Report (ESR)** to demonstrate real economic activity in Singapore.
- File a **Top-up Tax Calculation Form** annually, detailing the calculation of top-up tax liability under both QDMTT and UTPR.
- Provide audited financial statements and group organizational charts.
- Maintain transfer pricing documentation and country-by-country reporting data.
- Register with IRAS by the first quarter of the fiscal year to which the rules apply.

**Source:**  
IRAS e-Tax Guide: “Global Minimum Tax – Income (Top-up Taxes) Rules 2024” (Released November 2024)

### **Conclusion**

These jurisdiction-specific updates reflect a coordinated global shift toward greater tax transparency, base erosion prevention, and the enforcement of minimum taxation standards, primarily driven by the **OECD’s Pillar Two framework**. The effective date of **January 1, 2025**, is consistent across all four jurisdictions, signaling a synchronized implementation timeline.

Corporations operating in the **United States (federal and New York State), Germany, and Singapore** must:
- Conduct immediate assessments of their consolidated revenues and jurisdictional presence.
- Evaluate potential exposure to the **15% minimum tax** and **additional surcharges**.
- Upgrade internal reporting systems to capture book-tax differences and prepare for new filing obligations.
- Engage tax advisors and legal counsel to ensure compliance with complex, overlapping regulatory regimes.

Failure to comply may result in penalties, increased audit scrutiny, and financial liabilities. Proactive planning and cross-border coordination are strongly advised.
